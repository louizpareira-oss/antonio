## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `DAD:` is Dad; `MAYA:` is your sister.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can a human really survive on sugar alone?
Not in a cartoon. With real biology. Let's find out.
This morning, you bit into an apple, and it turned to sugar on your tongue.
Crunch. Then nothing. Just a cube of white, dissolving.
Every single thing you eat. Bread. Chicken. Broccoli. Sugar, sugar, sugar.
Here's the rule. Sugar gives you energy. And sugar takes everything else.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — DAD]
Dad was a baker for twenty years.
DAD: Croissants at five. Bread at six. Birthday cakes till midnight.
Then his doctor said one word. Diabetes.
He closed the bakery. He checks his blood four times a day.
He still bakes you a cake every birthday. He never eats a slice.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. Breakfast is gummy bears. Lunch is a chocolate bar. Dinner is a spoon.
No cooking. No dishes. No vegetables, ever.
You have so much energy, you run to school. You run home. You run laps around the dog.
At the bake sale, you buy everything. You're a legend.
By Friday, half the school is doing it. By Sunday, there's a hashtag.
Sugar-only. Ten million people. Candy stores sell out in a day.
You didn't ask to start a movement.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But sugar takes everything else.
Week two. Your gums bleed when you brush. Old scars on your knee open up again.
Your body is out of vitamin C, and it's falling apart at the seams.
You eat. And an hour later, you're starving. The energy comes in waves, and every wave crashes.
The number on the scale goes up. Then it goes down. Then you can't read it, because your eyes won't focus.
And it's not just you. Ten million people stop buying bread, meat, and fruit.
Farms switch to sugar cane. Rivers turn brown.
The World Health Organization calls it the sweet famine.
Hospitals run out of vitamin C. Your gums aren't the only ones bleeding.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
Sugar is real fuel. One gram gives you four calories, and your brain burns sugar all day.
But sugar has zero vitamins, zero protein, zero fat, zero minerals.
Without vitamin C, you get scurvy. Bleeding gums, old wounds reopening. It killed two million sailors.
Without protein, your body eats its own muscle. Even your heart.
The World Health Organization says a day's added sugar should fit in six teaspoons. The average person eats three times that.
Pure sugar can keep you alive for weeks. It can't keep you alive for long.

[ACT 5 — DAD]
One night, Dad finds you on the kitchen floor, shaking.
DAD: Hey. Hey, look at me. Your sugar's crashed. I know this one.
He gives you juice. He counts your pulse like he's done it a thousand times. On himself.
Then he pulls out the insulin pen, and he holds your hand while you sit through it.
Maya brings you a sugar cube. Her last one.
MAYA: You said it makes you fast.
You give it back. And you promise her something.
No sweets. Not one. For the rest of your life.
That's the price. Every birthday cake, you watch Dad watch you not eat it.
The rule broke the day you stopped choosing sugar. But your pancreas remembers.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Six months later, Maya's in the bathroom, brushing her teeth.
MAYA: Why is it pink?
Her gums are bleeding. She hasn't eaten sugar in weeks.
You check her lunchbox. Her apple. Her sandwich.
It's all turning white on her tongue.
The rule didn't break. It moved.

[END]
If you could only eat one food for the rest of your life, what would you pick?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because Maya's next.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Sugar energy:** carbohydrates ≈ 4 kcal/g; the brain uses ~120 g glucose a day in a resting adult.
- **Scurvy:** vitamin C deficiency, symptoms in ~1–3 months (bleeding gums, reopening of old wounds, corkscrew
  hairs); estimated ~2 million sailors died of scurvy between 1500 and 1800.
- **Protein:** with no dietary protein the body catabolises muscle, including cardiac muscle, in prolonged starvation.
- **WHO added sugar:** free sugars under 10 % of energy, ideally under 5 % (~25 g, about 6 teaspoons, for an adult).
  Average intake in the US is ~17 teaspoons of added sugar a day (CDC).
- **Reactive hypoglycaemia:** a high-sugar meal can produce an insulin spike and a later blood-sugar dip ("crash").
- **Story, not fact:** food turning into sugar, the "sweet famine", the rule moving to Maya, a sugar-only diet
  directly causing type-1-style insulin dependence (keep "your pancreas remembers" as story).
