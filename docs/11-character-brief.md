## Why this character is yours, not a copy

The pages we measured use a wolf in a suit, a pig, and a bulldog. Sleeve shares none of that:
different animal, different costume, different attitude (playful, not smug), and a built-in reason
to be explaining hidden mechanisms (he is a stage magician).

Kept from the technique (nobody owns these): a recurring cast, a strong close-up of the face for the
punchline, one consistent art style.

## The character in one paragraph

Sleeve is a masked raccoon stage magician. Grey fur, dark eye mask, ringed tail. A cheeky lopsided
grin and one eyebrow that goes up before every reveal. A small black top hat tilted to one side, a
deep plum cape lined with gold stars, a cream shirt, white gloves. He carries one playing card,
which he uses like a pointer. He never explains anything without first pretending it is magic.

**Signature moves (use in every video):**
1. **The eyebrow:** goes up right before the reveal.
2. **The card:** appears between two fingers when he says "here is the trick".
3. **The hat portal:** the camera drops into his top hat to start the story, and rises back out of it at the end.
4. **The cape swirl:** the cape sweeps across the screen as the sign-off.

**Palette (fixed):** plum `#5B2A6E` cape, gold `#D9A520` stars and card edge, warm cream `#F3E6CC`
shirt and background, deep teal `#1F5560` curtain and night, grey `#8A8F98` fur, near-black `#2A2530`
mask. Gold is also the selective-colour accent in every story shot.

## Voice and behaviour

- He addresses the viewer directly and teases them first ("You said three ninety-nine. Of course
  you did.").
- Then he does the magic-trick move: shows the trick, then shows how it is done.
- Dry, quick, never cruel. The joke is always on the trick, not on the viewer.
- Never says "they are lying to you". He says "here is what they are counting on".
