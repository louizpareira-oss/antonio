## What the 1M video actually did right

Before changing anything, here is why the last one worked. Every one of these is kept.

| Move | Where it happened | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|
| **The prophecy** | "You probably will," in second 2 | You made a *prediction about the viewer* in the first two seconds. Not advice. A verdict. Nobody scrolls away from a verdict about themselves. |
| **The flattering premise** | "You constantly think about getting rich" | It tells the viewer their obsession is evidence, not a flaw. People share things that make them feel already-chosen. |
| **The refusal of magic** | "not because thinking about money makes it appear" | You pre-empted the cynic in the comments. That is why it didn't get ratioed as manifestation slop. |
| **Chained clauses** | The whole thing is roughly one sentence | No sentence ever fully closes, so there is never a clean place to scroll. This is the actual retention engine. |
| **The loop** | Last line returns to the first idea | The ending makes the opening mean something new, so the replay feels designed. |

**What it left on the table:** it is 100% comfort. There is no cost, no warning, no turn.
A script that only reassures gets watched; a script that reassures *and then names a pain
the viewer hasn't admitted* gets sent to someone. That is the upgrade below.

## The voiceover script

Record it straight through, calm, slightly faster than feels natural. Line breaks are
breaths, not full stops — never let your voice drop at the end of a line except the last two.

```
You think about money more than you'd admit to anyone.

You'll probably get it.

Not because wanting something hard enough bends reality,

but because a person who stays quietly obsessed with one question for years

ends up knowing things that can't be taught in a weekend.

You start noticing which people in a room are building something

and which ones are only describing it.

You learn the difference between a risk and a gamble,

and you stop reading every loss as proof you were wrong to try.

Nobody warns you about the hard part.

Understanding it doesn't make the waiting easier — it makes it worse,

because the clearer it gets, the more unbearable it is to watch yourself not doing it yet.

That discomfort isn't a sign you're failing.

It's the distance between who you are and who you've already become in your head,

and that distance is the only thing that has ever moved anyone anywhere.

So it keeps pulling, and you keep closing it,

a year at a time, badly, in private, with no one clapping.

Until one day the life you kept picturing stops sounding like a fantasy —

because you were never daydreaming.

You were rehearsing.
```

**Word count:** 199 · **Pace:** ~170 words per minute — the same brisk, unhurried delivery as
the last one. Do not slow down to "poetry voice." The calm speed is part of why it works.

---

## The four upgrades

### 1. The hook is a confession, not a statement
Old: *"You constantly think about getting rich."* — true, but neutral.
New: *"You think about money more than you'd admit to anyone."*
The words **admit** and **anyone** turn it into a secret the video already knows. The viewer's
first reaction is *how do you know that*, and that reaction is one second long, which is
exactly the length of the decision to keep watching.

### 2. There is now a turn at 31 seconds
*"Nobody warns you about the hard part."*
This is the single most important addition. Every retention graph in this niche dips between
25 and 40 seconds, because by then the viewer has got the idea. That line restarts the video.
It promises information they don't have yet, right at the moment they were going to leave.

### 3. It names the pain instead of only the reward
*"the more unbearable it is to watch yourself not doing it yet"* — this is the line people
screenshot, and the reason is that nobody says it out loud. The last video described a
pleasant process. This one describes the specific ache of being ambitious and stuck, then
reframes it as proof of progress rather than failure.

That sequence — **name the private pain → reframe it as evidence** — is what converts a
view into a share. People send it to say *this is what I couldn't explain*.

### 4. The ending is a two-word reversal
Old ending: *"...is simply the beginning of becoming the person who eventually does."*
It is correct, but it is long, and it restates what you already said.

New ending:
> *because you were never daydreaming.*
> *You were rehearsing.*

Three words and then two. It re-labels the viewer's entire inner life in one move, and it
sends you back to the first line to check whether it was true all along — which is a replay.
Short final lines outperform long ones every time, because they can be repeated from memory.

## Captions

2–4 words at a time, changing with the voice. Centred, safe zone: 12% clear at top, 20% at
bottom, 20% on the right. Break on sense, never mid-phrase:

```
You think about money / more than you'd admit / to anyone
You'll probably / get it.
Nobody warns you / about the hard part.
the more unbearable it is / to watch yourself / not doing it yet
you were never / daydreaming.
You were / rehearsing.
```

Give the last four cards extra time. Put **"not doing it yet"** and **"You were rehearsing"**
on screen a beat longer than the audio needs — those are the two frames people screenshot,
and a screenshot is a free repost.

## Alternative hooks (test these)

The first three seconds decide it. Keep the whole script, swap only the opening:

1. *"You think about money more than you'd admit to anyone. You'll probably get it."* (default)
2. *"You're not obsessed with money. You're obsessed with not finding out how far you could have gone."*
3. *"Everyone around you thinks this is a phase. It isn't, and you already know that."*
4. *"You'll probably get rich. Not for the reason you think."*

Hook 4 is the closest to what already worked at 1M — it leads with the verdict and holds back
the reason. Hook 3 is the highest-variance: it will either outperform everything or flop,
because it picks a fight with the viewer's family.

Post the default first. If it clears its first hour under expectation, repost with hook 4
three days later and change nothing else. Never change two variables at once.

## The engine, so you can write the next ten yourself

Every script in this lane is the same eight moves. Change only the subject.

1. **Name a private thought** the viewer has never said out loud. *(secs 0–4)*
2. **Deliver a verdict** on their future. Early, confident, unhedged. *(secs 4–6)*
3. **Refuse the magic explanation.** "Not because…" This buys you the sceptics. *(secs 6–10)*
4. **Give the real mechanism**, in plain cause and effect. *(secs 10–30)*
5. **Turn.** "Nobody warns you about the hard part." *(sec 31 — never later than 35)*
6. **Name the specific pain** they have not admitted to anyone. *(secs 33–45)*
7. **Reframe the pain as evidence** that it is working. *(secs 45–60)*
8. **Close the loop** in under six words, re-labelling the opening. *(last 3 secs)*

Next three premises that fit the engine without repeating this one:

- *"You keep starting over. Everyone thinks that's the problem."* → the mechanism is that
  restarting is how you shorten the loop, and the pain is that nobody can see the compounding.
- *"You're the most reliable person you know, and it's costing you."* → competence as a trap.
- *"You don't want to be rich. You want to stop calculating."* → the hard part is that the
  calculating does not stop at the number you picked.

Do not post two of these closer than four days apart. The engine is recognisable, and the
same audience that made one video work will feel the pattern if you stack them.
