# 1. Why the appeal failed

## The hard truth about the course

You paid $44 for a tutorial that teaches, step by step, the exact format TikTok just
told you it will not monetize. Making "similar content" from that course will get you
rejected again.

Here is TikTok's stated reason, word for word:

> Your account contains content that uses formats, methods or resources such as
> **split-screen videos, irrelevant reactions, slides format, random shots, Q&A format,
> screen recordings or black screens**. These do not produce high-quality work and your
> account is ineligible for the Creator Rewards Program.

Now the course steps, mapped against it:

| Course step | What it produces | Policy problem |
|---|---|---|
| Step 1 — "use TikTok and Instagram to find quotes that are currently popular" | Someone else's words, already circulating | No original creative input. This is the definition of repurposed content. |
| Step 2 — AI voiceover (ElevenLabs) | Synthetic narration of that borrowed text | Nothing of yours is in the video yet — not your voice, not your words. |
| Step 4 — "GIFs from Giphy, Tenor, eyecannndy, Pinterest… clips of cartoons and anime" | Third-party clips you did not create or license | This is **"random shots"** in the policy text. It is also copyrighted studio footage. |
| Step 5 — 4:3 aspect ratio | 768×576 canvas | See below. |
| Step 6 — export 4:3, drop into a 9:16 project, "place it slightly above center" | A small framed box with empty space above and below | This is **"black screens"** in the policy text. Roughly 40–45% of the screen is dead pixels. |
| Step 5 — VHS filter | Degraded, low-resolution look | Works against the "high-quality work" bar. A grain/VHS filter over a 576p GIF is not 1080p content. |

**Every single step of the tutorial lands on the rejection list.** That is not bad luck —
the course is a recipe for the exact thing TikTok has been demonetizing since 2024.

## The video you sent me

I checked the file: **768×576, 38 seconds.**

Two independent failures, before anyone even looks at the content:

- **768×576** is far below TikTok's quality bar. Creator Rewards wants full-screen 1080×1920.
  Yours fills a box in the middle of a 1080×1920 frame, and the box itself is 576p.
- **38 seconds.** The Creator Rewards Program only pays on videos **longer than 1 minute.**
  Even if your account were approved tomorrow, a 38-second video earns $0.

## The part nobody tells you

The person who sold you this course makes money by **selling the course** — Stan store,
digital product, Instagram DMs for support. They are not living off Creator Rewards from
this format, because this format does not get approved for Creator Rewards. That is worth
sitting with for a minute before you buy anything else like it.

The $44 is spent. What is still useful in it: the editing intuition — matching a visual to
the pause in a voiceover, pacing, caption timing. That skill transfers. The **sourcing**
(scraped GIFs) and the **framing** (4:3 in a black box) are what have to go.

## What "original" actually means to TikTok

You are not being accused of stealing. You are being told your videos contain no
material that originated with you. To pass, a video needs at least one of:

1. **Footage you shot** — phone camera, your hands, your city, your room, your face.
2. **Your voice** — actually recorded, not synthesized reading someone else's quote.
3. **Your words** — a script you wrote, not a quote lifted from a popular page.
4. **Visuals you generated or built yourself** — AI images/video you prompted and composed,
   original motion graphics, your own photography.

Best case: three or four of those in every video. Minimum to have a chance: two.
