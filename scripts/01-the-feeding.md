# Script 01 — "The Feeding"

**Target length:** 75 seconds (72.5s of voice + 2.5s silent hold)
**Format:** 1080 × 1920, full bleed, no bars
**Beats:** 22 · **Average shot:** 3.3s
**Register:** voiceover monologue with one spoken line. Character: human body, pigeon head.

---

## The idea underneath

> People are not cruel to you. They are **comfortable** — they met a version of you before
> you arrived, and they are relieved you showed up as it. The trap is not their version.
> The trap is that you will audition for it, alone, where nobody can see.

The controlling image is **feeding**: bread, crumbs, flocks, being fed versus buying your
own. Every abstract idea arrives wearing it.

**The return:** the park bench in beat 1 comes back as beat 22 — same frame, dawn instead
of afternoon, two people on it instead of one.

## MASTER CHARACTER

Paste verbatim into every prompt where he appears. Never paraphrase.

```
MASTER CHARACTER — MARCUS
A man in his late twenties with the head of a common city pigeon:
smooth grey feathers, an iridescent green-violet sheen across the
throat, a narrow coral-red ring around each dark orange eye, short
pale beak. Human body, average height, slightly narrow shoulders,
human hands. Wearing a good navy wool overcoat over a white shirt,
with an office lanyard at the chest.
Visual style: 90s cel-shaded anime, clean line art, hand-painted
watercolour backgrounds, muted nostalgic palette, subtle film grain.
Lighting: overcast grey daylight, sodium streetlight at night, or
cold blue dawn.
Environment: an unnamed grey city — a park bench, an open-plan
office, a corner shop.
Keep the head, eye-ring, throat sheen, proportions, overcoat and
lanyard consistent across every shot.
```

**Short form** (hands, objects, shots where he is not the subject):

```
the same man with a pigeon's head — coral-red eye-ring, navy overcoat,
lanyard — same design as previous shots
```

Anime, to match the account's established look (doc 02). A pigeon-headed man reads as
absurd in photoreal and as matter-of-fact in cel shading, which is the register this needs.

---

## A production decision, made early

**A beak cannot lip-sync.** Doc 06 asks whether the character looks like it is talking;
here the honest answer is that it never will. So:

- The script is **voiceover-led**. Marcus never speaks on camera.
- The only spoken line belongs to a **human** character, whose mouth animates normally.
- Marcus's half of that exchange is a **reaction shot** — beat 21 is his eye, not his beak.

This is the doc 06 fix applied at the writing stage instead of discovered in the edit.

---

## The voiceover script

Record straight through. Line breaks are pauses. Dry, tired, faintly amused — not sad.

```
The first one hit me in the chest.

I was twenty-six. I was wearing my good coat.

Nobody throws bread at a man in a good coat.

My name is Marcus. I do accounts payable.

I have a flat, a season ticket, and a mother who calls on Sundays.

What I wanted was one conversation that didn't end in crumbs.

At work, someone left a slice of white bread on my keyboard.

I laughed. That was the mistake.

Because once you laugh, it's a bit. And a bit has to keep going.

A date put a basket of breadsticks in front of me and waited.

A woman pulled her son back by the hood of his coat.

Not one of them was cruel. That's the part I can't explain.

They were comfortable. They'd met me already, before I got there.

In November I bought a bag of bread. For myself.

I ate it on the bench in the dark, where nobody could see.

That's how it works. You don't get replaced. You audition.

In the morning the real ones came down for the crumbs.

They didn't know me either.

So I sat there. Not one thing, and not the other.
```

**Beat 20 — spoken, on camera, by the old woman:**

```
OLD WOMAN: Morning, Marcus.
    (action: sits down on the far end of the bench, does not look at him,
     sets her own bread bag between them, unopened)
```

**Beats 21–22 — voiceover:**

```
Nobody had used it in a year.

(silence — hold)
```
