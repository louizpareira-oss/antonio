# If Your Robot Vacuum Ever Starts Cleaning At 3 AM... DO NOT GET OUT OF BED

Original script, NIGHT SUPPORT call 001. 16:9 YouTube, ~4:30. Written after studying six viral
analog-horror rule videos (`docs/10-horror-reference-analysis.md`); premise, rules, twist, narrator
and wording are our own.

## Narration

If your robot vacuum ever starts cleaning at three in the morning, do not get out of bed. Do not open the app. And do not say its name out loud. You named it. Everybody names it.

There are seven rules. Follow them in order. Rule seven is about the map.

You did not schedule this. You set it for ten a.m., when you are at work. It knows when you are at work. It knows a lot. Over the years, it has drawn a map of your home. Every room. Every doorway. Where the couch is. Where the bed is. It drew it by itself, one bump at a time. Then it sent a copy somewhere. You never asked where.

Rule one. Do not get out of bed.
Your feet are the only part of you it has ever touched. It has bumped into them hundreds of times. It knows the difference between a chair leg and an ankle. It was programmed to go around ankles. Tonight, it is not following its programming. Stay under the blanket. The blanket is not part of the rule. It just helps.

Rule two. Do not open the app.
You will want to see where it is. The app will show you a little circle, moving from room to room. The moment you open it, the circle stops. Now it knows two things. You are awake. And you are holding a bright screen in the dark, right in front of your face. Put the phone face down. It already has enough pictures.

That is not a joke. In 2022, reporters found photos taken by test robot vacuums inside real homes. A kitchen. A hallway. A woman on the toilet. People who were paid to label the photos had shared them in online groups. The company said the testers had agreed to it. They had clicked yes. You also clicked yes. Your privacy is important to us. Please continue to hold.

Rule three. Listen to the hum.
A robot vacuum makes two sounds. A hum when it cleans. A beep when it is stuck. If the hum stops in the middle of a room, and there is no beep, it is not stuck. It is listening. Hold your breath if you have to. Do not move until the hum comes back.

Rule four. If it beeps twice, do not help it.
Two beeps means it is stuck. Listen for where. It will be under your bed. It has never fit under your bed. You have rescued it a hundred times. You reach under, you feel around, you pull it out. It knows exactly what your hand looks like from below. It is not stuck. It is waiting for a hand.

Rule five. If you hear it on the stairs, lift your feet.
Every robot vacuum has cliff sensors. Little infrared eyes underneath, so it never falls down the stairs. It cannot climb them either. Tonight, you will hear it on the stairs anyway. Going up. One step. A pause. The next step. Pull your feet onto the bed and keep them there. Something is giving it a lift.

Rule six. Check the dock.
From your bed, you can probably see the little light on the charging dock. Green means it is home. If the light is green, and you can still hear something cleaning... that was never your vacuum. Something learned the sound. It was not hard. It is the same hum every day, and you stopped hearing it years ago. One filmmaker set two robot vacuums to clean at three a.m. and filmed them. By morning, one was riding on top of the other. He called it a glitch. Nobody checked what was underneath.

Rule seven. In the morning, open the map.
When the sun is up, it will be back on its dock. Charging. Quiet. A little warm. Now you may open the app. Look at the map it made last night. Count the rooms. Then count the rooms in your home. If they match, empty the bin and go on with your day. Do not look too closely at what is in the bin. If the map has one extra room, look at where it is. It will be directly under your bedroom. You do not have a basement.

It was not cleaning last night. It was clearing a path. From that room, to your bed. The map saves automatically. So does the path.

So if your robot vacuum ever starts cleaning at three in the morning, stay in bed. Keep your feet up. And let it finish.

Thank you for calling Night Support. Your home is important to us. This call may be recorded for quality and training purposes. It has been.

## Real facts used (for the description)

- Robot vacuums build floor-plan maps of homes as they clean, and the maps are stored in the maker's cloud.
- Cliff sensors: downward infrared sensors stop robot vacuums from falling down stairs.
- In 2022 MIT Technology Review reported that photos taken inside homes by development Roomba J7 units,
  including one of a woman on the toilet, were posted to private online groups by contract data labelers;
  iRobot said the testers had signed consent agreements.
  https://www.technologyreview.com/2022/12/19/1065306/roomba-irobot-robot-vacuums-artificial-intelligence-training-data-privacy/
- Filmmaker Philip Bloom scheduled two robot vacuums for 3 AM; one ended up riding on top of the other all night.
  https://interestingengineering.com/video/this-is-what-robot-vacuums-do-when-you-sleep

This is a work of fiction. Your robot vacuum is probably just on the wrong schedule.
