## 1. The shared skeleton (all four videos)

Every video is the same four-part machine:

| Part | What happens | ORIG | V2 | V3 |

Part C is about 65–78 s of every video. That one part is what carries the "over 1 minute" length.

**V1 breaks the skeleton.** There is no black-and-white section. The whole video is staged on one
theatre stage: a bulldog boss rallies a crowd, the wolf takes the microphone, and the "evidence"
appears as pictures pinned on the wall behind him. It runs: boss rally (0–15 s), wolf takes over
(15–29 s), office-vs-farm comparison (30–57 s), boss interrupts (57–61 s), history of the work week
(61–95 s), "who is buying your hours" (95–111 s), sign-off with a fade to black (111–115.7 s).

## 3. What the copy does differently, ranked by how much it probably matters

1. **It uses the whole screen.** The original keeps its picture in a box with black above and
   below. The copy is full-bleed 9:16. That is the difference that decides Creator Rewards
   eligibility ("black screens" is in your rejection text), and the picture also looks about 2.5× bigger on a phone.
2. **It talks slower and lets images land.** About 110 words per minute against 179, with real pauses.
   In V1 about 20% of the running time is pauses. Silence after a punchline plus a close-up of the
   wolf's face is the "acting" the original does not have.
3. **It holds the shot longer and animates inside it.** V2 and V3 average 3.4–4.6 s per shot, with camera moves
   inside the shot. The original cuts every 2.2 s between mostly still drawings.
4. **One art style start to finish.** The original mixes cartoon styles; the copy keeps one palette.
5. **Selective colour.** In V2 the orange juice stays orange while the rest of the archive world is grey, so
   the eye always goes to the subject. The original never does this.
6. **A visible portal.** Spiral (V2) and white flash (V3) tell the viewer "now the story starts". The
   original just cuts.
7. **One word per caption**, changing about every 0.5 s. It is more readable at speed than a full line of text.
8. **A close-up punchline.** V1 and V2 end on an extreme close-up of the wolf's face.

## 4. What the copy did NOT improve (your openings)

- **Text inside the AI pictures is garbled**: calendars, price tags, crate labels and candy boxes contain
  nonsense letters in V2 and V3. Generate those scenes without text and put real text on top.
- **Filler shots**: V3 repeats the same restaurant shot several times with tiny changes.
- **Sign-off** in all three is the same "like, subscribe, evolve quietly" line. It asks for a follow but gives no reason to follow.

## 5. Who is copying what

The frame story (a wolf and a pig at a table), the "who told you that?" opening, the "old belief was
invented by an ad man" story and the "you're free to think" ending come from the original. The
copy's contribution is packaging and execution: full-screen, slower, one style, acting.

The original and the copy both sell a book from the profile link (the original links an Amazon
book; the copy's grid shows its own book cover). In both cases the videos look like the funnel
for the book, not the income source. That is inference from the profiles, not something I can see in their earnings.

## 6. What to take, and what not to

Take (techniques, nobody owns these):
- the four-part skeleton (frame story → portal → archive story → return)
- full-bleed 9:16 from the first frame
- ~110 words per minute with deliberate pauses
- one art style, selective colour, close-up punchline, one-word captions
- a 65-second middle so the video clears the one-minute mark

Do not take:
- the wolf, the pig, the bulldog, the catchphrases or the "evolve quietly" sign-off
- their scripts or topics (the "apple a day" and "orange juice" stories are their content)
- their frames

Two cautions for your own version. First, I did not fact-check the history told in these videos;
it mixes documented events with spin, and a wrong claim in a video with your name on it is your
problem. Second, use TikTok's AI-generated-content label.
