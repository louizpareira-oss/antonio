# Script 01 — "Forgive the Past"

**Target length:** 70 seconds (67.5s of voice + 2s silent hold at the end)
**Format:** 1080 × 1920, full bleed, no bars
**Beats:** 20 · **Average shot:** 3.4s

---

## The idea underneath

Most "forgive the past" videos say *let go and be free*. That is a slogan, and slogans
scroll past. This one argues something specific:

> You are not still angry about what happened. You are angry that it did not happen
> differently — so you keep replaying it, hoping to edit it. Forgiveness is not absolution.
> It is putting down the case you have been building for a trial that will never happen.

The controlling image is a **courtroom**: you as prosecutor, collecting evidence for years,
against someone who moved on. The final turn reveals you were never the judge — you were
the defendant, serving the sentence.

---

## The voiceover script

Record this straight through. The line breaks are your pauses — breathe at each one.

```
You're not still angry about what happened.

You're angry that it didn't happen differently.

I was nineteen the last time I spoke to him.

I've had that conversation four hundred times since.

In the shower. On the bus. At three in the morning.

Every time, I win. Every time, I say the perfect thing.

And every time, I wake up, and it still went the way it went.

That's what people get wrong about forgiveness.

It isn't saying it was okay.

It isn't letting them back in.

It's putting down the case you've been building.

Because you've been a lawyer for years now —

collecting evidence for a trial that is never going to happen.

They moved on. They sleep fine.

You're the only one still sitting in that courtroom.

Forgiving the past is not a gift you give them.

It's you deciding you've served enough time —

for something you didn't even do.

You were never the judge.

You were the one in the cell.
```

**Word count:** 164 · **Read slowly.** Around 120 words per minute with real pauses. If your
recording comes in under 60 seconds you are rushing — slow down, do not add words.

---

## Make it yours (important)

Lines 3–7 are the personal section, and they are currently **my placeholder, not your life.**
Swap them for your own memory. That is the part TikTok's reviewers — and your audience —
respond to, and it is what makes the video original in the way that matters.

Rules for replacing them:

- **Name an age or a date.** "I was nineteen" beats "when I was younger."
- **Name one physical place.** A bus, a kitchen, a hospital corridor, a car parked outside.
- **Give a number.** "Four hundred times" beats "so many times."
- **Do not explain the feeling.** Say what happened. The feeling arrives on its own.

Template to fill in:

```
I was [AGE] the last time [SPECIFIC EVENT].
I've [REPEATED ACTION] [NUMBER] times since.
[PLACE]. [PLACE]. [TIME OF NIGHT].
Every time, [HOW IT GOES IN YOUR HEAD].
And every time, I wake up, and it still went the way it went.
```

If you genuinely do not want to be personal, change "I" to "you" throughout that section
and it still works — but it will be weaker, and everything in the niche is drifting toward
the personal version.

## Captions

2–4 words at a time, changing with the voice. Centred, in the safe zone
(12% clear at top, 20% at bottom, 20% on the right).

Break them like this — never mid-phrase:

```
You're not / still angry / about what happened
You're angry / that it didn't / happen differently
They moved on. / They sleep fine.
You were never / the judge.
You were / the one / in the cell.
```

Give the last three cards extra time on screen. That is the line people screenshot.

## Alternative hooks (test these)

The first 3 seconds decide whether the video lives. If this one underperforms, keep the
whole script and swap only the opening:

1. *"You're not still angry about what happened. You're angry that it didn't happen differently."* (default)
2. *"You've won that argument four hundred times. In your head. Alone."*
3. *"They're asleep right now. You're watching this."*
4. *"Forgiveness isn't for them. It's the end of a job you were never paid for."*

Hook 3 is the most aggressive and will probably test best — it puts the viewer in the
video's situation in the first second.
