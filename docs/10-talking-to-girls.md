# 10. Talking to a girl you like

"Rizz" is not a line you memorise. It is three things happening at once:

1. She can **hear** you and understand you (that is doc 11).
2. You are **calm** — you are not auditioning for her approval.
3. You are **actually curious** about her, and it shows.

Nothing below works without those three. A perfect sentence delivered by a nervous
man who is not listening still fails. A plain sentence delivered by a calm man who is
listening usually works.

There is no script that makes a stranger like you. What the good approach really does
is **remove the reasons to say no** — you are not loud, not creepy, not boring, and
you do not take her time hostage. That is most of it.

---

## The one rule that makes the rest work

> **She is allowed to not be interested, and that is fine.**

Everything charming comes out of that sentence. If you accept the "no" before you even
open your mouth, you stop performing, your voice drops, your body relaxes, and you
become someone worth talking to. If you cannot accept it, she will feel the pressure in
the first five seconds and the conversation is already over.

Practical version: **decide before you speak that you will walk away smiling if she is
not interested.** Then speak.

---

## Opening

Forget pickup lines. A pickup line announces "I am running a routine on you." Say
something **true about right now**.

The formula: **observation + light question**.

| Situation | What you say |
|---|---|
| Queue, café, shop | "This queue is a test of character. Have you tried anything here that's actually good?" |
| Gym | "Are you using this one? … Ok. How many sets you got left?" |
| Class / campus | "Did you understand anything he said in the last ten minutes? Because I didn't." |
| Party | "I don't know anybody here except the guy who invited me, and he's gone. I'm Antonio." |
| Bookshop, record shop | "Is that good? I'm looking for something and I have no idea what." |
| Bus stop, street (be careful) | "Sorry, quick one — I liked your jacket, where's it from?" |

Rules for the opener:
- **Say it at normal volume, facing her, then stop talking.** Let her answer.
- **Do not open with a compliment about her body.** Ever. Clothes, style, energy, or the
  thing she is doing — fine. Body — no.
- **One opener per person.** If it lands flat, you say "Alright, have a good one" and you
  leave. That exit is what makes the next attempt easy.

### Your name, early
Give your name in the first thirty seconds: "I'm Antonio, by the way." It turns a
stranger into a person. Almost nobody does it and it always works.

---

## The middle: how to not run out of things to say

Running out of words is not a vocabulary problem. It is a **listening** problem. You
were thinking about your next sentence instead of hearing her last one.

### The 2-step loop
Use this all night. It never runs dry.

1. **Take the last thing she said and go one level deeper.**
2. **Give something of your own on the same subject.**

> She: "I'm studying nursing."
> — Bad: "Oh cool. So, do you like music?" *(you changed the subject, she is now doing all the work)*
> — Good: "Nursing is hard. What made you pick it?" *(deeper)* … then: "I could never do the blood part. I nearly passed out getting a needle last year." *(yours)*

**Deeper** means: why, how did that start, what's the worst part, what would you do
instead. Never: what, where, how many. Facts kill conversations. Reasons and feelings
grow them.

### Give something back
If she answers three questions in a row and you have said nothing about yourself, you
are interviewing her, not talking to her. Alternate. Question, then something of yours.

### Statements beat questions
"You don't look like you're from here" gets more than "Where are you from?" A statement
invites her to correct you, agree, or tease you — that is a conversation. A question is
a form to fill.

### Silence is fine
Two seconds of quiet is not an emergency. If you rush to fill every gap you look
anxious. Let the pause sit. Often she fills it, and what she fills it with is the real
conversation starting.

---

## Teasing (the part people get wrong)

Light teasing works because it says "I am not afraid of you and I am not trying to buy
your approval." But there is a line.

**Yes:**
- "You're way too proud of that."
- "Ok, that's the worst opinion I've heard today. Say more."
- "You're trouble. I can tell already."

**No:**
- Anything about her body, her weight, her intelligence, her family, her accent.
- Anything said in front of her friends that could embarrass her.
- Anything you would not say with a smile on your face.

Rule of thumb: **tease the opinion, never the person.** And if her face changes even
slightly, drop it immediately and say "Sorry — that came out wrong."

## Reading the signal (so you are never "that guy")

You do not need to guess. Check these:

**Green — keep going**
- She asks *you* questions back.
- She turns her body toward you; her feet point at you.
- She gives long answers, adds details you did not ask for.
- She introduces you to her friend.
- She laughs and then keeps the subject going.

**Yellow — change something**
- Short answers, polite tone, eyes moving around the room.
- She has not asked you a single question in five minutes.
  → Try a new subject once, or say "Anyway, I'll let you get back to your friends" and
  see if she stops you. If she doesn't stop you, it was a no.

**Red — leave, warmly, now**
- Headphones back on, phone up, turning away, one-word answers, looking for her friends.
- "I have a boyfriend," "I'm busy," or any version of no.
  → "No worries at all — have a good night." Smile. Go. Do not negotiate, do not ask
  why, do not make a joke about it. **A clean exit is the most attractive thing in this
  whole document.**

Never touch a stranger beyond a handshake or a hand on the shoulder in greeting. Never
follow. Never block her path. Never keep going after a no. This is not just decency —
pressure is what makes a maybe turn into a no.

---

## Getting the number

Do it **before** the conversation dies, at the high point — usually 5–10 minutes in,
right after a good laugh.

Say: **"Listen, I have to go, but I like talking to you. Give me your number and let's
get a coffee this week."**

Why this works:
- *"I have to go"* — you are not stuck to her, you have a life.
- *"I like talking to you"* — honest, direct, no game.
- *"Let's get a coffee this week"* — a specific, small, low-pressure plan. Much easier
  to say yes to than an open-ended "we should hang out."

Hand her your unlocked phone with the keypad open. If she hesitates: "All good" —
smile, leave. Do not ask twice.

---

## Texting after

- **Text the same day or the next day.** Waiting three days is old nonsense that only
  makes her forget you.
- **First text: remind her who you are + one callback.** "It's Antonio — the guy with
  the terrible opinion about pineapple pizza. Still standing by it."
- **Do not have a long text relationship.** Text is for making a plan, not for
  entertaining her. After a few messages: "Free Thursday evening? There's a place near
  X I want to try."
- **One unanswered text, then stop.** If she doesn't reply, she doesn't reply. Sending a
  second "hello???" is the single fastest way to be blocked.
- No good morning paragraphs, no chains of messages, no "wyd" at 2am.

---

## The first date

- **Short and active beats long and formal.** Coffee, a walk, a market, a cheap drink.
  60–90 minutes. Leave while it is still good.
- **Pay for the first cheap thing without making it a moment.** If she insists on
  splitting, let her, immediately.
- **Phone in your pocket, face down, the whole time.**
- **Ask about things she cares about and remember the answers.** Bringing up something
  she mentioned last time is worth more than any line in this document.
- End it yourself: "This was good. Let's do it again." Then go.

---

## When it doesn't work

It will not work most of the time, and that is the normal, correct result — for
everyone, including the men you think are naturals. A no costs you nothing except a
feeling that passes in ten minutes.

What actually makes you better:
1. **Volume.** Talk to people with no goal — the cashier, the guy at the gym, the old
   lady with the dog. Charisma is a muscle and it has to be used daily.
2. **Reps of the exit.** Getting comfortable with walking away smiling is 80% of the
   skill.
3. **A life she can join.** Gym, work, a craft, friends, the account. Nobody is attracted
   to a man whose whole schedule is free.

---

## Never do these

- Never continue after "no," "I have a boyfriend," or a turned back.
- Never comment on her body.
- Never approach a girl who is working and cannot leave (waitress, cashier, shop) with
  anything more than one friendly exchange — she is trapped and she knows it.
- Never approach a woman alone at night on an empty street. She cannot know you are safe.
- Never lie about your job, your age, your money. It always comes out.
- Never insult her to make her "chase" — that is not confidence, it is cruelty, and it
  only works on people who are already unwell.
- Never send anything you would not want a screenshot of.

## Two-week practice plan

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | 3 small talks a day with anyone, no goal. Say your name to one stranger a day. |
| 4–6 | Same, plus hold eye contact until *they* look away first. |
| 7 | 1 real approach. Goal is **not** the number — the goal is to say the opener and leave calmly. |
| 8–10 | 2 approaches. Practise the 2-step loop for 5 minutes minimum. |
| 11–13 | Ask for a number once, at the high point, however it looks like it is going. |
| 14 | Review: which openers felt natural in your mouth? Keep those, delete the rest. |

Do this next to the 15-minute speaking drill in doc 11. The two feed each other — when
your mouth is sure of the words, your face and body calm down, and calm is the whole game.
