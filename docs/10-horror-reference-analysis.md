# 10. Viral analog-horror "rule videos": reference analysis

Notes from studying viral videos in the "If your [familiar thing] ever [does something wrong]... [WARNING]"
format. Use them for **format, pacing and look only**. Every script we make is written from scratch:
own premise, own rules, own twist, own wording (see `01-why-the-appeal-failed.md` on originality).

## Why Bradikinin gets 5–10× the views of Final Sight

Same format, same look, very different results. What Bradikinin does that Final Sight doesn't:

1. **Real, googleable facts reframed as horror.** Phantom vibration, ghost touch, the green camera dot,
   optimized charging, battery health; the 1967 CIA cat memo, cats knowing their names, the collar-camera
   "second family" study, the cry inside the purr. Viewers recognise their own life ("my cat does this"),
   which drives comments and shares. Sources are listed in the description with "This is a work of fiction."
2. **Search-driven topics and tags.** Titles and tags match what people actually type
   ("why does my cat watch me sleep", "phone battery 101%", "is my phone listening to me").
3. **Shorter, drier sentences and real jokes.** "You bought it a little raincoat." "It was not selling you
   anything. It was letting you know it heard." "Please disregard that." Humor makes the dread shareable.
4. **"You already did it" lines** that make the viewer feel caught ("You already follow six of them").
5. **A narrator with a running identity and a meta twist** (the agency is the threat; it cuts off mid-word;
   "We were asked to read it to you"), a one-word sign-off ("Stay." "Closer."), and rule 7 marked
   `[classified]` in the chapters for curiosity.

Final Sight leans on longer narrative, the same mimic-voice tropes every time ("you'll hear your own voice"),
and "I'm not talking about X. I'm talking about Y." No jokes, no real facts, fewer views.

**Do not copy** either channel's brand: no "Department of Domestic ___", no "trusted government agency",
no "Thank you for your cooperation", no "File 0XX". We build our own identity.

## Story formula both share

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:30).** Title line spoken word-for-word as the first sentence, plus 1–2 extra "do nots".
   Then "There are seven rules" and a hook about one of them.
2. **Why it's happening.** 2–4 sentences explaining the threat in plain, deadpan terms.
3. **Seven rules**, each 20–45 s: rule card → instruction → the reason → a creepy consequence.
4. **One "case file" / "report" story** around rule 3: a single person who broke the rule, 3–5 short sentences,
   ending on one chilling concrete detail.
5. **Escalation.** The threat learns: mimics voices, copies you, learns names.
6. **Rule 7 twist.** Reframes everything (Bradikinin: the narrator *is* the threat; Final Sight: check the paws).
7. **Sting / recap.** Repeat the title line, then one last line that lands on the viewer.

Voice: calm, deadpan, official ("We are a trusted government agency"). Short sentences. Dry humor lines
(Bradikinin: "You bought it a little raincoat") make it shareable. Description includes chapters per rule and a
"File 0XX" label.

## Visual style both share

- **Flat white pictograms on pure black**: airport-sign stick figures, white dog silhouette, no faces.
- Accents only: **red** (X marks, warnings, danger), **green** (check marks, safe), **yellow** (caution triangles).
- **HUD**: top-left notice label (`PET SAFETY NOTICE // RULE 3 OF 7`, `FILE 041`), top-right running timecode,
  bottom-centre caps subtitles.
- **Rule title cards**: `RULE` + big number, red divider, typewriter title, 1–7 progress tracker with current rule lit.
- Infographic language: dashed sightlines, bounding boxes, measurement rulers, waveform comparisons
  (`HIS FOOTSTEPS` vs `UPSTAIRS` → `MATCH`), progress bars (`FOCUS`, `YOUR ESCAPE`), counters, fake phone UI.
- Analog texture: scan lines, grain, vignette, RGB split and tracking glitches on tense beats and voice spikes.
- Mostly static 2D framing with hard cuts; pose-to-pose movement; 2–4 s per shot.

## Our own identity: NIGHT SUPPORT

- **Narrator:** a calm customer-support recording ("Thank you for calling Night Support"). Running gags:
  "Your privacy is important to us." "Please continue to hold." Sign-off: "This call may be recorded for
  quality and training purposes. It has been."
- **Look:** not white pictograms on black. Our frames are the **device's own map and app**: pale-cyan
  floor-plan line art on near-black (`#05070a`), the device's path drawn as a glowing trail, app-style
  status chips (battery %, CLEANING / STUCK / DOCKED), red for danger, green for safe.
- **Signature HUD:** a real night-time clock top-right that starts at **03:07 AM** and advances to sunrise
  as the rules progress (instead of a video timecode); `NIGHT SUPPORT // CALL 001` top-left.
- **Rule cards:** app-notification style ("Rule 3 of 7" as a push notification sliding in), not title slates.
