## Step 1 — Ten ideas

**1. YOU CAN'T SLIP WHAT YOU SEE** ★ *selected*
- **Hook:** "You can't slip a punch you see."
- **Angle:** Slipping isn't a speed race against the glove. It's reading what moves *before* the glove.
- **Turn:** Stop watching the glove → watch the shoulder, and drill calling the punch before slipping it.
- **Visual world:** (1) A midnight ring corner: one hanging lamp, taut ropes, a towel on the top rope, a glove frozen an inch from a head. (2) A dusty sepia beginners' gym: a cracked heavy bag on a chain, tall-window light, a row of trainees rocking onto their heels. (3) A navy rooftop at night: a weathervane that turns before the wind arrives, chimney smoke, a pennant that hasn't moved yet.
- **Fact to verify:** Skilled fighters anticipate punches from early body cues rather than reacting to the glove.
- **Emotion arc:** alarm (Noir) → frustration (Sepia) → curiosity (Navy) → focus (Navy) → confidence (Vibrant) → calm callback (Noir)

**2. SMALLER SLIPS WIN**
- **Hook:** "Your slip is too big to work."
- **Angle:** A big slip feels safe, but it's slower, it costs balance, and it moves you out of counter range. Inches beat feet.
- **Turn:** Size the slip: head just outside the glove's width, then back.
- **Visual world:** (1) A tailor's workshop: measuring tape, chalk, a mannequin ducking an absurdly wide swing of a yardstick. (2) A narrow alley where a bicycle squeezes past a wall with a hair to spare. (3) A navy blueprint ring with a glove-width cyan corridor drawn through it.
- **Fact to verify:** An effective slip moves the head only a few inches off the punch line.
- **Emotion arc:** overconfidence (Vibrant) → exposed (Noir) → precise (Navy) → efficient (Navy) → calm (Sepia)

**3. SLIP WITH YOUR LEGS**
- **Hook:** "Slipping isn't a neck movement at all."
- **Angle:** Neck-only and waist-bend slips are slow and fragile. The slip is built from the knees and torso rotation.
- **Turn:** Bend the knees and rotate the torso; the head rides along as a passenger.
- **Visual world:** (1) An elevator car dropping one floor while its passenger stays perfectly upright. (2) A lighthouse that bends at its base instead of tilting its lamp. (3) A sepia gym where a ghost overlay compares a neck-dodge with a whole-body slip.
- **Fact to verify:** Bending forward at the waist leaves you off-balance and in uppercut range.
- **Emotion arc:** doubt (Sepia) → discomfort (Noir) → insight (Navy) → strength (Vibrant)

**4. THERE'S A WRONG SIDE**
- **Hook:** "There's a wrong side to slip."
- **Angle:** Left and right aren't equal. The outside slip moves you away from the power hand; the inside slip moves you toward it.
- **Turn:** Against a same-stance jab, slip outside by default and go inside only when you mean to counter.
- **Visual world:** (1) A two-lane road where one lane veers toward an oncoming truck and the other toward an empty shoulder. (2) A top-down navy ring with a cyan safe zone and a red danger zone. (3) A revolving door where one side opens into a storm.
- **Fact to verify:** For two orthodox fighters, slipping to your right (outside the jab) moves you away from the opponent's rear hand.
- **Emotion arc:** confusion (Noir) → danger (Sepia) → clarity (Navy) → control (Vibrant)

**5. EVERY SLIP LOADS A COUNTER**
- **Hook:** "Slipping isn't defense. It's a wind-up."
- **Angle:** A slip that doesn't load a punch only invites the next punch.
- **Turn:** Slip outside → the rear hand loads; slip inside → the lead hook loads. Throw it.
- **Visual world:** (1) A slingshot drawn back as a pebble whizzes past it. (2) A wind-up spring toy on a navy workbench, key turning. (3) A noir ring where slip and counter are one continuous motion.
- **Fact to verify:** Slipping rotates the torso and pre-loads the hips for a counter.
- **Emotion arc:** passive (Sepia) → tension (Noir) → realization (Navy) → power (Vibrant)

**6. YOUR BLINK IS THE OPENING**
- **Hook:** "Your blink is the opening they need."
- **Angle:** The flinch-and-blink reflex blinds you at the exact moment you need to see, and it can be trained down.
- **Turn:** Light, slow touches with the eyes open; raise the speed only when the blink stops.
- **Visual world:** (1) A camera shutter that snaps shut at the wrong moment. (2) A lighthouse lamp that goes dark just as a ship passes. (3) A bright training room with soft foam balls tossed gently.
- **Fact to verify:** The flinch/blink response can be reduced with gradual exposure in training.
- **Emotion arc:** fear (Noir) → helplessness (Sepia) → understanding (Navy) → steadiness (Vibrant)

**7. SLIP LEVEL, NOT LOW**
- **Hook:** "Your slip is setting up their uppercut."
- **Angle:** Dipping the head down and forward puts it exactly where uppercuts travel.
- **Turn:** Stay level: knees bend, chin stays tucked, head stays over your base and not past your lead knee.
- **Visual world:** (1) A periscope that drops too low into the water. (2) A sepia bowling lane where one pin leans into the ball's path. (3) A navy ring with a cyan "safe box" drawn above the lead knee.
- **Fact to verify:** Leaning the head forward past the lead knee leaves you exposed to uppercuts.
- **Emotion arc:** surprise (Noir) → regret (Sepia) → clarity (Navy) → poise (Vibrant)

**8. THE WAY BACK GETS HIT**
- **Hook:** "The slip isn't over when it misses."
- **Angle:** Fighters get caught *returning* to center along the same path. The return is the second half of the slip.
- **Turn:** Come back behind a punch, or roll under to the other side instead of retracing.
- **Visual world:** (1) A pendulum swinging back into a waiting hand. (2) A sepia train returning on the same track into a closed signal. (3) A top-down navy ring showing a U-shaped return path.
- **Fact to verify:** Returning to the same position after a slip makes you predictable for follow-up punches.
- **Emotion arc:** relief (Vibrant) → shock (Noir) → insight (Navy) → control (Navy)

**9. ONE ROPE, NO PARTNER**
- **Hook:** "You don't need a partner to slip."
- **Angle:** The slip rope is an old-school solo drill that builds the movement before anyone throws at you.
- **Turn:** Rope at chin height; shadowbox down its length, slipping and rolling as you go.
- **Visual world:** (1) A sepia backyard clothesline with a figure weaving under it. (2) A garage with a rope between two hooks and a tennis ball on a string. (3) A navy night scene with one taut string glowing cyan.
- **Fact to verify:** The slip rope (slip line) is a traditional boxing drill.
- **Emotion arc:** doubt (Sepia) → curiosity (Navy) → momentum (Vibrant) → mastery (Noir)

**10. PREDICTABLE SLIPS GET TIMED**
- **Hook:** "Your best slip is getting you caught."
- **Angle:** Slip the same way every time and a smart opponent feints to it, then punches where you're going.
- **Turn:** Mix slips, blocks and steps; let the punch decide, not habit.
- **Visual world:** (1) A cuckoo bird popping out on schedule into a waiting cat's paw. (2) A sepia station platform where every passenger stands on the same worn spot. (3) A navy ring with footprint trails that repeat the same path.
- **Fact to verify:** Experienced opponents feint to draw a habitual defensive reaction, then target it.
- **Emotion arc:** pride (Vibrant) → trap (Noir) → understanding (Navy) → adaptability (Sepia)

### Why #1

- It has the strongest contradiction: everyone believes slipping is about seeing the punch.
- It gives one mechanism (read the shoulder) and one drill you can do tonight.
- It doesn't overlap the five topics already on @jabmaxapp's grid (guard, reach, tense/loose, the
  jab, ring cutting), so it isn't a remake of their videos.

## Step 2 — Script

### Voiceover

Blank line = 0.5s breath. The last blank line (before "Stop watching it.") = 1.0s.

```
[HOOK] You can't slip a punch you see.

[HOOK] By the time you react, the glove is already close. [FACT]

[PROBLEM] So you wait for it.
[PROBLEM] Then you flinch, a beat late.

[PROBLEM] Eyes shut. Chin up. Weight on your heels.
[PROBLEM] That's when the second punch finds you. [FACT]

[REFRAME] People who slip well aren't faster than you.
[REFRAME] They just move earlier.

[REFRAME] They read the punch as it starts. [FACT]
[REFRAME] Watch the shoulder. It often moves first. [FACT]
[REFRAME] That's your signal. Not the glove.

[TURN] Now the slip itself.
[TURN] Bend your knees, not your waist. [FACT]

[TURN] Turn your shoulders a few inches. [FACT]
[TURN] Just enough for the punch to miss.

[TURN] Eyes stay forward. Hands stay home.
[TURN] And your hips are already loaded to counter. [FACT]

[LANDING] Don't drill faster reactions.
[LANDING] Drill the read.

[LANDING] Have a partner throw slow jabs that stop short.
[LANDING] Don't slip yet. Just say "now" when the shoulder moves.

[LANDING] Next round, slip on your "now."

[LANDING] The glove is the last thing to warn you.

[LANDING] Stop watching it.
```
