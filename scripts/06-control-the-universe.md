## Our own stones (not Marvel's)
"Thanos", "Infinity Stones" and the gold glove are Marvel/Disney's: in a title or drawn on screen they invite copyright
claims and limited ads, and the channel already had a monetization rejection. So the stones are **ours**: six
hexagonal crystals that snap into a badge on the hoodie, named **TIME** (gold), **SPACE** (cyan), **ENERGY** (orange-red),
**MIND** (violet), **MATTER** (green), **LIFE** (pink).

## The rule of the story
- Six stones. Each one controls one thing: time, space, energy, mind, matter, life.
- **Every stone gives you something, and every stone takes something back.**

Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `GRANDMA:` is Grandma; `(thought)` lines are heard in the hero's head.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can one person really control the universe?
Not in a movie. With real physics. Let's find out.
This morning, at seven fifty-nine, your coffee stopped falling.
It's just hanging there. One inch from the floor.
And there's a gold stone in your hand, humming like a phone on silent.
Last night, six lights fell into Grandma's backyard.
Six stones. Time. Space. Energy. Mind. Matter. And life.
Here's the rule. Every stone gives you something. And every stone takes something back.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — GRANDMA]
Grandma Rosa was an astronomer for forty years.
GRANDMA: That one's Vega. That one's a satellite. That one's... a plane. Never mind.
Lately, she forgets things. Her glasses. They're on her head.
Sometimes, your name.
But she never forgets a star.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. You're late. You freeze time, walk past the frozen bus, and sit down early.
Math test. Wrong answer. You rewind ten seconds.
Blink. You're first in the lunch line.
Blink. Ramen in Tokyo. Blink. Back before the bell.
Every dead phone in class charges in your hand. Broccoli turns into fries.
And every stone you touch snaps onto your hoodie.
By Friday, you have gloves. Boots. And a cape.
You didn't ask for the cape.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But every stone takes something back.
When you freeze time, you keep living. By Sunday, you're nine days older than your birthday.
Every teleport leaves a hole in the air, and the air slams back in. Boom. The school windows crack.
The mind stone has no off switch.
At the football game, you hear forty thousand thoughts at once. One of them is your best friend's.
THEO (thought): I liked him better before.
And six stones together bend time around you. Just a few billionths of a second.
That's enough. Every GPS near you is wrong. Map apps send cars into the river.
NASA calls it the anomaly.
It's you.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
Time really does bend. Clocks on GPS satellites run thirty-eight millionths of a second faster every day.
Without a fix, your map would be ten kilometres off by tonight.
Teleporting people? No. But in 2017, scientists teleported the state of a particle of light to a satellite more than a thousand kilometres up.
Energy? Turn one paperclip completely into energy, and you'd beat the bomb that hit Hiroshima.
And there's a jellyfish that can turn itself back into a baby. Scientists call it the immortal jellyfish.
Every power is real. Just never all in one hand.

[ACT 5 — GRANDMA]
One night, Grandma doesn't know you. She sees the cape, and she steps back.
GRANDMA: Who are you? Where's my grandson?
The life stone fixes what's broken. And it takes something back. A memory, for a memory.
You press it into her hand. She blinks. She smiles.
GRANDMA: There you are. You got tall.
You get one perfect day at the old observatory.
And you realise you can't remember the first night she showed you the stars. That's what the stone took.
GRANDMA: You were six. You asked me if the stars could see us back. I said yes.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Then she goes quiet.
GRANDMA: I've seen these stones before. Nineteen sixty-nine. Six lights. Same backyard.
That night, NASA picks up six new lights. They're not falling. They're slowing down.
They're not coming back for the stones. They're coming back for whoever could hold them.
Your coffee stops falling.

[END]
If you could keep only one stone, which one would you keep?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because they're almost here.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **GPS time dilation:** satellite clocks gain about 38 µs a day relative to ground clocks (≈ +45 µs from weaker
  gravity, ≈ −7 µs from orbital speed); uncorrected, position errors grow by roughly 10 km a day.
- **Quantum teleportation:** 2017, the Micius satellite team (Chinese Academy of Sciences) teleported photon states from
  a ground station in Tibet to the satellite at up to ~1,400 km (*Nature*, 2017). Information, not matter.
- **E = mc²:** a paperclip is about 1 g; 1 g × c² ≈ 9 × 10¹³ J ≈ 21 kilotons of TNT; Hiroshima was about 15 kilotons.
- **Immortal jellyfish:** *Turritopsis dohrnii* can revert from the adult medusa to the polyp stage.
- **Story, not fact:** the stones, freezing time, GPS breaking around a person, the "anomaly", the 1969 lights.
