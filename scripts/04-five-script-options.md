(`docs/10-horror-reference-analysis.md`). Same winning format: title line spoken first, seven rules,
one real-world "report", real googleable facts turned creepy, dry jokes, a twist that lands on the viewer.
Own premise, rules, wording and narrator ("Night Support") in every one. Pick one and it gets voiced
and animated. The robot vacuum script (`03-robot-vacuum-3am.md`) is still available as a sixth option.

## 1. If Your Dog Ever Barks In Its Sleep... DO NOT WAKE IT UP

**Hook:** every dog owner has filmed this. **Twist:** you never get caught in your chase dreams, because your dog shows up.

If your dog ever barks in its sleep, do not wake it up. Do not say its name. And whatever you do, do not hold its paws while they are running.

You have seen this. The little muffled woofs. The twitching legs. The eyes moving under the lids. You laughed. You filmed it. You said, "He's chasing rabbits." You posted it. It did very well.

There are seven rules. Follow them every night. Your dog already does.

Dogs dream. That part is science. Small dogs dream about every ten minutes. Big dogs dream less often, but their dreams last longer. Researchers spent years asking whether dogs dream. Nobody asked what about. We did. Every dog dreams about the same thing.

Rule one. Do not wake it up.
It is not chasing rabbits. Your dog has never caught a rabbit in its life. It would not know what to do with one. It is chasing something away. Every night, while you sleep, your dog goes somewhere and stands in the doorway. If you wake it up in the middle of that, it has to come back. The other thing does not.

Rule two. Count the barks.
One bark is a warning. Two barks means it is getting close. Three barks means it is close to you. Do not get up. Pull the blanket over your feet. Your dog is fine. Your dog is the only one in this house who is working.

Rule three. Do not hold its paws.
When the legs are running, they are running somewhere. You will want to hold a paw to calm it down. One woman did. She said the paw was cold. And wet. Like it had been running through grass, outside, at night. Her dog had not left the bed. In the morning, there was mud on her sheets. Little paw prints. They went from the dog, to the window... and back.

Rule four. If it growls, look where its nose points.
A sleeping dog growls at one thing only. If its nose points at the door, close the door. If it points at the window, close the curtains. If it points under the bed... pull your hand back onto the mattress. Slowly. That is where it lost track of it last time.

Rule five. If the barking stops but the legs keep running, say its name.
This is the only time you speak. Once. Quietly. Do not shake it. Dogs know their names. Your voice is how it finds the way home. If the legs slow down, keep talking. Tell it about your day. It does not understand the words. It follows the sound. If the legs speed up... stop talking. It is running toward your voice. And something is right behind it.

Rule six. Let it sleep at the end of the bed.
You have wondered why it always sleeps there. At your feet. Facing the door. You thought it was being cute. It is choosing a position. You have bought it three dog beds. It has used none of them. It is not being difficult. Those beds are in the wrong place.

Rule seven. Think about your own dreams.
You have had the dream where something is chasing you. You cannot run. Your legs are too heavy. It is right behind you. And just before it catches you... you wake up. Everybody has that dream. Nobody ever gets caught. Ask yourself why.

Look at your dog in the morning. Look how tired it is. Dogs sleep twelve to fourteen hours a day. You thought that was lazy. It is not dreaming. You are. It is just the one who shows up.

So if your dog ever barks in its sleep, let it bark. And if it ever stops sleeping at the end of your bed... sleep with the lights on. You are on your own.

Thank you for calling Night Support. Please hold. Your dog is on the other line.

**Real facts:** small dogs dream roughly every 10 minutes, large dogs less often but longer (Stanley Coren, via
[AKC](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/lifestyle/do-dogs-dream/) and [Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/what-do-dogs-dream-about));
adult dogs sleep about 12–14 hours a day.

## 2. If Your Car Ever Beeps For The Empty Passenger Seat... DON'T LOOK

**Hook:** everyone's car has beeped at their groceries. **Twist:** highway hypnosis. You weren't driving. You were the passenger.

If your car ever beeps for the empty passenger seat, do not look at the seat. Do not reach across. And do not, under any circumstances, buckle it in.

You have heard this before. You put your bag on the seat. Your groceries. A pizza. The little seatbelt light came on and the car started beeping, like your pizza had a death wish. You moved it, and the beeping stopped. Tonight, there is nothing on the seat. It is still beeping.

There are seven rules. Six get you home. The seventh is about who drove.

Under the passenger seat is a sensor. It measures weight. If something on that seat weighs as much as a person, the car asks it to buckle up. The car does not know what a person is. It only knows what one weighs. Tonight, something weighs exactly that much.

Rule one. Do not look at the seat.
Eyes on the road. Hands on the wheel. You will feel the urge to glance over. Everybody does. Do not tilt the mirror either. The mirror was set for the road. Keep it that way.

Rule two. Do not put anything on the seat.
You will want to toss your bag over, so the beeping has a reason. Do not give it a reason. Do not give it anything. Things put on that seat tonight do not come back.

Rule three. Take the lit roads.
Stay under the streetlights. Every time you pass under one, the beeping will stop. Just for a second. Count the seconds between lights. If the beeping stops in the dark too... it has gotten used to you. That is not good news.

Rule four. Let it choose the music.
If the radio changes station by itself, leave it. Passengers pick the music. Everybody knows that. It has terrible taste. Do not say so. And do not turn it down. It turned it up for a reason. There is something it does not want you to hear.

Rule five. Do not stop until you are home.
Not for gas. Not for food. Not to check the tires. If a red light makes you stop, keep both hands on the wheel and look straight ahead. Something will tap on the window. Not the passenger window. Yours.

Rule six. When you park, do not look in the back seat.
Some cars can now tell when something moves in the back seat. Hyundai offers one. After you park and lock, a sensor watches the rear seats. If something moves, the car honks the horn, flashes the lights, and texts your phone. It was built so nobody forgets a child or a dog in a hot car. It was not built for this. Lock the car. Walk inside. If your phone buzzes, "movement detected in rear seat"... do not go back. You did not forget anything. It just has not gotten out yet.

Rule seven. Do not try to remember the drive.
Here is something true. You have driven home before and not remembered the drive. Not one turn. Not one light. You just arrived. Everybody has done it. It is called highway hypnosis. Experts say your brain drives on autopilot while your mind wanders.

Your mind was not wandering. It was moved. One seat to the right.

That is why the passenger seat weighed exactly as much as a person tonight. It was you. For the first time, you were awake for the ride.

So if your car ever beeps for the empty passenger seat, don't look. You might see who has been driving.

Thank you for calling Night Support. Please drive safely. Someone has to.

**Real facts:** passenger seats use a weight sensor for the seatbelt reminder (bags and groceries set it off);
Hyundai's Ultrasonic Rear Occupant Alert watches the back seat after locking, then honks, flashes and texts
([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/car-safety/hyundai-to-expand-child-monitoring-systems-to-more-models-by-2022), [Auto123](https://www.auto123.com/en/news/hyundai-system-prevent-forgotten-passengers/64025));
highway hypnosis, driving with no memory of the drive ([WSLS / Virginia Tech](https://www.wsls.com/news/2019/05/08/highway-hypnosis-is-real-and-we-all-do-it/)).

## 3. If A Crow Ever Leaves You A Gift... DO NOT PICK IT UP

**Hook:** the famous "girl who gets gifts from crows" story. **Twist:** if you already accepted, you're family now. And in a crow family, everyone eats.

If a crow ever leaves you a gift, do not pick it up. Do not say thank you. And do not, ever, leave something back.

You have heard the story. A little girl in Seattle fed the crows in her yard. Peanuts. Dog food. And the crows started bringing her things. Beads. Buttons. A paper clip. A little blue Lego. A pearl-coloured heart. She kept every one in a labelled bag, with the date she found it. It is a sweet story. It went around the world. The crows near you have heard it too.

There are seven rules. Crows can count. They are counting along.

Crows remember faces. Researchers in Seattle put on a caveman mask, caught a few crows, tagged them, and let them go. Then they walked around campus wearing that mask. The crows screamed at it. Dove at it. Five years later, they were still screaming. Including crows that had never been caught. Their parents taught them which face to hate. You have one face. It is already on file.

Rule one. Do not pick it up.
The first gift will be somewhere you will find it. Your windowsill. The hood of your car. Something small and shiny. Leave it there. Picking it up is accepting. Accepting is a deal. Nobody has ever read the terms.

Rule two. Do not feed them.
You fed one once. A fry, in a parking lot. It looked at you for a long time. It was not saying thank you. It was memorizing.

Rule three. If the gifts start to be yours, stay calm.
After a while, the gifts will change. Your earring. The one you lost last summer. A button from your old coat. The spare key you hid under the flowerpot. You never told anyone where that key was. Somebody watched you hide it.

Rule four. Do not change your face.
No new haircut. No sunglasses. No hood. They know you by your face. If you change it, they will not know who you are. Then you are a stranger. Ask the man in the caveman mask how they treat strangers.

Rule five. If they gather on your lawn, stay inside.
Crows hold funerals. That is real. When a crow dies, the others gather around the body and scream. Scientists studied it. They are not mourning. They are learning. Working out what killed it. Where. And who. If they are gathered on your lawn, and they are all facing your house... they think they know.

Rule six. If the gifts stop, check the windowsill.
One last gift will be waiting. On the inside of the windowsill. The inside. Your windows were locked.

Rule seven. If you already picked one up, feed them.
Every day. Same time. Same place. For the rest of your life. You are in a relationship now. It will be the most loyal relationship you ever have. They will follow you to work. They will scream at anyone who raises their voice at you. They will remember your birthday. They will remember everything.

And if one ever brings you a gift that is still warm... do not look at what it is. Just say thank you. They have decided you are family. And in a crow family, everyone eats.

So if a crow ever leaves you a gift, leave it where it is. If it is too late for that... buy peanuts. In bulk.

Thank you for calling Night Support. A crow has been listening to this call. Say hello. It already knows your voice.

**Real facts:** Gabi Mann, Seattle, received beads, a paper clip, a blue Lego and a pearl-coloured heart from crows she fed
([Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/seattle-girl-befriends-neighborhood-crows-making-bird-lovers-everywhere-jealous), [My Modern Met](https://mymodernmet.com/gabi-mann-gifts-from-crows));
crows scolded the "dangerous" caveman mask for five years, including crows never trapped (John Marzluff, University of Washington,
[The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/never-cross-a-crow-it-will-remember-your-face-2121));
crow "funerals" are used to learn about danger (Kaeli Swift, [UW thesis](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/handle/1773/33178), [KQED](https://kqed.org/science/1923458/youve-heard-of-a-murder-of-crows-how-about-a-crow-funeral)).

## 4. If You Ever Feel Like You're Falling As You Fall Asleep... DON'T GRAB THE BED

**Hook:** up to 7 in 10 people feel it, so almost every viewer has. **Twist:** the jump is something catching you. Three in ten never feel it, and nobody knows what happens to them.

If you ever feel like you are falling right as you fall asleep, do not grab the bed. Do not open your eyes. And whatever you do, do not say "I'm awake."

You know the feeling. You are drifting off. Then your whole body jumps, like you missed a step on the stairs. Your heart is pounding. Whoever is next to you asks if you are okay. You laugh. Up to seven out of ten people feel it. Doctors call it a hypnic jerk. They say it is harmless. They say it like they have checked.

There are seven rules. You will need them tonight. You felt it last night too.

There is a theory. Our ancestors slept in trees. As they fell asleep, their muscles relaxed, and the brain thought: we are falling out of the tree. So it jerked them awake, to grab the branch. It is a nice theory. Here is what it leaves out. Next time it happens, ask yourself which way you were falling. Not off the side. Down. Through the bed.

Rule one. Do not grab the bed.
When you jump, your hands will want to grab the mattress. Keep them still. Grabbing tells it exactly where you are. It feels around in the dark, the way you feel for a light switch. Do not be the light switch.

Rule two. Count the jumps.
One jump is normal. Two in a row means it has a grip on your ankle. Three means you are not in your bed anymore. You just have not noticed. Keep your eyes closed. Think about something boring. Taxes. A spreadsheet. It loses interest in boring people.

Rule three. If you cannot move, do not try.
Every night, your brain switches off most of your muscles so you do not act out your dreams. Sometimes you wake up before they switch back on. You can see your room. You cannot move. Doctors call this sleep paralysis. A lot of people see a figure in the corner of the room. Doctors call that a hallucination. Do not argue with it. And do not try to move. As long as you cannot move, you look asleep. It does not bother sleeping people. They are not finished yet.

Rule four. If you hear a bang, do not get up.
Some people hear a loud bang just as they fall asleep. A gunshot. A door slamming. Nobody else hears it. Doctors named this one, and they did not try very hard. It is called exploding head syndrome. That is the real name. We checked. It is not a door slamming. It is a door closing. Between where you were going, and where you are. It closed in time. That is good news. Go back to sleep.

Rule five. If you dream you are falling, look up.
In the dream, you might be falling down a stairwell. Off a roof. Into dark water. Do not look down. Look up. Someone will be reaching for you. Take the hand. Do not look at the face.

Rule six. Do not sleep on your back.
People who sleep on their backs report sleep paralysis more often. Researchers found that too. Face the wall, or face the room. Never face the ceiling. If you open your eyes, the ceiling is the first thing it wants you to see.

Rule seven. Say thank you.
The jump is not you catching yourself. It is someone catching you. Every night, something pulls you down as you fall asleep. And every night, something else grabs you and yanks you back up. That is the jump. That is your heart pounding. You have been rescued hundreds of times. You never once said thank you.

Seven in ten people feel it. Three in ten never do. Nobody knows what happens to those three. They say they sleep very well.

So if you ever feel like you are falling as you fall asleep, don't grab the bed. Let it catch you. And whisper thank you. It is very tired. It has been holding on to you your whole life.

Thank you for calling Night Support. Please stay on the line. Somebody is holding.

**Real facts:** 60–70% of people get hypnic jerks, often with a falling sensation; the "primate falling out of a tree"
idea is a real but unproven hypothesis (Frederick Coolidge) ([Live Science](https://livescience.com/39225-why-people-twitch-falling-asleep.html), [Sleep.com](https://sleep.com/sleep-health/hypnic-jerks));
REM muscle paralysis and sleep paralysis; "exploding head syndrome" is the real medical name for hearing a bang while falling asleep.

## 5. If An Elevator Ever Stops On A Floor Nobody Pressed... DON'T GET OFF

**Hook:** two facts nobody knows: the "door close" button often does nothing, and most tall buildings hide floor 13.
**Twist:** the rules are a placebo too, like the button.

If an elevator ever stops on a floor nobody pressed, do not get off. Do not hold the door. And do not press "door close." It does not work. It never has.

That part is true. In a lot of elevators, the door close button does nothing. It was switched off decades ago, so the doors stay open long enough for everyone to get in. They left the button there anyway. It makes people feel better. You have pressed it maybe a thousand times. You felt better every time.

There are seven rules. Unlike that button, they work.

Here is another true thing. Most tall buildings do not have a thirteenth floor. The elevator company Otis once estimated that about eighty-five percent of its tall buildings skip the number. The floor is still there. It is just called fourteen. Somebody decided you would rather not know what floor you are on. They were right.

Rule one. Do not get off.
The doors will open. The hallway will look like every other floor. Same carpet. Same lights. Same little sign with room numbers and an arrow. Look closer at the sign. The arrow points both ways. Do not step out. Stand still. Let the doors close on their own. They will. Eventually.

Rule two. Do not hold the door.
If someone comes running toward the elevator, do not hold the door for them. You will want to. You were raised well. Let it close. Nobody runs on this floor. Nobody here is late for anything.

Rule three. Read the display.
Look at the number above the door. If it says fourteen, you are fine. Mostly. If it says thirteen... it was never supposed to say that. There is no thirteen button. Whatever brought you here did not need one.

Rule four. If someone gets on, face the doors.
They will press a button. Do not look at which one. They will stand a little too close to you. That part is normal. People do that in elevators. Here is what is not normal. They will not look at their phone. Everybody in an elevator looks at their phone.

Rule five. If it stops between floors, do not climb out.
Elevators stop between floors all the time. It is usually a sensor. Someone will come. Do not pry the doors. Do not climb through the hatch in the ceiling. You have seen that in movies. In the movies, there is nothing waiting on the roof of the elevator.

Rule six. Count the dings on the way down.
When it moves again, count the floors. If you went up twelve floors, you should hear twelve dings coming down. Count carefully. If you hear thirteen... you are somewhere that takes one more floor to leave. When the doors open on a lobby, do not get off. It will look exactly like your lobby. Wait for the next one.

Rule seven. Remember the button.
Earlier, we said these rules work. Unlike the door close button. That was not true. These rules are a door close button. They do not make the elevator do anything. They are here so you feel better while it decides.

You have been pressing rules for four minutes. You do feel better. That was the point.

The elevator has decided.

So if an elevator ever stops on a floor nobody pressed, do not get off. Stand still. Feel better. It is easier for everyone.

Thank you for calling Night Support. You have reached the thirteenth floor. Please hold.

**Real facts:** many US elevators' "door close" buttons don't work for passengers since the 1990 ADA (2016 New York Times report, via
[CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/door-close-elevator-button-dont-work-placebo));
Otis estimated in 2002 that about 85% of its buildings with 13+ floors have no floor labeled 13
([Wikipedia, Thirteenth floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_floor)).
