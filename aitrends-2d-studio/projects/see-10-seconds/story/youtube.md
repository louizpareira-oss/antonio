**Description:**

You step back from the curb. One second later, a bike flies past exactly where you were standing. You didn't hear it.
You didn't see it coming. You saw it ten seconds ago.

What would it be like to live with a faint blue copy of the world running ten seconds ahead? You'd win every game of
rock, paper, scissors, save every penalty and never step in a puddle again. But every joke would reach you twice,
surprise parties would stop existing, and one Friday at 4:12 p.m. ten seconds might be the difference between seeing an
accident and stopping it.

What would YOU do with ten seconds of the future? Tell us in the comments.

**The science (fact notes):**
- Your brain processes what your eyes see roughly 0.1 s after it happens, so perception lags reality slightly.
- To hide that delay, the brain predicts where moving objects are about to be (the flash-lag effect is one result).
- A 95 mph fastball covers the 60.5 ft from mound to plate in about 0.4 s, so batters swing at the predicted ball.
- Ten seconds ahead is 100 times further than the brain's own ~0.1 s prediction.
