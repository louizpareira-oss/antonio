# 9. Getting anime and cartoon visuals — legitimately

You want the anime look. That is a good instinct: it is your audience's language, it is
why the reference video works, and there is no reason to give it up.

What you have to give up is **downloading it**. Ghibli, Netflix and Sony own those frames,
and TikTok reads them as "random shots." The look is not the problem. The *source* is.

Three ways to get anime visuals that are yours. Ranked by how fast you can start.

---

## Route 1 — Stylize your own footage (cheapest, fastest, most underrated)

**Film it on your phone, then turn it into anime.**

CapCut and most modern editors carry AI style-transfer effects — usually under
**Effects → AI effects**, labelled *Anime*, *Manga*, *Cartoon*, *AI Portrait* or similar
(the names change between versions, so look through the AI effects tab). Point it at real
footage and it re-renders it as animation.

The workflow:

1. Film the shot for real. You walking away down a street. Rain on a window. Your hand on
   a door handle. 5 seconds is enough.
2. Drop it into CapCut, apply the anime style effect.
3. Grade it cold, add light grain.

**Why this is the best route for you right now:**

- It is free and you can start today.
- The footage is 100% yours — you filmed it. There is nothing to flag, ever.
- It solves the matching problem completely: you are not hunting for a clip that shows
  someone lowering their eyes. You film someone lowering their eyes.
- It exports at full resolution, so you get real 1080×1920.

The style effects are not perfect. They wobble, faces shift. **Use that** — shoot wide,
shoot backs and silhouettes and hands, and the wobble reads as style instead of error.

## Route 2 — Generate it (best quality, closest to the reference)

Write the shot, generate it in anime style, natively vertical.

### The anime prompt formula

```
[shot size] + [character] + [action] + [environment] + [time/weather] + [light]
+ [anime style block] + [mood] + 9:16 vertical
```

### The style block — copy this

Do **not** name a studio or a living artist. Many tools block it, and it is imitation
rather than authorship. Describe the qualities instead — you get a better, more
controllable result:

> *"cel-shaded anime, hand-painted watercolour background, soft gradient sky, muted
> nostalgic palette, warm rim light, subtle film grain, 90s anime aesthetic, clean line
> art, atmospheric perspective, anime film still"*

Swap pieces for different moods:

| Mood you want | Swap in |
|---|---|
| Wistful, nostalgic | *"golden hour haze, soft focus, warm dusty palette, gentle bloom"* |
| Cold, lonely | *"cool blue-grey palette, flat overcast light, high contrast shadows"* |
| Dreamy, unreal | *"pastel palette, glowing highlights, soft edges, floating light particles"* |
| Bleak, heavy | *"desaturated, near-monochrome, heavy grain, hard shadow, sparse composition"* |
| Retro / VHS | *"90s cel animation, visible line art, slight colour bleed, analogue grain"* |

### Keeping the same character across shots

This is the thing that separates a real video from a slideshow. Four techniques, use all four:

1. **Copy-paste the character description word for word** in every single prompt. Not
   "a young man" then "a guy" — the *identical string*:
   > *"a young man in his mid-twenties, dark overcoat, black hair, thin build"*
2. **Lock the seed** if your tool exposes one. Same seed + same character string = same person.
3. **Generate one character reference image first**, then use the tool's reference-image /
   image-to-video feature for every shot.
4. **Hide the face.** Shoot him from behind, in silhouette, from a distance, or in shadow.
   If you cannot see the face, it cannot be inconsistent. This is the trick that does the
   most work — and it happens to be the most emotional way to frame a lone figure anyway.

### Tools

Any current image or video generator will do this — the prompt formula matters far more
than the tool. Generate stills and animate them with an image-to-video pass if you cannot
generate video directly; a slow push-in on a still anime frame looks *exactly* like the
reference video, because half the reference video is basically that.

## Route 3 — Public domain and licensed animation

Real, but narrower than people think:

- **Public domain animation** exists — early Fleischer cartoons, some 1930s shorts, early
  Betty Boop entries. Archive.org hosts them. But the look is 1930s rubber-hose black and
  white. It does not match your niche at all.
- **Licensed stock** — Storyblocks, Artgrid, Envato carry animated loops and anime-style
  motion backgrounds under a paid license that permits monetized use. Legal and safe, but
  it is stock: other creators use the same clips, and "everyone has this footage" is its own
  kind of unoriginal.

Useful as a supplement. Not a foundation.

**Not a route:** Giphy, Tenor, Pinterest, eyecannndy. None of them grant you the right to
put a studio's frames in a monetized video. Whatever the course says.

## The anime shot vocabulary

Anime carries emotion through a small set of recurring compositions. Learn these fifteen
and you can visualize any line you write. This is the anime version of the translation
table in [`07`](07-matching-visuals-to-dialogue.md).

| Emotion | The iconic anime composition |
|---|---|
| Loneliness | A single figure on an empty train platform at night, vending machine glow |
| Longing | A hand pressed against a train window, city sliding past |
| Time passing | Clouds moving fast over a still town. Cicadas, summer haze |
| Regret | Walking up long stone steps, turning to look back at the town below |
| Distance between people | Two figures at opposite ends of an empty classroom, desks between |
| Confession / truth | A rooftop at dusk, fence, wind, two silhouettes |
| Grief | Rain, a black umbrella, standing still while others walk |
| Isolation in a crowd | A scramble crossing from above, one person not moving |
| Peace | A flooded rice field reflecting the sky. Nothing moving |
| Memory | A room at golden hour, dust in the light, empty chair |
| Freedom | Cycling downhill toward the sea, no hands |
| Anxiety | A corridor of identical doors, receding, slightly too long |
| Small in the world | A tiny figure at the bottom of a huge sky |
| Waiting | A bus stop in snow, breath visible, nobody coming |
| Letting go | A paper or ribbon lifted away by wind, watched until it's gone |

Notice: almost none of them contain a face, and none of them show the thing the words say.
That is why they work.

## The honest comparison

| | Downloaded Ghibli clips | Your anime visuals |
|---|---|---|
| Time per video | 30 min hunting | 45 min first time, 20 min once you have a library |
| Matches your script | Rarely — you take what exists | Always — you specify it |
| Resolution | 480p source, upscaled badly | Native 1080×1920 |
| Creator Rewards | Never eligible | Eligible |
| Copyright exposure | Real | None |
| Anyone else has it | Thousands of pages | Nobody |

That last row is the one that matters in a year. Every page in this niche is pulling from
the same few hundred Ghibli scenes. Yours would look like yours.
