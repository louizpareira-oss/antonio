# 11. Speaking English clearly

Being understood is not about having a small accent or a big vocabulary. People
understand you when four things are right:

1. **Rhythm** — the right syllables are long and loud, the rest are short and quiet.
2. **Endings** — the last sound of each word actually arrives.
3. **Speed and pauses** — slower than you think, with real gaps.
4. **Volume and posture** — the sound leaves your body.

Fix those four and a strong accent stops mattering. Ignore them and perfect grammar
still gets you "sorry, what?"

**An accent is not a defect.** The goal here is clear, not British.

---

## 1. Rhythm: the thing that matters most

English is a **stress-timed** language. Portuguese and Spanish are syllable-timed — every
syllable gets roughly equal time. That difference, not your vowels, is what makes
listeners struggle.

In English, in every sentence:
- **Content words are strong** — nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, question words.
- **Function words are weak and squashed** — a, the, to, of, for, and, was, can, have, that.

> **I** WANT to **GO** to the **BEACH** on **SAT**urday.

The capitals are long and loud. Everything between them is fast and almost swallowed:
"to" becomes *tuh*, "the" becomes *thuh*, "and" becomes *'n*, "was" becomes *wuz*,
"can" becomes *kn*. Squashing them is not lazy or rude — it is **correct English**, and
it is what makes the strong words stand out.

**Drill (5 min).** Take any sentence. Say it hitting only the strong words, like hitting
a drum, exaggerated to the point of silly. Then say it normally. Do ten sentences a day.

### Word stress
Every multi-syllable word has one strong syllable, and putting it in the wrong place is
the #1 cause of "what?" — more than any consonant.

| Word | Stress | Not |
|---|---|---|
| **CO**mfortable | KUMF-ter-bul | com-for-TAB-le |
| **VEG**etable | VEJ-tuh-bul | ve-ge-TA-ble |
| **PHO**tograph / pho**TO**grapher | two different words, two different stresses | |
| ho**TEL** | ho-TEL | HO-tel |
| de**VEL**op | de-VEL-op | DE-ve-lop |
| **IN**teresting | IN-truh-sting | in-te-RES-ting |
| **COM**fortable, **CHOC**olate, **BUS**iness | all 2–3 syllables in speech, not 4 | |

**Rule:** when you learn a new word, learn *where the stress is* at the same time. Write
it down in CAPS. A word with the wrong stress is a word nobody recognises.

## 2. Endings: finish your words

The most common clarity killer for Portuguese and Spanish speakers is **dropping or
softening the final consonant**.

- "I wan**t**" → sounds like "I wan"
- "my frien**d**s" → "my fren"
- "he as**ked**" → "he ass"
- "I live**d** there" → "I live there" (your listener now hears present tense — the whole
  meaning changed)

**Past tense -ed** is three different sounds. Learn them once:
| After this sound | -ed says | Example |
|---|---|---|
| t / d | **id** | wanted = WANT-id, needed = NEED-id |
| voiceless (p, k, s, sh, ch, f) | **t** | walked = WALKT, washed = WASHT, helped = HELPT |
| everything else (voiced) | **d** | lived = LIVD, played = PLAYD, called = CALLD |

**Plural -s** is two sounds: **s** after voiceless (books = BOOKS), **z** after voiced
(dogs = DOGZ, jobs = JOBZ). And **never** add a vowel in front of s-words:
*school*, not "eschool"; *Spain*, not "Espain"; *street*, not "estreet." Start the word
directly on the **s**. Practise: "s-s-s-street."

**Drill (3 min).** Read any paragraph aloud and exaggerate every final consonant until it
feels ridiculous. That level of exaggeration is roughly normal to a native ear.

---

## 3. Speed, pauses, and fillers

Fast speech is not fluent speech. Fluent speech is **grouped** speech.

- **Speak in chunks of 3–6 words, then pause.**
  > "I've been working on this account | for about a year | and it finally started moving."
- **Slow down by 20%.** You will feel like you are speaking strangely slowly. To the
  listener it will sound confident. Nervous people speed up; calm people take their time.
- **Pause instead of filling.** When you need the next word, close your mouth and think.
  Silence reads as confident. "ahhh… ehhh… you know… like…" reads as nervous, and it is
  the single fastest fix in this document.
- **When you lose the word, say so plainly:** "How do you say… the thing you keep money
  in?" That is charming. Freezing and going quiet is not.

**Drill (2 min).** Record 60 seconds about your day. Listen and count the fillers. Record
again and replace every filler with a closed-mouth pause. Do this daily for a week — the
count drops fast.

---

## 4. Volume, posture, and the mouth

- Stand or sit **straight**, shoulders back, chin level. A collapsed chest gives a thin,
  quiet voice.
- Breathe from the belly, not the top of the chest. Speak on the way out of a full breath.
- **Open your mouth more than feels natural.** Most unclear English is under-articulated,
  not mispronounced.
- Speak **to the back of the room**, not to your own chin. Aim your voice at her ear, not
  at the floor.
- Look at the person. Eyes down = voice down.

## 5. Sounds worth fixing (Portuguese / Spanish speakers)

Skip this section if it is not your first language. Do not try to fix all of them —
pick **two per week**.

| Sound | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **th** (think, three) | becomes *t* or *f* | Tongue tip lightly between the teeth, blow. Feel the air. |
| **th** (this, mother) | becomes *d* | Same tongue position, but with voice buzzing. |
| **h** (house, here) | disappears | Push a real puff of air: "h-h-house." |
| **r** (red, right) | rolled or becomes *h* | Pull the tongue **back**, touching nothing. It should feel like a growl, not a tap. |
| **v** vs **b** (very / berry) | merged | v = top teeth on bottom lip. b = lips together. Practise "very berry" ten times. |
| **i** vs **ee** (ship / sheep, bit / beat) | both become *ee* | Short one: relax the mouth, make it fast and lazy. Long one: smile, stretch it. |
| **-ing** (going, talking) | becomes *een* or *ink* | Back of tongue up, nose open, no hard *g* at the end. |
| final **l** (well, full) | becomes *u* | Tongue tip touches behind the top teeth and stays there. |
| **s** at start (school, Spain) | gets an *e* in front | Start on the s. "sss-chool." |

**Drill (5 min).** Minimal pairs. Say each pair ten times, slowly:
ship/sheep · bit/beat · very/berry · think/sink · free/three · day/they · full/fool ·
walk/work · man/men · live/leave

## 6. Shadowing — the exercise that beats everything else

This is the one that actually changes how you sound.

1. Pick **20–40 seconds** of a native speaker you want to sound like — a podcast, an
   interview, a YouTube video. Same clip for a whole week.
2. Listen 3 times with no talking. Hear the rhythm, not the words.
3. Play it and speak **at the same time as them**, a half-second behind, copying the
   melody, the speed, and the pauses — not just the words.
4. Record yourself doing it. Play both back.
5. Repeat until you can do the clip without reading it.

Twenty minutes a day of this for a month does more than a year of grammar study. Copy
**rhythm and melody** first — vowels come free afterwards.

---

## 7. Phrases that buy you time (memorise these)

When you are searching for a word, do not freeze. Use one of these, then keep going:

- "How do you say…"
- "What's the word — …"
- "Let me put it another way."
- "Sorry, I'll start again."
- "I mean…"
- "Something like that, you know what I mean?"
- "Say that again? / Sorry, one more time?" ← use this instead of nodding when you did
  not understand. Nodding at something you did not hear always ends badly.

And to slow the other person down: **"Can you slow down a bit? Your English is too fast
for me."** Said with a smile, this is charming and completely normal. Nobody thinks less
of you for it.

---

## 8. The 15-minute daily routine

Do this every day. It is short on purpose.

| Min | What |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Read a paragraph aloud, exaggerating **every final consonant**. |
| 2–5 | Stress drill: 10 sentences, hit only the strong words like a drum. |
| 5–10 | Shadowing: this week's clip, 4–5 passes. |
| 10–12 | Minimal pairs: this week's two sounds, ten reps each. |
| 12–15 | Record 60 seconds talking about your day. Count fillers. Say the same 60 seconds again with pauses instead of fillers. |

**Once a week:** listen to Monday's recording and Sunday's back to back. That is the only
progress check you need.

---

## 9. Talking to her, specifically

Everything above is training. In a real conversation, do only these five things:

1. **Slow down.** 20% slower than your instinct.
2. **Finish your words.** Especially the last consonant.
3. **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. Short and clear beats long and impressive,
   always — for native speakers too.
4. **Pause instead of "ehhh."**
5. **When you don't understand, ask.** "Sorry, one more time?" Two seconds, no damage.
   Guessing and answering the wrong question is the damage.

Your accent is not a problem to hide. Being hard to hear is. And if she likes you, your
accent is a feature — but only if she can understand what you are saying with it.
