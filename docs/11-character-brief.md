## Why this character is yours, not a copy

The pages we measured use a wolf in a suit, a pig, and a bulldog. This character shares none of
that: different animal, different costume, different attitude (playful, not smug), and a built-in
reason to be explaining hidden mechanisms (he is a stage magician).

Kept from the technique (nobody owns these): a recurring cast, a strong close-up of the face for
the punchline, one consistent art style.

## The character in one paragraph

A masked raccoon stage magician. Grey fur, dark eye mask, ringed tail. A cheeky lopsided grin and
one eyebrow that goes up before every reveal. A small black top hat tilted to one side, a deep plum
cape lined with gold stars, a cream shirt, white gloves. He carries one playing card, which he
uses like a pointer. He never explains anything without first pretending it is magic.

**Signature moves (use in every video):**
1. The eyebrow: goes up right before the reveal.
2. The card: appears between two fingers when he says "here is the trick".
3. The cape flip: the cape swirls across the screen as the transition into the story.

**Palette (fixed):** plum `#5B2A6E` cape, gold `#D9A520` stars and card edge, warm cream `#F3E6CC`
shirt and background, deep teal `#1F5560` night, grey `#8A8F98` fur, near-black `#2A2530` mask.
Gold is also the selective-colour accent in every story shot.

## Voice and behaviour

- He addresses the viewer directly and teases them first ("You picked the four-dollar one. Of course
  you did.").
- Then he does the magic-trick move: shows the trick, then shows how it is done.
- Dry, quick, never cruel. The joke is always on the trick, not on the viewer.
- Never says "they are lying to you". He says "here is what they are counting on".

## Consistency rules (so he looks the same in every video)

1. Always use the locked reference sheet as an image reference for every shot he appears in.
2. Never change: mask shape, ringed tail, hat, cape colour, white gloves.
3. Generate him in the same art style as the reference (gouache picture book).
4. If a generated shot drifts (wrong hat, wrong colours), throw it away. Do not fix it in the edit.
5. Give him no text on clothing, and put no words on the card. Add any text in the editor.
