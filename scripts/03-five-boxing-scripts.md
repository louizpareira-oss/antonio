# Script 03 — Five boxing explainer scripts

Built with [`prompts/master-boxing-explainer.md`](../prompts/master-boxing-explainer.md): HOOK → PROBLEM →
REFRAME → TURN → LANDING, 150–175 words, lines 3–12 words, calm coach tone, no real fighters, no links.

What makes these spread, taken from [`docs/10`](../docs/10-jabmax-reference-teardown.md):
- **The hook flips something the viewer believes about themselves.** "You're not out of shape" works
  better than "breathing tips".
- **One mechanism and one drill.** Something they can try tonight is something they save.
- **A two-line landing that mirrors itself** ("Punches show them… / Feints show you…"). That's the line people repeat.
- **The last card is a question, never a link.** Comments push the video further than likes do.

Blank line = 0.5s breath. Runtimes assume 145 wpm plus breaths plus a 2s end hold.

| # | Title | Words | Runtime | Who shares it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You're Not Out of Shape | 151 | ~69s | Every beginner who gasses out in round two. Widest audience. |
| 2 | The Heavy Bag Is Lying | 151 | ~70s | Anyone who trains at a gym. Calls out a habit they all have. |
| 3 | Stop Aiming at the Head | 161 | ~73s | Fight fans. Head vs body is an argument they'll have in the comments. |
| 4 | Your Best Punch Is Fake | 152 | ~69s | People who spar. The "lying" angle and the mirrored landing. |
| 5 | Your Punch Isn't Over | 150 | ~68s | Every coach who has yelled "hands up". Gets tagged to friends. |

**Make #1 first.** It speaks to the biggest audience, including people who don't box yet.

## 1. YOU'RE NOT OUT OF SHAPE

**Hook:** "You're not out of shape. You're holding your breath."
**End card (editor, no voice):** `WHAT ROUND DO YOU GAS OUT? / COMMENT IT.`

```
[HOOK] You're not out of shape.
[HOOK] You're holding your breath.

[PROBLEM] Round one, you feel great.
[PROBLEM] Round two, your arms feel like concrete.

[PROBLEM] So you run more miles.
[PROBLEM] And next week, it happens again.
[PROBLEM] Because the problem isn't only your lungs. [FACT]

[REFRAME] Watch what you do when you punch.
[REFRAME] Your shoulders climb. Your fists squeeze. Your breath stops. [FACT]

[REFRAME] You're not tired from punching.
[REFRAME] You're tired from bracing between punches. [FACT]
[REFRAME] Every second you're tense, you're burning fuel. [FACT]

[TURN] Fix it with one sound.
[TURN] A short, sharp exhale on every punch. [FACT]
[TURN] Teeth together. One quick hiss.
[TURN] It pushes air out, so the next breath comes easy. [FACT]

[TURN] Then let your shoulders drop.
[TURN] Between punches, you breathe in. Loose.
[TURN] On the punch, you hiss out. Tight.

[LANDING] Shadowbox one round with only that sound.
[LANDING] If you hear silence, you were holding it.
[LANDING] Then the bag. Then sparring.

[LANDING] Conditioning isn't just how much air you have.

[LANDING] It's how much you waste.
```

**Fact flags:** Breath-holding and tension under pressure are common in beginners (coaching
consensus). A sharp exhale on each punch is standard coaching. "Isn't *only* your lungs" is
deliberate: cardio fitness still matters, so don't claim it doesn't.

## 2. THE HEAVY BAG IS LYING

**Hook:** "The heavy bag is teaching you bad habits."
**End card:** `WHAT'S YOUR WORST BAG HABIT? / COMMENT IT.`

```
[HOOK] The heavy bag is teaching you bad habits.
[HOOK] Because it never hits back.

[PROBLEM] So your hands drop after every punch.
[PROBLEM] You stand square, right in front of it.
[PROBLEM] You throw, then admire the swing.

[PROBLEM] The bag lets you.
[PROBLEM] A person won't.

[REFRAME] Every round on the bag is practice.
[REFRAME] What you repeat there shows up in sparring. [FACT]

[REFRAME] Under pressure, you don't rise to your plan.
[REFRAME] You fall back to your habits. [FACT]

[TURN] So make the bag hit back.
[TURN] Picture its counter after every combination.
[TURN] Hands back to your face before the bag stops moving.

[TURN] Then move. A step, a pivot, or a slip.
[TURN] Never finish standing where you started.

[TURN] When it swings back at you, treat it like a jab.
[TURN] Slip it. Don't push it away.

[LANDING] Three rounds this way will feel slower.
[LANDING] Your punch count drops. Your habits change.

[LANDING] Don't hit the bag like a bag.

[LANDING] Hit it like it hits back.
```

**Fact flags:** "Habits on the bag carry into sparring" is coaching consensus, not a study, so
keep it phrased as "shows up". Slipping the swinging bag is a common drill. Keep the bag's swing
gentle.

## 3. STOP AIMING AT THE HEAD

**Hook:** "Stop aiming at the head."
**End card:** `HEAD OR BODY FIRST? / COMMENT IT.`

```
[HOOK] Stop aiming at the head.
[HOOK] It's the hardest target in the ring.

[PROBLEM] The head is small. It moves. It's guarded.
[PROBLEM] So you throw ten punches at it.
[PROBLEM] Most hit gloves. Some miss. One lands light.
[PROBLEM] And you're the one getting tired.

[REFRAME] Now look lower.
[REFRAME] The body is bigger. It can't move like the head. [FACT]

[REFRAME] And every shot there costs them something. [FACT]
[REFRAME] Breath. Legs. Hand speed.

[REFRAME] It doesn't look like much in round one.
[REFRAME] By the late rounds, they feel every one. [FACT]

[TURN] Here's the setup.
[TURN] Jab to the head. Make them lift their guard.
[TURN] Then bend your knees and go under it.
[TURN] Don't lean. Level change, then punch. [FACT]

[TURN] Lead hook to the body, under their elbow.
[TURN] Then right back up to the head.

[LANDING] Head, body, head.
[LANDING] Up, down, up. Until they can't guard both.
[LANDING] One round on the bag. Every combo touches the body.

[LANDING] Hunt the head and you'll chase it all night.

[LANDING] Take the body and the head comes to you.
```

**Fact flags:** "Ten punches… one lands" is an illustration, not a statistic. Keep it on screen
as a picture, never as a number. Body shots wearing an opponent down is coaching consensus.
Level-changing with the knees instead of leaning is standard. The landing echoes an old boxing
saying and isn't attributed to anyone.

## 4. YOUR BEST PUNCH IS FAKE

**Hook:** "Your best punch is the one you fake."
**End card:** `DO YOU FEINT? BE HONEST. / COMMENT IT.`

```
[HOOK] Your best punch is the one you fake.
[HOOK] It never lands. It doesn't need to.

[PROBLEM] Most beginners throw everything for real.
[PROBLEM] So every punch tells the truth.
[PROBLEM] Your opponent sees it, blocks it, and waits.
[PROBLEM] You're easy to read, because you never lie.

[REFRAME] A feint is a question.
[REFRAME] You ask, "What do you do when I jab?"
[REFRAME] They answer with their body.

[REFRAME] Hands up. A flinch. A step back. A counter.
[REFRAME] Now you know where the opening will be. [FACT]

[TURN] Keep it small.
[TURN] A quick dip of the lead shoulder.
[TURN] Half a step. A twitch of the glove.
[TURN] Watch what they do.

[TURN] If their hands rise, the body is open. [FACT]
[TURN] If they lean back, step in with the real one.
[TURN] Feint the jab. Throw the jab. Same look. [FACT]

[LANDING] Next sparring round, feint before every real punch.
[LANDING] Count how often they bite.

[LANDING] Punches show them what you have.

[LANDING] Feints show you what they have.
```

**Fact flags:** All three [FACT] lines are coaching consensus: feints draw reactions, raised hands
open the body, and a feint has to look like the real punch. Nothing here needs a number.

## 5. YOUR PUNCH ISN'T OVER

**Hook:** "Your punch isn't over when it lands."
**End card:** `DO YOU LEAVE YOUR JAB OUT? / COMMENT IT.`

```
[HOOK] Your punch isn't over when it lands.
[HOOK] It's over when your hand is home.

[PROBLEM] You throw the jab. It connects.
[PROBLEM] For half a second, you watch it.
[PROBLEM] Your hand hangs out there. Your chin is open.
[PROBLEM] That half second is the counter's window. [FACT]

[REFRAME] The return is the other half of the punch.
[REFRAME] Most people only train the way out.

[REFRAME] Watch skilled fighters in slow motion.
[REFRAME] The hand snaps back as fast as it went. [FACT]
[REFRAME] Same path. Same speed. Back to the chin.

[TURN] Here's how you train it.
[TURN] Make the return the part you focus on.
[TURN] On the bag, touch and snap back.

[TURN] Count "one" on the way out. "Two" on the way home.
[TURN] If "two" comes late, you're still admiring it.
[TURN] Rear hand stays glued to your chin. [FACT]

[LANDING] After every combination, move your head.
[LANDING] Hand home. Head off the line.

[LANDING] Anyone can throw a punch out.

[LANDING] Fighters bring it home.
```

**Fact flags:** "Half a second" is a figure of speech. Don't put it on screen as a measured time.
"Snaps back as fast as it went" is the coaching ideal, not a measurement. Rear hand at the chin is
standard guard coaching.
