# Script 02 — "Why Prices End in .99"

**Target length:** about 104 seconds (187 words, roughly 108 words a minute including pauses)
**Format:** 1080 × 1920, full bleed, no bars
**Shots:** 30 · **Average shot:** 3.5 s
**Host:** **Sleeve**, the raccoon magician (see `docs/11-character-brief.md`)
**Status:** first draft. Topic and script are my defaults, the character and style are yours. Nothing
here has been generated yet, so no credits are spent on this script.

---

## The idea underneath

Most "why is it priced like that" videos say *retailers are tricking you*. That is a slogan.
This one makes a smaller, sharper claim:

> The "9" is not a discount. It is a signal your brain reads before you do the maths. Even the
> popular story about where it came from is probably just a story.

The frame is a **small theatre stage**. Sleeve, a playful raccoon magician, asks a simple question
and lets the viewer get it wrong. Then he does what a magician does: shows the trick, then shows how
it is done. At the end the viewer gets one tiny habit: **round it up in your head**. That is the
reason to follow and the reason to rewatch.

## The voiceover script

**Record this in your own voice**, straight through. Each line break is a breath. Do not rush; if
your recording comes in under 95 seconds, you are rushing. Slow down, do not add words.

```
Which is cheaper: four dollars, or three ninety-nine?

You said three ninety-nine.

It's one cent.

So why does it feel like a discount?

Let's look in the hat.

Nine. Nine. Nine. Nine.

The popular story starts in the eighteen-hundreds.

Shopkeepers didn't trust their cashiers.

An odd price meant opening the drawer for change. Every sale, recorded.

It's a great story. Nobody has properly proved it.

Here's what has been proved.

You read left to right.

Three ninety-nine? Your brain grabs the three, and files it as three-something.

Four dollars? That's a four.

One cent. A whole category.

Researchers call it the left-digit effect.

In one catalogue test, a dress sold more after its price rose to end in nine.

The higher price won.

And the nine has a second job.

Nine whispers sale. Round numbers whisper quality.

So the tag isn't telling you the price.

It's telling you how to feel about it.

Next time at the till, round it up in your head.

Three ninety-nine is four dollars.

Still want it?

Then buy it. Your choice, not the tag's.

Follow for one hidden habit a day.
```

**Word count:** 187. The line "Follow for one hidden habit a day." is a placeholder: replace it with whatever your
account really promises. Sleeve's voice is dry and teasing. Deliver "You said three ninety-nine."
with a grin.

## Facts to check before you record

I wrote this from what I know, and I have **not** verified it against the papers. A wrong claim in a
video with your name on it is your problem, so check each row. If a row fails, use the fallback.

| Claim in the script | Where to check | If it does not hold up |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers anchor on the leftmost digit ("left-digit effect") | Thomas & Morwitz (2005), *Penny Wise and Pound Foolish: The Left-Digit Effect in Price Cognition*, Journal of Consumer Research | Cut lines 12–16 and keep the hook and the ending |
| A dress sold more after its price rose to a 9-ending | Anderson & Simester (2003), *Effects of $9 Price Endings on Retail Sales: Evidence from Field Experiments*, Quantitative Marketing and Economics. Check the direction and the exact prices | Replace with: "Stores have tested it, and the nine often wins." Or cut the two lines |
| 9-endings signal a deal; round prices feel like quality | Search for Wadhwa & Zhang (2015) on round prices, and the price-ending literature | Cut "Round numbers whisper quality." and keep "Nine whispers sale." |
| The cash-drawer origin story | Widely told, thinly documented | The script already says "nobody has properly proved it". Keep that line, it is the honest version |

The eighteen-hundreds date belongs to the story, not to the proof. Keep the phrase "the popular story".

## Pacing and edit rules (from the measured videos)

- **Speech:** about 108 words a minute *including* pauses. That is slower than the original we
  measured (179) and in line with the copies (102 to 114). Silence after the punchlines (shots 3, 21, 29)
  is doing work. Do not fill it.
- **Shots:** about 3–5 seconds each, with a slow camera move inside every shot. Never a still image.
- **Captions:** 1 to 3 words at a time, white with a dark outline, in the lower third. Type the
  numbers ($3.99, $4.00) as **gold** text so the gold accent carries into the caption layer.
- **Full bleed:** 1080 × 1920 from the first frame. No boxes, no bars, no title strip.
- **Portal:** hold black for 0.3 s at the end of shot 5, then start shot 6.
- **Label it:** switch on TikTok's AI-generated-content label when you upload.
