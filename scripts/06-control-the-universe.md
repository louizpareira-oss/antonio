## Our own stones (not Marvel's)
"Thanos", "Infinity Stones" and the gold glove are Marvel/Disney's: in a title or drawn on screen they invite copyright
claims and limited ads, and the channel already had a monetization rejection. So the stones are **ours**: six
hexagonal crystals that snap into a badge on the hoodie, named **TIME** (gold), **SPACE** (cyan), **ENERGY** (orange-red),
**MIND** (violet), **MATTER** (green), **LIFE** (pink). Viewers still get the idea in one second; nobody owns it.

## The rule of the story
- Six stones. Each one controls one thing: time, space, energy, mind, matter, life.
- **Every stone gives you something, and every stone takes something back.** (This is the rule that turns the fun into
  the curse, and the price you pay at the end.)

## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `GRANDMA:`, `THEO:` etc. are character lines; `(thought)` lines are heard
in the hero's head (light echo in the mix).

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can one person really control the universe?
Not in a movie. In real life, with real physics. Let's find out.
Because this morning, at seven fifty-nine, your coffee stopped falling.
It's just hanging there. One inch from the floor.
The clock on the wall isn't ticking. The pigeon outside is stuck in the middle of a flap.
And there's a gold stone in your hand. It's warm. It hums, like a phone on silent.

[ACT 1 — RULES]
Last night, six lights fell into Grandma's backyard.
Six stones. Each one controls one thing. Time. Space. Energy. Mind. Matter. And life.
And you found all six.
Here's the rule. Every stone gives you something. And every stone takes something back.
Remember that. It's going to matter a lot.

[ACT 1 — GRANDMA]
Grandma Rosa was an astronomer for forty years.
By the time you were six, you knew every star she knew.
GRANDMA: That one's Vega. That one's a satellite. That one's... a plane. Never mind.
Lately, she forgets things. Where she put her glasses. They're on her head.
Sometimes, your name.
But she never forgets a star.
GRANDMA: Stars don't twinkle, you know. The air does. Remember that.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
So. Day one. You're late for school. You freeze time.
You walk past the bus, through the frozen traffic, and sit down at your desk.
Time starts again. You're early. First time in your life.
Math test. Question four is wrong. You rewind ten seconds and fix it.
The space stone. You blink, and you're first in the lunch line.
Blink. Ramen in Tokyo. Blink. Back before the bell.
You land with a puff of light and a little pop. Nobody notices. Yet.
The energy stone. Every dead phone in the classroom charges in your hand.
THEO: Bro. Do mine. Do mine!
The mind stone. You can hear the teacher think.
TEACHER (thought): Please. Nobody ask about the homework.
Nobody asks. You don't either.
The matter stone turns broccoli into fries.
The life stone makes the dead plant on the windowsill bloom in two seconds.
And every stone you touch snaps onto your hoodie, right in the middle of your chest.
By Friday, your hoodie has gloves. And boots. And a cape.
You didn't ask for the cape. It just showed up.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But remember the rule. Every stone takes something back.
Time, first. When you freeze time, you don't freeze. You keep living.
Every frozen minute is a minute only you lived.
By Sunday, you've frozen time for nine whole days. You're nine days older than your birthday.
MOM: Why do you look so tired?
Space. Every jump leaves a hole in the air, and the air rushes back in.
Boom. Every teleport is a thunderclap. The school windows crack. The principal blames the pipes.
And once, you blink into the wrong apartment. A family, eating dinner. You blink out. They're still talking about it.
Energy. The heat has to go somewhere.
Your ice cream melts before it reaches your mouth. Your sneakers leave black footprints on the gym floor.
Mind. There's no off switch.
At the football game you hear the whole stadium. Forty thousand thoughts at once.
And one of them is Theo's.
THEO (thought): He's different now. I liked him better before.
Matter. Things don't stay changed.
At midnight, the fries turn back into broccoli.
On Tuesday you fixed a cracked bridge. On Wednesday it starts to turn back. With a bus on it.
You hold it together with your bare hands until the last passenger steps off.
And then it gets bigger.
Six stones in one place bend time around you. Not much. A few billionths of a second.
That's enough. Every GPS within a mile of you is wrong.
Map apps send cars into the river. Planes fly around your town.
NASA has a name for it. The anomaly.
It's you.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Can one person really control the universe? Let's check, stone by stone.
Time. Time really does bend.
The clocks on GPS satellites run thirty-eight millionths of a second faster every day than clocks on the ground.
If engineers didn't correct for it, your map would be off by about ten kilometres by tonight.
Astronaut Scott Kelly spent almost a year in space. He came back a few milliseconds younger than his twin brother.
Space. Teleporting a person? No.
But in 2017, scientists in China teleported the state of a particle of light to a satellite more than a thousand kilometres up.
Not the thing itself. The information. That part is real.
Energy. Einstein's E equals m c squared means mass is energy, packed tight.
Turn one paperclip, one gram, completely into energy, and you'd release more than the bomb that hit Hiroshima.
Mind. In 2024, a man who couldn't move his arms played chess just by thinking.
A chip in his brain read his signals.
Matter. Atoms are almost completely empty space.
Squeeze the empty space out of every person on Earth, and all eight billion of us would fit inside a sugar cube.
Life. There's a jellyfish that can turn itself back into a baby when it's hurt or old. And then grow up again.
Scientists call it the immortal jellyfish.
So every power is real. Just never all of them in one hand.

[ACT 5 — GRANDMA]
One night, Grandma doesn't know who you are.
She looks at the cape, and the glowing stones on your chest, and she steps back.
GRANDMA: Who are you? Where's my grandson?
You've stopped time. You've held up a bridge. And you can't make her remember you.
Except... the life stone.
It fixes what's broken. And it takes something back.
You already know what it will take. A memory, for a memory.
You press the stone into her hand.
She blinks. And she smiles.
GRANDMA: There you are. You got tall.
You get one perfect day. She beats you at cards. Twice.
You take her to the old observatory, and she names every star.
And somewhere around Vega, you notice something.
You can't remember the first night she showed you the stars.
It's gone. That's what the stone took.
So you ask her.
GRANDMA: You were six. You wore your pyjamas under your coat.
GRANDMA: You asked me if the stars could see us back. I said yes.
She can't give the memory back to your head. So she gives it to you in words.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Then she gets very quiet.
GRANDMA: I've seen these stones before. Nineteen sixty-nine. Six lights. Same backyard.
GRANDMA: And something told me: we'll come back for them, when someone is ready to hold them.
That night, NASA's telescopes pick up six new lights.
They're not falling. They're slowing down.
They're not coming back for the stones.
They're coming back for whoever could hold them.
Your coffee stops falling.

[END]
So, if you could keep only one of the six stones, which one would you keep, and what would you do with it?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because they're almost here.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Twinkling:** stars twinkle because of turbulence in Earth's atmosphere, not in the stars themselves.
- **GPS time dilation:** satellite clocks gain about 38 µs a day relative to ground clocks (≈ +45 µs from weaker
  gravity, ≈ −7 µs from orbital speed); uncorrected, position errors grow by roughly 10 km a day.
- **Kelly twins:** Scott Kelly spent 340 days on the ISS (2015–2016); special relativity made him a few milliseconds
  younger than his twin Mark (estimates around 5–9 ms).
- **Quantum teleportation:** 2017, the Micius satellite team (Chinese Academy of Sciences) teleported photon states from
  a ground station in Tibet to the satellite at up to ~1,400 km (*Nature*, 2017). Information, not matter.
- **E = mc²:** 1 g × c² ≈ 9 × 10¹³ J ≈ 21 kilotons of TNT; the Hiroshima bomb was about 15 kilotons.
- **Thinking chess:** in 2024 Noland Arbaugh, paralysed from the shoulders down, played chess with a Neuralink brain
  implant controlling a cursor. Company name only as plain text, if at all.
- **Sugar cube:** atoms are almost entirely empty space; compressed to nuclear density, all humans' mass fits in about
  a sugar-cube volume (a common, order-of-magnitude illustration).
- **Immortal jellyfish:** *Turritopsis dohrnii* can revert from the adult medusa to the polyp stage.
- **Story, not fact:** the stones, freezing time, GPS breaking around a person, the "anomaly", the 1969 lights.
