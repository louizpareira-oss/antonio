# Script 01: What If You Had an Inventory in Real Life?

**Target length:** about 9:30–10:00 at a normal-to-slightly-fast read
**Voiceover:** ~1,380 words. Pauses, sound-effect beats and the end-screen hold bring it to about 9:30
**Scenes:** 45. Each `S##` number matches a prompt in
[`../prompts/01-inventory-in-real-life-visuals.txt`](../prompts/01-inventory-in-real-life-visuals.txt)
**Character:** Sprout, the cream-colored bean in the mustard hoodie (see [`../02-style-guide.md`](../02-style-guide.md)). Sprout is the viewer's stand-in, so the voiceover always says "you".

**The rules, as the video defines them** (keep these consistent, because commenters will check):
1. Touch something and think "take", and it goes into a slot. Think of the slot and it comes back out.
2. 36 slots: a hotbar of 9 plus 27 in storage.
3. Identical items stack, up to 64 per slot.
4. Size doesn't count. A sock and a couch each take one slot.
5. Time stops inside the inventory.
6. Nothing alive, and nothing attached to the ground.
7. Nobody else can see the inventory.

---

## 1. Golden hook (0:00–0:30)

**S01** [Visual: Sprout wakes up in bed, soft morning light through the curtains. A row of nine empty, glowing square slots floats along the bottom of the frame like a game hotbar. Sprout stares at them, eyes huge.]
Voiceover: You wake up, and there's a row of little boxes floating at the bottom of your vision. You blink. Still there.

**S02** [Visual: Sprout pokes their phone on the nightstand. The phone vanishes with a sparkle and reappears as a tiny icon in the first slot of the hotbar.]
Voiceover: You touch your phone and think, "take." It's gone. It's in slot one.

**S03** [Visual: The full inventory grid opens over the bedroom, 9 hotbar slots plus 27 storage slots, all empty except the phone. Big on-screen text: 36 SLOTS.]
Voiceover: Congratulations. You have an inventory. Thirty-six slots. Anything you can touch, you can carry. And a pencil takes up exactly as much space as a couch.

**S04** [Visual: Sprout slowly turns to look at their bed, then looks straight into the camera with a small, mischievous grin.]
Voiceover: You look at your bed. Your bed looks at you. Let's find out how long it takes this to ruin your life.

## 2. The rules (0:30–2:00)

**S05** [Visual: Sprout, now dressed, stands next to a chalkboard titled THE RULES, holding a pointer, wearing tiny safety goggles.]
Voiceover: Okay. Before you put your house in your pocket, you need to know how this works. So you spend the whole morning testing it, like any responsible gamer would.

**S06** [Visual: Sprout touches a coffee mug on the kitchen counter. It vanishes. A small label pops up: "Mug x1". Then Sprout holds out a hand and the mug reappears in it.]
Voiceover: Rule one. To store something, you touch it and think "take." To get it back, you think about the slot, and it appears in your hand. Or, if it's too big for your hand, right in front of you.

**S07** [Visual: Sprout drops pencils into a slot one by one. The counter climbs 1, 2, 3… 64. The sixty-fifth pencil pops into a brand-new slot. Sprout looks personally offended.]
Voiceover: Rule two. Identical things stack, sixty-four per slot. Sixty-four pencils, one slot. Sixty-five pencils? Two slots. Nobody knows why it's sixty-four. It's always sixty-four.

**S08** [Visual: Sprout puts a hand on the living-room couch and it vanishes. The slot shows a tiny couch icon. In the next slot, a single sock. Both are labelled "x1".]
Voiceover: Rule three. Size means nothing. Your couch takes one slot. So does one sock. Honestly, that's the most upsetting part.

**S09** [Visual: Sprout stores a steaming slice of pizza. A wall calendar flips through a month of pages. Sprout pulls the slice back out, still steaming, with a sparkle.]
Voiceover: Rule four. Inside the inventory, time stops. Put in a hot slice of pizza, take it out a month later, and it's still hot. Still crispy. Science has no explanation. You have dinner.

**S10** [Visual: Sprout reaches toward their sleepy orange cat. A big red X pops up with an error buzz. The cat stares at Sprout, deeply unimpressed.]
Voiceover: Rule five. Nothing alive. You find this out by trying to store your cat. The cat does not go in. The cat will remember this.

**S11** [Visual: Sprout in the front yard tries to take a tree: red X. A fire hydrant: red X. Then the neighbor's garden gnome, which vanishes. Sprout freezes, looks around, and quietly puts it back.]
Voiceover: Rule six. Nothing attached to the ground. Trees, no. Fire hydrants, no. Your neighbor's garden gnome? Yes. You put it back. Probably.

**S12** [Visual: Breakfast table. Sprout waves a hand through the glowing hotbar right in front of Mom's face. Mom sees nothing, just her kid flapping a hand at her, and sips her coffee.]
Voiceover: And rule seven, the important one. Nobody else can see your inventory. To everyone else, things just… disappear around you. Which will look totally normal. Nobody will ever notice.

## 3. The fun part (2:00–5:00)

**S13** [Visual: Sprout strolls to school with no backpack, hands in hoodie pocket, whistling. Behind them, other students are hunched over under enormous backpacks.]
Voiceover: And at first? It's incredible. Day one, you walk to school with no backpack. Every book, every binder, your laptop, three water bottles and a backup hoodie, all floating in slots you can't even feel.

**S14** [Visual: Classroom. The teacher announces a pop quiz. Sprout opens a hand and a perfectly sharpened pencil appears. Classmates turn around, and Sprout hands out pencils in every direction.]
Voiceover: Someone needs a pencil? You've got sixty-three. A calculator? You've got four. A charger for literally any phone ever made? Slot twelve. You become the most useful person in every room you walk into. By Wednesday, teachers are asking *you* for things.

**S15** [Visual: School cafeteria. Sprout pulls a whole steaming pizza out of thin air. Friends' jaws drop over their sad, soggy sandwiches.]
Voiceover: Lunch is a different game now. You bought a pizza last Friday, and it is exactly as hot as it was last Friday. Your friends eat cold sandwiches. You eat Friday.

**S16** [Visual: Sprout at home pulls an ice-cream bar out of thin air on a snowy winter evening. It's perfectly frozen, with a tiny summer sun icon floating above it. Their old fridge sits unplugged in the corner with a cobweb on it.]
Voiceover: You stop needing a fridge. Ice cream never melts. Milk never goes bad. You bought a stack of sixty-four ice-cream bars in July, and it's still July in slot seven.

**S17** [Visual: Sprout's mom searching under couch cushions, Dad checking his coat pockets. Sprout calmly holds out keys, earbuds and the TV remote, one in each hand and one balanced on their head.]
Voiceover: You never lose anything again. Keys, slot two. Earbuds, slot three. The TV remote, slot four. Your family stops saying "has anyone seen…" and just looks at you.

**S18** [Visual: Sprout's bedroom. Sprout taps the gaming PC, monitor, desk, gaming chair and mini fridge, and each vanishes with a pop. In seconds the room is completely empty.]
Voiceover: Then you realize what this really means. Your entire gaming setup, PC, monitor, desk, chair, mini fridge, fits in five slots. Your setup isn't in your room anymore. Your setup is wherever you are.

**S19** [Visual: A sunny park. Sprout has set up the full desk, chair, monitor and PC under a big tree and is gaming happily. A row of ducks watches from the pond.]
Voiceover: You game in the park. You game on the beach.

**S20** [Visual: The same setup on a windy mountain peak above the clouds. Sprout is wrapped in a blanket, staring at a spinning loading wheel on the monitor, with a breathtaking sunset behind.]
Voiceover: You game on top of a mountain, which has terrible Wi-Fi, but the view is incredible.

**S21** [Visual: Rain starts in the park. Sprout pulls out a giant patio umbrella, then a couch, then a floor lamp, and sits under it all, cozy and dry, sipping hot cocoa while people run past soaked.]
Voiceover: It starts to rain? You pull out an umbrella. And a couch. And a lamp. You're not outside anymore. You're in your living room. It just happens to be outside.

**S22** [Visual: Moving day. Parents and two movers struggle with heavy boxes. Sprout walks calmly through the house tapping everything, and furniture vanishes room by room.]
Voiceover: Then your family moves house. The movers quote two days and eleven hundred dollars. You walk through the house touching everything. The whole house fits in twenty-two slots.

**S23** [Visual: The empty house. Dad stands in the bare living room, wiping a single tear with a tissue. The mover stares into a completely empty truck.]
Voiceover: Your parents cancel the movers. Your dad cries a little. Nobody talks about it.

**S24** [Visual: Sprout beside a hand-painted sign: ONE KID. NO TRUCK. MOVING. A long line of neighbors waits with furniture. Sprout holds a fan of cash.]
Voiceover: And that's when you realize this isn't just convenient. It's a business. One kid, no truck. You charge half what the real movers charge, and you still make more in a weekend than you used to make all summer.

**S25** [Visual: Airport departure hall. Everyone drags huge suitcases. Sprout strolls past with nothing at all, waving cheerfully at the confused check-in staff.]
Voiceover: Vacations? No suitcase. No checked bags. No overweight fees. You bring your whole wardrobe, your bed, and a fresh pizza for every day of the trip. At the beach, you pull out a beanbag chair, and nobody can figure out how you got it there.

**S26** [Visual: Snowball fight in a snowy backyard. Sprout fires snowballs in a rapid-fire stream from an outstretched hand while friends dive for cover.]
Voiceover: Snowball fights? You come prepared. Sixty-four snowballs, frozen in time, ready to go.

**S27** [Visual: Quick montage on a split screen: Grandma handing Sprout a tray of cookies; Sprout pulling a full-size trampoline out of nowhere at a friend's birthday party; kids cheering.]
Voiceover: Grandma's cookies? A stack of sixty-four, saved for emergencies. Somebody's birthday? You pull a trampoline out of thin air. You're not just a person anymore. You're a walking cheat code.

## 4. The cost (5:00–8:00)

**S28** [Visual: The inventory grid fills the screen, nearly full. Each slot holds one weird item: a chewed pencil, a left sock, a right sock, a rock, a traffic cone, one single french fry. A red INVENTORY FULL banner flashes.]
Voiceover: But here's what every gamer already knows. Inventories fill up. And yours fills up with junk, because "identical" means identical. A chewed pencil doesn't stack with a fresh one. A left sock doesn't stack with a right sock. Thirty-six slots sounded like a lot. Now one of them holds a single french fry, and you can't remember why.

**S29** [Visual: Sprout sits on the bedroom floor at 2 a.m., surrounded by piles of stuff, with dark circles under their eyes. A laptop glows with a giant color-coded spreadsheet.]
Voiceover: So you start managing it. Then you start managing it a lot. You have a spreadsheet. You lose sleep over which slot gets the couch. Inventory management becomes your entire personality.

**S30** [Visual: Cafeteria. Sprout, mid-story, gestures to friends with a big grin. The moment they say it, everything bursts out around them at once: couch, fridge, bed, trampoline, snowballs, pencils, pizza, all flying across the cafeteria.]
Voiceover: And then there's the drop button. Because the drop button isn't a button. It's a thought. And you know what's really hard to control? Your thoughts. One day at lunch you say, "Honestly? I'd drop everything for pizza." Thirty-six slots. All at once. In the cafeteria.

**S31** [Visual: Aftermath, dead silence. The cafeteria is buried in furniture. A single snowball slides slowly off the top of a teacher's head. Every student is staring at Sprout.]
Voiceover: Remember rule seven? Nobody can see your inventory. They can absolutely see a couch, a fridge and sixty-four snowballs appear around you at once.

**S32** [Visual: Dozens of phones held up, all filming. Cut to a phone screen showing a viral video with a giant view counter spinning upward, with Sprout frozen mid-cafeteria-explosion on the thumbnail.]
Voiceover: Everybody had their phone out. By the end of the day, forty million people have watched you summon a refrigerator.

**S33** [Visual: Sprout opens their front door to a crowd of strangers holding things out: a guitar case, a fish tank, a lamp, and at the front, a man in a trench coat holding a locked metal briefcase.]
Voiceover: Now everybody wants something. Your mom wants you to hide the birthday presents. Fine. Your friend wants you to hide his report card from his parents. Less fine. Then his friends want you to hold stuff. Then people you've never met, holding things you didn't ask about.

**S34** [Visual: The man in the trench coat hands Sprout the briefcase, finger to his lips, then turns and disappears into fog. Sprout stands holding it, very small and very confused.]
Voiceover: One guy hands you a locked briefcase and says, "Don't open it." You put it in slot nine. You never see him again. Slot nine is his now. Forever.

**S35** [Visual: Airport security. Sprout walks through the metal detector and nothing beeps. Two security officers exchange a look. One whispers into a radio. In the background, people in dark suits and sunglasses appear.]
Voiceover: And that's when the wrong people start paying attention. Think about it. You can carry anything, anywhere, and no scanner on Earth can find it. To a normal person, that's convenient. To certain people, it's the most valuable thing in the world.

**S36** [Visual: A bright, overly friendly office. Sprout sits across a table from two smiling agents in suits. On the table, a thick contract with a pen and a plate of cookies.]
Voiceover: The government wants to hire you. So do some people who are very much not the government. Everybody is extremely polite about it. And everybody asks you the exact same question.

**S37** [Visual: A chalkboard doodle: a little stick-figure game character falls over, and their items spill out around them in a circle. A big red question mark.]
Voiceover: In every game, when your character dies, they drop their inventory. Everything spills out, right where they fell. Does that work for you too? You don't know. And you'd really prefer not to find out.

**S38** [Visual: Outside the school gates, a man in a shiny suit leans against a fancy car, its open trunk stacked with gold bars. He grins and waves at Sprout, a little too friendly. Sprout's eyes go wide.]
Voiceover: The man who pays you to carry his gold bars would also like to know. He brings it up a lot. He's a little too curious about it.

**S39** [Visual: Night. Sprout lies in bed with the curtains shut tight, staring at the ceiling. The inventory grid glows faintly over the dark room.]
Voiceover: So now you don't go out much. Your friends stop asking how you are, and start asking what you're holding. You can carry anything in the world. Except, it turns out, a normal life.

## 5. The choice (8:00–10:00)

**S40** [Visual: Sprout sits on the edge of the bed. A floating menu appears with two glowing buttons: KEEP INVENTORY and DELETE FOREVER. A game cursor hovers between them.]
Voiceover: One night, a new option shows up in your menu. Two buttons. "Keep inventory." Or "delete everything, forever." No more slots. No more Friday pizza. No more one-kid moving company. But also, no more briefcase. No more polite people with contracts. No more strangers following you through airports.

**S41** [Visual: Split montage. Left side, in warm golden light: gaming in the park, the moving business, the pizza in the cafeteria, friends laughing. Right side, in cold blue light: the briefcase, the agents, the man with the gold bars.]
Voiceover: You think about the park. The mountain. Your parents' faces when the whole house fit in twenty-two slots. Then you think about slot nine. And the man with the gold bars. And the question nobody wants to answer.

**S42** [Visual: Close-up on Sprout's face, lit by the two glowing buttons, eyes darting left and right. The cursor drifts toward KEEP, then toward DELETE, then back again.]
Voiceover: Any gamer will tell you: you never, ever delete your save file. But any gamer will also tell you that when a game starts getting this weird, it's because something is about to happen. Your cursor drifts toward "keep." Then "delete." Then back.

**S43** [Visual: The cursor drifts toward a button, then stops. In the grid, slot 36, the last one, the one Sprout never uses, is glowing. Close-up: inside it, a small folded note.]
Voiceover: You've almost decided when you notice something. Slot thirty-six. The last one. The one you've never used. It isn't empty anymore. There's a note in it. You didn't put it there. Nobody else can even see your inventory. You take it out, and it's in your handwriting.

**S44** [Visual: The note fills the screen in Sprout's handwriting: "Don't press either button. Trust me. — You". Sprout's eyes go enormous. Hard cut to black.]
Voiceover: It says, "Don't press either button. Trust me." And it's signed… by you.

**S45** [Visual: Black screen. Sprout fades in, small and centered, the hotbar glowing beneath them, looking directly at the camera. On-screen text: WHAT'S IN YOUR SLOT 1?]
Voiceover: So. Would you keep the inventory, or delete it? And what's the first thing you'd put in slot one? Tell me in the comments. I read all of them. And whatever you do… don't press either button.

## Editing notes

- **Change the visual every 4–6 seconds.** For a 10-minute video that's roughly 100–120 shots,
  so each `S##` needs 2–3 shots: a wide, a close-up reaction, and an insert (the item, the
  slot, the UI). Animate each image into a 3–5 second clip with image-to-video.
- **Build the inventory UI in the editor, not the image generator.** Generators garble small
  grids and text. Make one hotbar/grid graphic, reuse it in every scene, and pop item icons in
  on the "take" beats with a short sound effect. Using the same UI every time is what makes
  this look like a series.
- **Running gag counters.** Keep a small "x64" counter on screen during the pencil, snowball and
  cookie beats. Gamers love seeing a number change.
- **Sound effects to source:** item pickup "pop", error buzz (S10, S11), inventory-full alarm
  (S28), the cafeteria explosion (S30), and a long silence after it (S31). The silence is the
  joke, so don't put music under it.
- **The toy moment** (S30): as everything bursts out in the cafeteria, pull the camera back
  to reveal the whole school is a diorama on a table, then snap back in for S31.
- **Color arc:** sherbet daylight up to S27, cooling toward teal from S28, deep teal night
  from S39. Grade the clips to match if the generator drifts.
- **The end screen** (S45) holds for 5 seconds. Put the next video's end-screen card there.
