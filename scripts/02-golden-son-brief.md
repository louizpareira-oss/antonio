# "Golden Son" — Production Brief

**Project code:** GS-02 · **Format:** Stylized 3D animated short
**Runtime:** 70s (1:10) · **Delivery:** 1080 × 1920, 9:16, full bleed, 30 fps
**Genre:** Deadpan absurdist satire · **Production method:** AI-generated clips, assembled

This is the refined, production-grade version of the original concept brief. It is written
to be handed to a generator, an editor and a voice performer without further explanation.
The companion files are the beat script ([`02-golden-son.md`](02-golden-son.md)) and the
copy-paste clip pack ([`../prompts/02-golden-son-3d.txt`](../prompts/02-golden-son-3d.txt)).

---

## 1. Logline

A father pours molten gold on one son and mud on the other before either of them can fly,
then spends eighteen years refusing to notice he chose wrong.

## 2. Thematic statement

The film is not about wealth. It is about **pre-assignment** — the way a verdict gets
delivered first and the evidence gets collected afterwards. Every craft decision below
serves that one idea:

- The **pour** is the verdict. It happens in the first five seconds, before a single line
  of character dialogue.
- The **palette split** is the sentence being served — two children living inside two
  different colour grades they did not choose.
- The **reversed pour** in Scene 10 is the appeal, and it is silent. Nobody apologises;
  the father simply gets poured on, and the film ends on an empty centre frame.

Comedy register: nothing in the film knows it is funny. Play every moment absolutely
straight. The instant a character winks at the audience, the satire collapses into a
cartoon.

## 3. Visual design

### 3.1 Texture and materiality

Highly stylized 3D character animation with feature-grade surfacing — Pixar-adjacent
polish, but with edgy urban street-style character design rather than soft family-film
shapes. Materiality is the primary storytelling tool and must be visibly different per
character:

| Surface | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Groomed feathers** (gold birds) | Soft, individually separated barbs; clean grooming; subtle anisotropic sheen; slight sub-surface translucency at the feather edges when backlit |
| **Matted feathers** (Poopy Son) | Clumped and spiked, visible dried residue, one permanently bent tail feather, dust caught in the down, zero sheen |
| **Gold jewellery and helmets** | High-spec metal — roughness 0.15–0.25, strong anisotropic streaking, visible environment reflection including the window frames |
| **Molten gold (liquid)** | Emissive amber core, heavy viscosity, slow surface skin forming as it cools, sharp specular highlights on the flow |
| **Mud (liquid)** | Matte, near-zero specular, thick and lazy, strings and ropes as it falls, dead sound |

The two liquids must read as the **same physical simulation with opposite surfacing**.
That equivalence is the joke: the father is performing an identical act twice and calling
one of them love.

### 3.2 Lighting

Cinematic, high-end commercial quality throughout. Soft natural sunlight through
floor-to-ceiling windows, motivated and directional. Warm golden-hour rim light along the
top edge of every character, chosen specifically to catch the metallic surfaces of helmets
and chains.

| Environment | Key | Colour temp | Contrast | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The pour room (Sc. 1, 10) | Large window, camera left | 5200K, warm bounce | Medium, clean | Architectural, even, no drama |
| The patio (Sc. 2) | Direct sun, high | 5600K | Low, lifted shadows | Aspirational lifestyle-ad lighting |
| The car (Sc. 3) | Moving daylight | 5600K, shifting | Medium-high | Light must animate across faces |
| Poopy Poop High (Sc. 4, 6) | Overcast top light | 6500K, flat | Low, muddy blacks | Grounded, drab, real |
| Golden High (Sc. 5, 7) | Over-lit practicals + sun | 4800K | Low | Deliberately excessive; flare permitted |
| The dining room (Sc. 8) | Low golden hour, raking | 3800K | High | Long shadows down the table |
| Graduation (Sc. 9) | Flat overcast | 6000K | Very low | The only neutral frame in the film |

### 3.3 Colour

A hard two-palette system, never blended except where noted:

- **Elite palette** — warm gold, amber, champagne, cream marble. Saturation high in the
  golds, everything else desaturated so the gold dominates.
- **Poopy palette** — drab desaturated browns, wet grey, olive, cracked concrete. No
  saturated colour anywhere in frame.

**The one exception:** Scene 9 (graduation) puts both palettes in a single frame under
neutral light. It is the only moment the film stops sorting people, and it is why that
scene is shot flat and symmetrical. Scene 10 then swaps which character carries which
palette. Do not let the palettes mix anywhere else — the discipline is what makes the
swap legible.

## 4. Cinematography

**Governing principle:** frame this like a high-end corporate or lifestyle commercial —
balanced, symmetrical, confidently static — and let grotesque content sit inside polite
composition. The tension between the two is the entire comic engine.

- **Lens package:** 35mm for wides and full-body two-shots; 50mm for coverage and
  expression close-ups. Nothing wider than 35mm — wide-angle distortion reads as cartoon
  and undercuts the commercial-polish gag.
- **Depth of field:** deep in wides (f/5.6 equivalent) so the environment does the
  bragging; shallow on the close-ups (f/2.0 equivalent) so a single expression carries
  the beat.
- **Camera height:** chest height on the birds, level, for every scene except Scene 7
  (slight low angle on Golden High's lecture theatre, to let the room look more important
  than it is).
- **Movement:** static is the default. Only three moves exist in the whole film — the
  subtle follow-pan in Scene 2, and the two slow push-ins in Scenes 6 and 7. Because
  movement is rationed, each push reads as the film leaning in.
- **Cutting:** cut on dialogue, never on action. Every cut lands in a silence.

**Symmetry rule:** Scenes 1, 5, 8, 9 and 10 are centre-framed and symmetrical to the
pixel. Scenes 4 and 6 are deliberately off-balance — the poopy world cannot hold a
composition together. The audience will not name this, but they will feel which world is
which before anyone speaks.

## 5. Character sheet

Consistency across sixteen separately generated clips is the single largest technical risk
in the production. Paste these descriptions verbatim into every prompt, and lock the seed.

### POP-CUH (the leader)
Adult pigeon. Slate-grey plumage, immaculately groomed. Gold motorcycle-style helmet worn
at all times, heavy gold chains, gold rings on both feet, gold tracksuit. Posture upright
and still; he does not fidget. **Performance:** never raises his voice, never hurries,
never explains. His warmth is rationed to exactly one line in the film.

### GOLDEN SON
Young pigeon. Gleaming iridescent gold-dipped plumage, small matching gold helmet, gold
chain. Vacant, contented expression; slow blink. **Performance:** total unearned
confidence, no interiority. Four words of dialogue in the entire film, all of them wrong.

### POOPY SON (the dirty one)
Young pigeon. Matted brown plumage, clumped and distressed, one permanently bent tail
feather, a plastic bottle cap worn as a helmet. **Bright, alert eyes — the sharpest thing
in any frame he is in.** **Performance:** hopeful and squeaky in the first half; from
Scene 6 onward his voice drops half an octave and quietens. That drop is the whole arc.
It should be invisible on the first watch and obvious on the second.

### NARRATOR
Never seen. Deep, gravelly, cynical, bored of being right. Two lines only — Scene 1 and
Scene 9 — so both must land.

**Continuity notes:** the bottle-cap helmet, the bent tail feather and the gold rings are
the three continuity anchors. If a generated take loses one of them, discard the take
rather than fixing it in post.

## 6. Sequence breakdown

Scenes 1–3 are the original concept, refined. Scenes 4–10 extend it to a monetizable
runtime (see §9). Timecodes are targets, not locks — the voiceover is cut first and the
picture follows it.

| Sc. | TC | Location | Shot | Story function |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00–0:05 | The pour room | Symmetrical medium-long, static, 35mm | The verdict |
| 2 | 0:05–0:09 | Mansion patio | Wide, subtle follow-pan, 35mm | The favouritism stated aloud |
| 3 | 0:09–0:15 | Car interior | Tight three-shot, static, 50mm | The sorting made institutional |
| 4 | 0:15–0:22 | Poopy Poop High | Exterior wide, static, off-balance, 35mm | Delivery without ceremony |
| 5 | 0:22–0:29 | Golden High | Exterior wide, static, symmetrical, 35mm | Ceremony without merit |
| 6 | 0:29–0:37 | Poopy classroom | Slow push-in, 35mm → 50mm | **The turn** — the wrong bird got the gold |
| 7 | 0:37–0:44 | Golden lecture hall | Static wide → slow push, 35mm | The turn confirmed |
| 8 | 0:44–0:53 | Dining room | Symmetrical wide + 50mm close | Evidence presented and refused |
| 9 | 0:53–1:02 | Graduation | Flat symmetrical two-shot, 35mm | The outcome, stated neutrally |
| 10 | 1:02–1:10 | The pour room | **Identical framing to Sc. 1** | The verdict returned |

### The matched pair
Scenes 1 and 10 must share camera height, focal length, framing, window positions and dust
behaviour. Generate them consecutively, on the same seed, and change only who is standing
in the centre and where each liquid lands. Scene 10 then holds two full seconds on an
**empty centre frame** under silence before the final mud drip.

This is the same technique as the doorway pair in Script 01 (beats 3 and 20) and it works
for the same reason: an identical frame, returned to with new information, does the
emotional work that a line of dialogue cannot. **It is the single most important shot in
the film. Do not cut it for length.**

## 7. Dialogue and voice direction

Full dialogue lives in [`02-golden-son.md`](02-golden-son.md). Direction for the booth:

- Record each character in a **separate continuous pass**, start to finish. Do not switch
  voices line by line — pitch drifts and the performance flattens.
- **"Cuh" is a rhythm instrument, not a punchline.** It ends most lines. Say it flat, at
  the same pitch every time, and never lean on it. The one line in the film without it —
  Pop-cuh's "Have a golden day, my golden son" — is his only moment of warmth, and it
  registers precisely because the tic is missing.
- **"Hell nah, cuh"** appears twice (Scenes 3 and 8). Deliver both at identical pitch,
  pace and volume. The repetition is the joke; a varied second read kills it.
- Leave **0.4s of air** after every line. The film's comedy is in the pauses, and an
  editor cannot create space that was not recorded.
- Peak dialogue at −6 dBFS, no compression at the recording stage.

## 8. Sound design

**Music bed:** slow lo-fi hip-hop, heavy bassline, 72–78 BPM, no vocal, no melody hook
that competes with dialogue. One continuous bed across the film with three mix states:

| State | Scenes | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Full bed | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 | Beat and bass, sitting at −18 LUFS under dialogue |
| Bass only | 6, 7 | Drums drop out; the film gets quieter as it gets smarter |
| **Full silence** | 8 (the report cards) | Bed muted entirely for 2 seconds; only a ticking clock |
| Single piano note | 9 | One repeated note, no percussion |
| Resolve, then silence | 10 | Bed resolves on the downbeat as he exits, then nothing |

**Spot effects:**
- The pour — metallic ring and shimmer for gold; a low, wet squelch for mud. Same duration,
  opposite character.
- Scene 3 — muffled engine hum, tyres on pavement, a turn indicator.
- Scene 4 — one wet splash, one distant sad coo.
- Scene 6 — ceiling drip into a bucket, timed to the bassline. Chalk on board.
- Scene 7 — one triumphant horn stab that does not belong in the scene.
- Scene 8 — clock tick under the silence. Nothing else.
- Scene 10 — a single mud drip as the last sound in the film.

**Mix target:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak −1 dBTP. Dialogue always clearly above the
bed; assume the first viewing is on a phone speaker in a noisy room.

## 9. Delivery and compliance

All of this is enforced by [`../docs/03-technical-spec.md`](../docs/03-technical-spec.md);
repeated here because it is the part that decides whether the video earns.

- **1080 × 1920, 9:16, full bleed.** No bars, no framing, no masking, no letterbox. A
  horizontal source cropped to fit is the exact failure mode that triggered the original
  "low quality content" rejection.
- **Over 60 seconds.** Creator Rewards pays only above the threshold. Target 70 so that a
  trim never drops the file under. If the assembly lands short, **extend the Scene 10
  empty-frame hold** — never cut a scene.
- **AI-generated content label ON at upload.** Non-negotiable; every frame is generated.
- **No watermark**, CapCut end-card off, highest available bitrate, H.264.
- **Captions** in the safe zone: 12% clear top, 20% bottom, 20% right. Narrator lines
  captioned in a distinct position and size from character dialogue, so a muted viewer can
  tell who is speaking.

**On the 15-second version:** the original three scenes are a complete comic unit and will
work as a short — but a 15-second upload earns nothing, which is the entire reason this
repo exists. Post the 70 as the monetized upload; if you want the short, post it the same
day as a trailer and treat it as promotion, not income. Do not pad to 61 seconds with
holds — reviewers read that instantly.

## 10. Risk register

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Character drift between generated clips | High — reads as cheap, invites the quality flag again | Verbatim character blocks, locked seed, discard takes that lose the bottle cap / bent feather / gold rings |
| Scene 1 and 10 fail to match | High — the payoff stops working | Generate the pair back to back, same seed, before anything else |
| Generator returns 16:9 | Fatal for monetization | Reject the take. Never crop to fit |
| Performance drifts cartoonish | Medium — satire becomes a gag reel | Every line flat; no mugging; no character acknowledges the joke |
| Comes in under 60s | Fatal for earnings | Extend the Scene 10 hold, not the dialogue |
| Palettes bleed together | Medium — the swap in Sc. 10 loses meaning | Grade the two worlds separately and only combine in Sc. 9 |
