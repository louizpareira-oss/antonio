# If Your Dog Ever Brings You Its Leash At 3 AM... DO NOT GO OUTSIDE

Original script for a 16:9 YouTube analog-horror "survival protocol" video, written in the
format of the Final Sight / Bradikinin rule videos (cold open → 7 rules → twist → sting) but
with its own premise, rules and ending. Narrated with ElevenLabs, animated with the
horror studio skills in `.claude/skills/horror-*`.

- Notice label on screen: `HOUSEHOLD SAFETY NOTICE 019 // NIGHT WALK`
- Look: white pictograms on black, rule cards with a 1–7 tracker, red X / green check,
  dashed sightlines, HUD + timecode, scan lines and tracking glitches.

## Narration

If your dog ever brings you its leash at three in the morning, do not go outside. Do not open the door. And whatever you do, do not say "okay."

There are seven rules. Follow them in order, and by rule seven, the sun will be up.

Your dog does not need to go out. It went at eleven. You were there. If it is standing beside your bed with the leash in its mouth, it heard something. Something outside has been whistling. Two notes. Low, then high. The way you call your dog in from the yard.

Rule one. Do not take the leash.
Taking the leash is a yes. Saying "okay" is a yes. Tonight, you are not saying yes to anything. Say "not now," and wait. Your dog will drop the leash on the floor, walk to the front door, sit down, and face it. Let it. It knows more than you do.

Rule two. Do not turn on the porch light.
Right now, the house is dark and the yard is dark, and that is fair. The moment you turn on a light, you become the only thing out there that can be seen. If the light is already on, leave it. If it turns on by itself, sit down where you are.

Rule three. Do not look through the peephole.
This is the one people break. You will tell yourself you are only checking. We have one report from a man who checked. The yard was empty. The street was empty. But at the far end of the yard, a leash was tied to the fence, pulled tight, as if something on the other end was waiting for its walk. His dog's leash was still on the bedroom floor.

Rule four. Sit with your dog.
Sit on the floor beside it, with your back to the door. Hold its collar, not its body. If it whines, let it. Whining means it is still talking to you. If it goes completely quiet, it is listening to something else. Hold the collar tighter.

Rule five. If you hear the whistle, cover your dog's ears.
Not yours. Dogs hear it from much farther away. If you can hear it too, it is close. Two notes. Low, then high. If it sounds exactly like you, that is because it has been practicing. Every evening, for years, it has listened to you call your dog inside. You have never once done it differently.

Rule six. If the neighborhood dogs start barking, listen.
Dogs pass a warning down the street, house by house, as something walks past. If the barking is moving toward you, turn off everything that hums. The fan. The fridge. The TV. If the dog next door stops in the middle of a bark... it is in your yard. Do not move. Your dog will not bark. It knows better.

Rule seven. At sunrise, take the walk.
When the sky turns gray, your dog will bring you the leash again. This time, say okay. Let it lead. Do not pull it away from the places it always stops. The fence post. The mailbox. The corner by the streetlight. That is not sniffing. It is checking. Every walk you have ever taken together was a patrol. Your dog has been walking a line around your home since the day you brought it there. Last night, something found a gap in it. Your dog came to ask for your help, and you said no. That was correct. In the dark, a gap goes both ways.

So if your dog ever brings you its leash at three in the morning, stay inside. It is not asking to go out. It is telling you that something wants in.

One last thing. If anything ever brings you a leash at three in the morning, and you do not own a dog... do not open your eyes. It already knows you are awake.
