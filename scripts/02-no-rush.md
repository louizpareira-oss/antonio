# Script 02 — "No Rush"

**Target length:** 78 seconds (75.5s of voice + 2.5s silent hold)
**Format:** 1080 × 1920, full bleed, no bars
**Beats:** 22 · **Average shot:** 3.4s
**Register:** voiceover monologue with one spoken line. Character: human body, tortoise head.
**From:** idea 09 in [`ideas/00-idea-bank.md`](../ideas/00-idea-bank.md)

---

## The idea underneath

> Nobody has ever been unkind to me. They have been **patient** — and patience is how
> people tell you that you cost them something, without ever having to say it. The kindness
> is real. That is what makes it unanswerable.

**Why it is arguable:** most people would call patience a virtue, full stop. This says it is
a transaction with a visible meter, and that being on the receiving end of it every day is
its own slow humiliation.

The controlling image is **the meter** — patience as something spent, watched, and itemised.
Held doors, kettles, running numbers, receipts, stairs counted.

**The return:** the held door in beat 1 comes back as beat 22 — same frame, but nobody is
holding it. It just swings.

## The beak problem, again

A hooked tortoise mouth lip-syncs no better than a beak. Same solution as Script 01,
applied at the writing stage: **voiceover-led**, Edwin never speaks on camera, the one
spoken line belongs to a **human** colleague whose mouth animates normally, and his half of
the exchange is a reaction shot — beat 21 is his eye.

---

## The voiceover script

Flat, precise, faintly formal. A man who has thought about this for years and is not
asking for anything.

```
He's been holding that door for nine seconds.

He'll hold it four more. Then he'll say "no rush."

Everyone says no rush.

My name is Edwin. I'm forty-one. I work in archives.

I'm good at it. Nobody has ever said otherwise.

What I wanted was to arrive somewhere without being watched arriving.

At work, they moved my desk nearer the door.

Nobody asked me. It was a kindness.

They put the kettle on when they saw me stand up.

They finished my sentences. Gently. Always correctly.

A woman on the bus said "take your time, love." The bus was not moving.

Patience is a thing people spend.

And they let you watch the meter run.

Every "no rush" is a receipt.

So I started coming in at six. Before anyone could watch me arrive.

I ate breakfast in the stairwell for two years. It worked.

In March they fitted a new door. It doesn't open before seven.

So I arrive at nine now. Like everybody.

Forty-one steps. Eleven people behind me. Nobody says anything at all.
```

**Beat 20 — spoken, on camera, by a young colleague:**

```
COLLEAGUE: Same time tomorrow?
    (action: falls into step beside him on the stairs, matches his pace,
     does not slow down for him — arrives with him)
```

**Beats 21–22 — voiceover:**

```
Nobody had ever asked to be slow with me.

(silence — hold)
```

**Matched pair: beats 1 and 22.** Same framing, same lens, same distance. In beat 1 someone
performs patience; in beat 22 the door is simply a door. Generate them together, same seed.

**The turn that carries the video is beat 20.** She does not slow down *for* him — she
arrives *with* him. That distinction is the whole payoff, and it has to be visible in the
shot before the line is spoken.
