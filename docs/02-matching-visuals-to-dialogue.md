# 2. Matching a picture to a line

You have a beat sheet where every line has a job. This is how each job becomes a picture.

The skill is the same whether you generate, film or search — but once you **generate**, you
stop searching and start **specifying**. You never fail to find the shot, because you make
it. What you still need is knowing **what shot the line needs**.

---

## The core rule

> **Do not illustrate the words. Show the feeling underneath them.**

The script says a thing. The picture must not repeat that thing — it must make you *feel*
it. If the line says "peace," do not show a peace sign. Show stillness: an empty room, a
flat sea, a person breathing.

Repeating the word is what beginners do, and it always looks cheap.

---

## The 3-question method

For every beat, answer all three in order. Never open a generator before question 3.

### Question 1 — What is the emotion?
Not the topic. The **feeling in the chest**. One word.

> Line: *"What I wanted was one conversation that didn't end in crumbs."*
> Topic: bread. **Emotion: tiredness. Wanting to be ordinary.**

### Question 2 — What does a body do when it feels that?
Emotions are invisible. Bodies are visible. This is the step everyone misses.

> Wanting to be ordinary → **sits among people, does nothing to be noticed, waits**

### Question 3 — What single picture shows that body doing that?

> **An open-plan office. He sits among colleagues. Nobody is looking at him.**

That is a shot you can specify exactly. You went from an abstract idea to a concrete
physical picture — the entire craft.

---

## The translation table

Keep this open while you write. Left is what your script says; right is what you make.

| Script says (abstract) | Show this (concrete) |
|---|---|
| Loneliness | One figure, wide shot, huge empty space around them |
| Feeling behind in life | Watching a train leave. Everyone walking fast past someone standing still |
| Avoiding something | Turning away from frame. A hand closing a door. Eyes down |
| Inner conflict | One person alone in a storm. Standing in heavy rain, not moving |
| Regret | Looking back over a shoulder while walking forward |
| A decision | A doorway. A fork in a road. A hand on a handle, not turning it |
| Discipline | Hands doing one small repeated thing — laces, chalk, writing, pouring |
| Being ignored | A face turned away in the foreground, subject blurred behind |
| Humiliation | An object placed deliberately where it does not belong |
| Freedom / release | Horizon. Open water. Sky filling the frame. Wind |
| Exhaustion | Sitting on the floor. Back against a wall. Lights off |
| Peace | Stillness. Flat water. An empty made bed. Nothing moving |
| Chaos | Crowd, movement, bright colour, fast cut, noise |
| Growth | Something small in a big frame — a plant, a figure climbing |
| Truth / confrontation | Direct eye contact. A mirror. A face lit from one side |
| Recognition | One person using another's name. Two figures, no gesture |
| Time passing | A window with light changing. A clock out of focus. An empty street at dawn |

If a line fits no row, run the 3-question method and add your own. Build this up over
months — it becomes your style.

## Worked example

From [`scripts/01-the-feeding.md`](../scripts/01-the-feeding.md), beats 7 to 12:

| # | Beat | Emotion | The shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | "someone left a slice of white bread on my keyboard" | Small humiliation | Top-down. A slice laid neatly on a keyboard |
| 8 | "I laughed. That was the mistake." | Self-betrayal | **Same man**, from behind, shoulders moving. We never see the face |
| 9 | "once you laugh, it's a bit" | Locked in | The same desk later: three slices, arranged. Empty chairs around |
| 10 | "a date put a basket of breadsticks in front of me" | Public | Across a table. Her hand pushing it forward. Her face kind |
| 11 | "a woman pulled her son back by the hood" | Sharper | A small hand reaching into frame, yanked out of it |
| 12 | "not one of them was cruel" | The claim | Static. Him alone in the office at dusk, monitors off |

Three things to notice:

- **Not one shot is "bread being thrown."** The literal picture would be wrong every time.
- **Beats 7→9 reuse one location and one prop.** That is what makes it feel like a film
  instead of a slideshow. Cheap edits change everything each cut; good ones follow one thing.
- **The energy has a shape.** Small → turn → tightening → public → sharp → still. Beat 11
  is sharp *so that* beat 12 lands quiet.

## Turning a shot into a prompt

Once the shot is in words, the prompt writes itself. For a shot with **no recurring
character** — a doorway, hands, rain:

```
[subject] + [action] + [environment] + [light] + [mood] + [camera] + 9:16 vertical
```

> *"A slice of white bread laid neatly on an office keyboard, seen from directly above,
> flat fluorescent light, quietly humiliating, static close-up, 9:16 vertical"*

When a **recurring character** is on screen, use the fuller formula in
[`03-character-consistency.md`](03-character-consistency.md) instead — it carries the
master block and a consistency instruction, which this one does not.

Generate 3 takes of each, keep the best. If you are filming instead, that same sentence is
your shot list. Read it out and go shoot it.

---

## The 8 folders

You do not need infinite variety. Nearly every video in this register is built from eight
emotional categories. Build a library and you can cut in 20 minutes without searching:

```
01-alone/        lone figure, wide, empty space
02-turning-away/ backs, doors, eyes down, leaving
03-storm/        rain, wind, dark water, heavy weather
04-crowd/        people passing, movement, city noise
05-hands/        small actions, close-up, effort
06-threshold/    doorways, corridors, roads, windows, benches
07-open/         sky, sea, horizon, sunrise
08-face/         eye contact, mirrors, one-side light
```

15–20 clips in each = 120–160 clips = you are never stuck again. One afternoon fills it.

---

## Timing rules

- **One picture per visual idea.** Not per sentence — see doc 01. If the picture does not
  change, hold the shot.
- **2–5 seconds per shot.** Under 2 feels frantic. Over 5 feels dead — unless it is the
  final beat, where a long hold is powerful.
- **Cut on the breath, not on the beat.** Change the picture in the silence between
  sentences, never while a word is being said.
- **Land the last shot early.** Bring the final image in about 1 second *before* the last
  line, and hold it 2 seconds *after* the voice stops. That silence is where people decide
  to rewatch.

---

## Two advanced moves

**Counterpoint.** Sometimes the strongest choice opposes the words. Line: *"I was fine."*
Shot: someone sitting alone on a bathroom floor. The gap between what is said and what is
seen creates the emotion. Twice per video at most — more and it gets confusing.

**The return.** Show an image early, bring it back changed at the end. The same bench, now
shared. The same street, now at dawn. Viewers feel the resolution without being told, and
they rewatch to catch it. This one technique will do more for retention than any filter.
