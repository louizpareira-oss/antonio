## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `GRANDPA:` is Grandpa Eli; `THEO:` is the best friend.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can you really live forever?
Not in a movie. With real science. Let's find out.
It's your sixteenth birthday. The cake knife slips. Your thumb is bleeding.
And then, while you watch, the cut closes. One second. Gone.
Not even a scar.
That night, a doctor runs one test. Then she runs it again.
Your cells have stopped getting older. All of them.
Here's the rule. You stop. The world doesn't.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — GRANDPA]
Grandpa Eli fixed clocks for fifty years. Every clock in town has his fingerprints inside.
Except one. A silver pocket watch, stopped at four twelve, the night Grandma June left.
GRANDPA: Some clocks you don't fix, kiddo. You just keep them.
His own heart runs on a little machine now. It ticks louder than his watches.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Week one. You fall off your skateboard, face first. You get up smiling.
No zits. No sore legs after gym. Never tired.
Football game. Broken arm in the first quarter. Healed by halftime. You score the winner.
You eat three pizzas in one night. Nothing happens.
And every birthday, the candles go up.
You don't.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But you stop. The world doesn't.
Five years later, Theo has a beard, a job and a car. You still get asked where your parents are.
THEO: Dude. You look exactly the same. It's getting weird.
Then the World Health Organization finds out about your blood.
Scientists copy it into one pill. They call it the Pause.
Two billion people take it. Nobody gets older. Hospitals empty out.
Then they fill up again.
Because in the lab, the paused cells won't stop dividing. They grow, and grow, and grow.
The Pause is banned in forty countries.
Your face is on every screen. Patient zero.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
Your cells really do have a limit. Most human cells can only divide about fifty times. Then they stop.
The countdown sits on the tips of your chromosomes. They're called telomeres. Every division, they get a little shorter, like the plastic tip of a shoelace wearing down.
But some cells cheat. They switch on an enzyme called telomerase, and they divide forever. We call them cancer.
Some animals cheat too. A Greenland shark may live around four hundred years. A tiny pond animal called the hydra barely seems to age at all.
In 2016, scientists at the Salk Institute partly reset the cells of mice that age too fast. They lived about thirty percent longer.
And the oldest person on record lived to a hundred and twenty-two.
Living longer? Real. Living forever? Not yet.

[ACT 5 — GRANDPA]
One night, Grandpa's machine starts skipping.
You run to the hospital with a needle in your pocket. One drop of your blood could keep him ticking.
GRANDPA: Put that away, kiddo. Some clocks you don't fix.
You hold his hand. His is old and spotted. Yours looks exactly like the day you turned sixteen.
That's the price. You will hold every hand you love. And you will watch every one of them let go.
He presses the silver watch into your palm.
GRANDPA: Open it. She'd want you to.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Inside, there's a photo of Grandma June. About twenty years old. Dated nineteen twelve.
Under it, another one. Her wedding day. Nineteen sixty-five. Same face. Not one day older.
And a third photo, taken last week. Outside your school.
On the back, in fresh ink: You stopped too. Come find me.
The watch starts ticking.

[END]
If you could stop getting older today, would you do it?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because Grandma June is still out there.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Hayflick limit:** Leonard Hayflick (1961) showed normal human cells in culture divide a limited number of times,
  roughly 40–60, before stopping (senescence).
- **Telomeres:** protective repeats at chromosome ends that shorten with each division; the shoelace-aglet comparison
  is a common teaching analogy.
- **Telomerase and cancer:** most cancers (about 85–90 %) reactivate telomerase, letting their cells keep dividing.
- **Greenland shark:** radiocarbon dating of eye-lens tissue (*Science*, 2016) estimated the oldest shark at about
  392 ± 120 years, at least 272; "may live around four hundred years" stays within that range.
- **Hydra:** studies (e.g. Martínez, 1998) found no measurable rise in death rate with age in lab hydra.
- **Salk Institute, 2016:** Izpisua Belmonte's lab (Ocampo et al., *Cell*, 2016) used brief, cyclic expression of the
  Yamanaka factors in progeria (fast-ageing) mice; their lifespan rose by about 30 %.
- **Oldest person on record:** Jeanne Calment, 122 years and 164 days (1875–1997), the longest verified human lifespan.
- **World Health Organization / Salk Institute:** plain text names only.
- **Story, not fact:** the healing, the Pause pill, two billion users, the ban, Grandma June and the photos, the
  inherited stop.
