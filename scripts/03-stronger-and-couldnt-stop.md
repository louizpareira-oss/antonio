## The voiceover script

```
Day six. You pick up your phone, and it folds in half.
Day seven. You high-five your best friend, and he lands in a hedge.
Day eight. You sneeze, and every window in the kitchen cracks.
And tomorrow, you'll be twice as strong as you are today.

What if you got stronger... and couldn't stop?

It starts on a Monday. You wake up twice as strong as you were yesterday.
On Tuesday, twice that again. Every single morning, your strength doubles.
That doesn't sound scary until you do the math.
Fold a sheet of paper in half forty-two times, and it would reach the Moon. That's what doubling does.
Day five, you're thirty-two times stronger. Day ten, a thousand. Day twenty, a million.
And it's not just your muscles. Your bones, your tendons, your skin, they all double too.
Everything about you gets stronger.
Everything around you stays the same.

At first, it's the best week of your life.
Day one, you carry every grocery bag in one trip.
Day two, you open the pickle jar your dad gave up on.
Day three, you move the couch with one hand, and your mom finds the remote she lost two years ago.
Day four, you do one push-up... and you hit the ceiling.

Then it stops being fun.
Day five, pencils snap in your fingers. You write your history test with a crayon.
Day six, your phone folds in half. You buy a new one. It folds too.
Day seven, door handles come off in your hand. Your mom stops locking the bathroom.
Here's what nobody tells you about super strength.
Strength isn't how hard you push. It's how hard you could push.
And your body doesn't always ask permission. A sneeze. A flinch. A bad dream.
Day eight, you have a nightmare... and you wake up in the apartment below yours.
Mr. Patel is very calm about it. He hands you a cup of tea.
It explodes.

By day nine, a video of you lifting a car off a cyclist has two hundred million views.
Every football league bans you. Every gym, too.
A construction company offers you a million dollars a day. An army general offers you a lot more.
You could build a house in an afternoon. Pull a ship into port. Stop a train with one hand.
But you can't hold a pencil.
And there's one thing you want more than all of it.

Your baby sister, Lily, is born on day nine.
Your dad drives everyone to the hospital. You follow on foot, because the last time you closed a car door, it came off.
Your dad is a mechanic. Big hands. Oil on everything.
When you were six, you tried to beat him at arm wrestling every single night. You never won. Not once.
And every night, he said the same thing.
DAD: "Strong is easy, kiddo. Gentle is hard."
At the hospital, everyone takes turns holding Lily. Your mom. Your dad. Grandma. Even the nurse who's scared of you.
You stand in the doorway with your hands in your pockets.
Because today, you're five hundred times stronger than you were last week. One flinch, and you could crush her.

That night, a black van parks outside your building.
Inside is a woman in a white coat. Her badge says Project Atlas.
DR. VOSS: "We can't stop the doubling. Nobody can. But we can teach you control."
So you go with them. Not because you want to.
Because Lily sleeps on the other side of your bedroom wall.

Atlas puts you in a steel room, deep inside a mountain.
On the table, there's a box of eggs.
The test is simple. Pick one up. Don't break it.
You break the first one. And the second. By the end of day ten, you've broken four hundred and twelve eggs.
Here's the problem. Every morning, you're twice as strong.
So every morning, you have to be twice as careful. Twice as slow. Twice as gentle.
Just to stay where you are.
Day eleven, you break ninety eggs. Day twelve, nine.
Day thirteen, you pick one up... and you hold it. For one whole minute.
It's the hardest thing you've ever done.

Then the phone rings.
An earthquake has hit your city. Your building is down. Your mom and dad got out.
Lily didn't.
You're there in four minutes. You didn't wait for the van. You ran.
The firefighters say the slab on top of her room weighs four hundred tonnes. They need a crane.
The crane is two hours away.
You don't need a crane.
You lift four hundred tonnes of concrete with your left hand.
And with your right hand, you reach into the dark... and pick up your sister.
Like an egg.
She grabs your finger and squeezes. For the first time in thirteen days, something squeezes back.
Your dad is crying. He puts his big hand on your shoulder.
DAD: "Strong is easy, kiddo. Gentle is hard."
And for the first time in your life, you beat him.

But tomorrow, you'll be twice as strong. And the day after that, twice again.
So you give Lily back to your mom. And you walk back to the mountain.
You live in a steel room you can't break. Yet.
Your family visits every Sunday. You wave through the glass.

So, is any of this real?
Not the doubling. But your body really does have brakes.
One of them is a protein called myostatin. It tells your muscles when to stop growing.
Some animals are born without that brake. Belgian Blue cattle look like bodybuilders.
And in two thousand four, doctors in Germany described a baby boy with the same mutation. He had about twice the muscle of other babies his age.
Your tendons have a brake too: tiny sensors that tell a muscle to ease off before it tears.
And size is a brake. Make an animal twice as big, and its muscles get four times stronger, but its body gets eight times heavier.
That's why an ant can carry many times its own weight, and an elephant can't jump.
So the real superpower isn't strength.
It's the brakes.

Day twenty. You're a million times stronger than the day it started.
Your phone rings. It's your mom. Her voice is shaking.
MOM: "It's Lily. She pulled the bars off her crib."
Lily is eleven days old.
And this morning, she was twice as strong as yesterday.

What would YOU do if your strength doubled every day? Tell us in the comments.
And subscribe, because Part 2 is coming.
```

## Fact notes (checked)
- Folding paper: a 0.1 mm sheet folded 42 times is 0.1 mm × 2⁴² ≈ 440,000 km, more than the Earth–Moon distance
  (~384,400 km).
- Doubling: day 5 = ×32, day 9 = ×512, day 10 = ×1,024, day 13 = ×8,192, day 20 = ×1,048,576.
- The 400-tonne slab on day 13: if day-0 strength is ~50 kg, then ×8,192 ≈ 410 tonnes. The numbers match the story.
- Myostatin limits muscle growth. Belgian Blue cattle carry a natural myostatin mutation ("double muscling").
- The German boy: Schuelke et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2004, myostatin mutation in a child. Widely
  reported as about twice the muscle mass of other children his age.
- Golgi tendon organs sense tension and can inhibit the muscle, a protective reflex.
- Square–cube law: muscle force grows with cross-section (×4 when twice the size), mass with volume (×8).
- Ants carry many times their body weight (often cited as 10–50×). Adult elephants can't jump.
