## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `NORA:` is your big sister.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can you really become invisible?
Not in a movie. With real science. Let's find out.
This morning, you look down, and you can see the floor through your hand.
Then through your arm. Then through your sneakers.
In the mirror, a toothbrush is brushing nobody's teeth.
Here's the rule. You can vanish whenever you want. But every time you come back, a little less of you comes back.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — NORA]
Your big sister Nora is an eye doctor.
When Dad left, she was seventeen. She worked two jobs and still came to every school play.
And when you were little, she always won hide-and-seek.
NORA: I see you, kid. I always see you.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. You skip the whole lunch line.
You sit in the front row of a sold-out concert. Nobody asks for your ticket.
You stand behind the principal and make his phone say "meow."
And for the first time ever, Nora can't find you.
NORA: Okay. You win. Where are you?

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But a little less of you comes back.
First, it's the dark. When you vanish, light goes straight through your eyes. Nothing catches it. The world turns white, and you're blind.
Then you come back without your shadow.
Next time, without your reflection.
In the class photo, there's an empty chair. Your phone doesn't know your face.
To hide you, light has to bend around you. The longer you stay gone, the wider it bends.
At night, a circle of stars goes missing above your town.
Sunlight slides around the streets. It snows in July.
NASA and the European Space Agency see a dark patch drifting across their maps. They call it the Blind Spot.
It's you.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
You're visible because light slows down and scatters inside you. To be invisible, every cell would have to bend light exactly like air.
To see, your eyes have to catch light. A truly invisible person would be blind.
But we're getting closer. In 2006, scientists at Duke University built a cloak that hid a copper cylinder from microwaves.
In 2014, the University of Rochester made objects disappear with four ordinary lenses.
And in the rainforest lives the glass frog. When it sleeps, it hides almost ninety percent of its red blood cells in its liver, and turns nearly see-through.
Every trick is real. Just never on a whole person.

[ACT 5 — THE LAKE]
Then the lake freezes. In July.
A little boy walks out on the ice. It cracks. Nora runs in after him.
You vanish, and the world goes white. You can't see her.
NORA: Kid! Follow my voice!
You find her hand in the cold.
NORA: I see you, kid. I always see you.
To pull them out, you have to come back. All the way.
You come back. But not all of you.
No shadow. No reflection. No photos. You will never be in a picture with your sister again.
She takes one anyway. One girl, one arm around nobody.
NORA: Doesn't matter. I see you.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
That night, Nora opens a box she's kept for ten years. Dad's photos.
In every single one, Dad has no shadow.
Dad didn't leave. Dad vanished.
On NASA's map, the Blind Spot over your town is gone.
But there's a second one. On the other side of the world.
And it's moving toward you.

[END]
If you could vanish for just one hour, where would you go?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because someone's coming that you can't see.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Why we're visible:** light refracts and scatters in tissue (refractive index of tissue ~1.35–1.45 vs air ~1.00)
  and is absorbed by pigments like melanin and haemoglobin. To be invisible, the body would need to neither absorb nor
  scatter light, i.e. match the refractive index of air everywhere.
- **The invisible-man problem:** vision needs the retina's photoreceptors to absorb light; a perfectly transparent eye
  absorbs none, so a truly invisible person would be blind (a well-known physics point about H. G. Wells's novel).
- **Duke cloak, 2006:** Schurig, Mock, Justice, Cummer, Pendry, Starr and Smith, *Science* 314 (2006): a metamaterial
  cloak at Duke University that guided microwaves around a copper cylinder; it worked in 2D, at one frequency.
- **Rochester Cloak, 2014:** Joseph Choi and John Howell, University of Rochester: four standard lenses that hide an
  object in the region between them over a range of viewing angles, with visible light.
- **Glass frogs:** Taboada et al., *Science*, 2022 (Duke University and the American Museum of Natural History): sleeping
  glass frogs pack about 89 % of their red blood cells into the liver and become 2–3 times more transparent.
- **Story, not fact:** vanishing at will, losing your shadow/reflection, bending sunlight around a town, missing stars,
  snow in July, NASA's "Blind Spot", Dad's photos, the second Blind Spot.
