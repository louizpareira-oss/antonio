## The rule of the story
- You can make a copy of yourself. It takes one hour and one drop of blood.
- **Every copy is you on day one. By day two, it's someone else.** And every copy you make takes one thing from the original: a memory.

## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `MIA:` is Mia; `COPY 2:` etc. are the copies.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can you really clone yourself?
Not in a movie. With real biology. Let's find out.
This morning, there are two toothbrushes in your cup.
And the guy using the other one has your face.
He waves. With your hand.
Last night, you found a machine in the garage. One drop of blood, one hour, and out steps you.
Here's the rule. Every copy is you on day one. By day two, it's someone else.
And every copy you make takes one thing from the original. One memory.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — MIA]
Your sister Mia is a nurse. Night shifts, twelve hours, every week since Mom left.
She has a scar across her left hand.
She got it when you were seven, pulling you out of the lake behind Grandpa's house.
MIA: You owe me a hand, little brother. I'm collecting when you're rich.
She says it every birthday. You always laugh.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. Copy number two does your homework. Number three does the dishes.
Number four goes to the dentist. He's not happy about it.
Saturday. Two birthday parties, same time. You go to both.
Football practice? Number five. Grandpa's garden? Number six.
You order one kebab. Six of you show up to eat it.
By Sunday night, you've slept twelve hours, and done a week's work.
This is the best idea you've ever had.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But by day two, it's someone else.
Number two starts liking a different song. Then a different girl. Then a different name.
COPY 2: Don't call me that. I'm Sam now.
Number three doesn't come home on Tuesday.
And on Wednesday, you find out why. The copies found the garage.
They're making copies. Of themselves.
By Friday, the machine's plans are online.
In one month, eight billion people become forty billion.
Every supermarket is empty by noon. Every tap runs dry by evening.
And at the election, nobody knows which you gets to vote.
Then one morning, you can't remember the lake.
You know there was a lake. You know Mia saved you.
But you're not in the picture any more.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
Cloning a mammal? Yes. Dolly the sheep was born in 1996, at the Roslin Institute in Scotland. It took two hundred and seventy-seven tries to get one lamb.
In 2018, the Chinese Academy of Sciences cloned two monkeys the same way. Zhong Zhong and Hua Hua. They're still alive.
Cloning a human has never been done, and in most countries it's illegal.
And here's the part the movies skip. Identical twins are natural clones. Same DNA. Different fingerprints. Different brains.
A clone of you would start as a baby, take twenty years to grow up, and remember nothing you remember.
A copy of you is real. A copy of YOU is not.

[ACT 5 — MIA]
Six of you are in the kitchen when Mia gets home from her shift.
She looks at each of you. She doesn't ask who's real.
MIA: Which one of you fell in the lake?
Five hands go up. Yours doesn't.
Because the machine took that one. It was the first memory you ever gave away.
She holds up the scarred hand.
MIA: Left hand. Second finger. Which rock did I cut it on?
COPY 2: The flat one. The grey one by the dock.
He's right. You don't know.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Mia takes a breath. She's crying.
MIA: He's right. And you're the only one who could have forgotten. Because you never had it.
She pulls out a photo. The lake. Her, the scar, still bleeding. And a boy.
That boy had a white number two on his shirt.
You were never the original.
Grandpa built the machine first. Twenty years ago.
And somewhere out there, number one is coming home.
In the cup, there's a third toothbrush.

[END]
If you could make one copy of yourself, what would you send him to do?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because number one is almost here.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Dolly:** born 5 July 1996 at the Roslin Institute (Edinburgh), announced Feb 1997; made by somatic cell nuclear transfer; 277 reconstructed eggs led to 29 embryos and one live lamb.
- **Monkeys:** Zhong Zhong and Hua Hua, crab-eating macaques, born late 2017 at the Institute of Neuroscience, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai), announced Jan 2018 in *Cell*; first primates cloned by nuclear transfer. Confirm "still alive" before voicing.
- **Human cloning:** no verified human reproductive clone exists; banned in many countries (UK Human Reproductive Cloning Act 2001, and a 2005 UN declaration calls for a ban). The US has no federal ban but several state bans; say "most countries" or "many countries" only after checking.
- **Twins / fingerprints:** monozygotic twins share almost all DNA; fingerprints are set by conditions in the womb and differ.
- **A clone has no memories:** memories are not in DNA; a clone would be a newborn with the donor's genome and a different epigenome.
- **Story, not fact:** the one-hour machine, adult copies, memory taken per copy, copies cloning copies, 40 billion in a month.
