# 7. How to match a clip to the dialogue

The course says: search Giphy for "cartoon argument." Then it admits *"sometimes it's not
that easy to find exactly what you want."*

That is the whole problem, and it is not your fault. Two reasons it is hard:

1. **You are searching the wrong thing.** "Cartoon argument" is a *literal* search — you
   took a word from the script and typed it in. Literal searching almost never works.
2. **You are searching a library that was not built for you.** Giphy and Tenor hold
   *reaction* GIFs — made for chat replies, tagged by meme name, 2 seconds long, low
   resolution. Nobody uploaded them to illustrate a line about avoiding conflict. You are
   hunting for something that mostly does not exist.

When you **generate or film** your visuals instead, you stop searching and start
**specifying**. You never fail to find the shot, because you make the shot. The difficulty
disappears completely — it was a symptom of the bad method.

But you still need the skill underneath: knowing **what shot the line needs**. That skill
is the same whether you search, film, or generate. Here it is.

---

## The core rule

> **Do not illustrate the words. Show the feeling underneath the words.**

The script says a thing. The picture must not repeat that thing — it must make you *feel*
it. If the line says "peace," do not show a peace sign. Show stillness: an empty room, a
flat sea, a person breathing.

Repeating the word is what beginners do, and it always looks cheap. Showing the feeling is
what makes a viewer stop scrolling.

---

## The 3-question method

For every beat of your script, ask these three questions in order. Never skip to the search
box before you have answered all three.

### Question 1 — What is the emotion?
Not the topic. The **feeling in the chest**. One word.

> Line: *"If you avoid conflict to keep the peace…"*
> Topic: conflict. **Emotion: swallowing something. Going quiet.**

### Question 2 — What does a human body do when it feels that?
Emotions are invisible. Bodies are visible. This is the step everyone misses.

> Swallowing something → **eyes drop, mouth closes, head turns slightly away, hands still**

### Question 3 — What single picture shows that body doing that?
Now, and only now, you have something to search for or generate.

> **A person at a table lowering their eyes while someone gestures at them off-frame.**

Compare:
- Course method: search "cartoon argument" → thousands of loud, wrong, meme-y results
- This method: "person lowering eyes at a table" → an exact, specific, findable image

You went from an abstract idea to a **concrete physical picture**. That is the entire craft.

---

## The translation table

Keep this open while you write. Left column is what your script says. Right column is what
you actually look for.

| Script says (abstract) | Show this (concrete) |
|---|---|
| Loneliness | One figure, wide shot, huge empty space around them |
| Feeling behind in life | Watching a train leave. Everyone walking fast past someone standing still |
| Avoiding something | Turning away from frame. A hand closing a door. Eyes down |
| Inner conflict / "war inside" | One person alone in a storm. Standing in heavy rain, not moving |
| Regret | Looking back over a shoulder while walking forward |
| A decision | A doorway. A fork in a road. A hand on a handle, not turning it |
| Discipline | Hands doing one small repeated thing — laces, chalk, writing, pouring |
| Being ignored | A face turned away in the foreground, subject blurred behind |
| Freedom / release | Horizon. Open water. Sky filling the frame. Wind |
| Exhaustion | Sitting on the floor. Back against a wall. Lights off |
| Peace | Stillness. Flat water. An empty made bed. Nothing moving |
| Chaos / drama | Crowd, movement, bright colour, fast cut, noise |
| Growth | Something small in a big frame — a plant, a figure climbing |
| Truth / confrontation | Direct eye contact. A mirror. A face lit from one side |
| Time passing | A window with light changing. A clock out of focus. Empty street at dawn |

If your line does not fit any row, run the 3-question method and add your own row. Build
this table up over months — it becomes your personal style.

## Worked example — the course's own quote

Script: *"If you avoid conflict to keep the peace, you start a war inside yourself. You
probably avoid conflict because it's easier than arguing or upsetting someone. But you
know, as well as I do, that avoiding conflict to protect yourself only causes harm. Just
because some people thrive on drama doesn't mean you have to participate."*

Split at every pause. One picture per beat. Eight beats:

| # | Beat | Emotion | The shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "If you avoid conflict to keep the peace…" | Swallowing it | Person at a table, eyes dropping, someone gesturing off-frame |
| 2 | "…you start a war inside yourself." | Internal storm | **Same person**, now alone, standing in heavy rain, not moving |
| 3 | "You probably avoid conflict because it's easier than arguing" | Relief that costs something | Walking away down a long corridor, shoulders low |
| 4 | "…or upsetting someone." | Guilt | A face across a table, hurt, looking straight at us |
| 5 | "But you know, as well as I do," | Direct address | Close-up. Eye contact. Or a face in a mirror |
| 6 | "…that avoiding conflict to protect yourself only causes harm." | Quiet damage | A cracked cup, still being used. A hand pressing a bruise |
| 7 | "Just because some people thrive on drama" | Chaos | Loud crowd, bright, fast, overlapping movement |
| 8 | "…doesn't mean you have to participate." | Chosen quiet | **Same person** walking out of that crowd into an empty street. Door closes |

Notice three things:

- **Not one of those shots is "an argument."** The literal search would have been wrong at
  every single beat.
- **Beats 1→2 and 7→8 reuse the same character.** That is what makes it feel like a film
  instead of a slideshow. Cheap edits change the person every cut; good ones follow one.
- **The energy has a shape.** Quiet → storm → quiet → close → loud → quiet. It is not flat.
  Beat 7 is loud *so that* beat 8 lands.

## Turning a shot into a generation prompt

Once you have the shot in words, the prompt writes itself. Formula:

```
[subject] + [action] + [environment] + [light] + [mood] + [camera] + 9:16 vertical
```

Beat 2 from the table above:

> *"A young man standing alone in heavy rain on an empty street at night, not moving,
> head down, streetlight behind him, cold blue light, melancholic, wide shot, shallow
> depth of field, cinematic, 9:16 vertical"*

Beat 8:

> *"A young man walking away from a crowded bright street into a quiet empty alley,
> seen from behind, warm light behind him and cold light ahead, calm, slow tracking
> shot, cinematic, 9:16 vertical"*

Generate 3 takes of each. Keep the best. **You will never again fail to find the shot you
want** — because you are describing it, not hunting for it.

If you are filming instead: that same sentence is your shot list. Read it out and go shoot it.

---

## The 8 folders

You do not need infinite variety. Nearly every video in this niche is built from eight
emotional categories. Build a library with these folders and you can cut a video in
20 minutes without searching anything:

```
01-alone/       lone figure, wide, empty space
02-turning-away/ backs, doors, eyes down, leaving
03-storm/       rain, wind, dark water, heavy weather
04-crowd/       people passing, movement, city noise
05-hands/       small actions, close-up, effort
06-threshold/   doorways, corridors, roads, windows
07-open/        sky, sea, horizon, sunrise
08-face/        eye contact, mirrors, one-side light
```

15–20 clips in each folder = 120–160 clips = you are never stuck again. One filming
session or one afternoon of generating fills it.

---

## Timing rules

- **One picture per pause.** Never two ideas on one shot. Never one shot across two ideas.
- **2–5 seconds per shot.** Under 2 feels frantic. Over 5 feels dead — unless it is the
  final line, where a long hold is powerful.
- **Cut on the breath, not on the beat.** Change the picture in the silence between
  sentences, not while a word is being said.
- **Land the last shot early.** Bring in the final image about 1 second *before* the last
  line, and hold it 2 seconds *after* the voice stops. That silence is where people
  decide to rewatch.

---

## Two advanced moves

**Counterpoint.** Sometimes the strongest choice opposes the words. Line: *"I was fine."*
Shot: someone sitting alone on a bathroom floor. The gap between what is said and what is
seen creates the whole emotion. Use this maybe twice per video — more and it gets confusing.

**The return.** Show an image early, then bring it back changed at the end. The same door,
now open. The same street, now at dawn. The same person, now looking up. Viewers feel the
resolution without being told it, and they rewatch to catch it. This one technique will do
more for your retention than any filter.
