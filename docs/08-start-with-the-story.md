# 8. Start with the story

> The story is the engine. A good-looking AI clip cannot rescue a weak sequence. Before
> opening an image generator, write the story in a way that gives **every shot a job**.

Doc 07 tells you which picture goes on which line. This one comes first: it is how you get
lines worth putting pictures on.

---

## Why this has to come first

The order most people work in:

> idea → open the generator → make some cool clips → try to arrange them

That order cannot produce a video. It produces a **slideshow of nice frames**, because
each clip was made before anything needed it. A shot generated without a job will never
find one afterwards — you end up cutting to it because it looks good, which the viewer
reads as filler, and filler is where they scroll.

The order that works:

> claim → shape → beats → jobs → prompts → generate

By the time you open the generator, every prompt is already decided. Generating stops
being exploration and becomes execution. This is also why it gets *faster* — you are no
longer browsing your own output hoping something fits.

**The AI is a camera, not a writer.** A camera pointed at nothing gives you nothing, in
very high resolution.

---

## Step 1 — One sentence somebody could disagree with

Not a topic. A **claim**.

A topic is a subject area: forgiveness, discipline, loneliness. Everyone has made that
video. A claim is a specific assertion about how the thing actually works — and the test
is simple:

> **Could a reasonable person hear this and say "no, that's not right"?**

If nobody can disagree with it, it is a slogan, and slogans scroll past.

| Slogan (dead) | Claim (a video) |
|---|---|
| Let go of the past and be free | You're not angry about what happened — you're angry it didn't happen *differently* |
| Discipline beats motivation | Motivation isn't weak, it's *early*. It gets you to day four. Nothing gets you to day forty except boredom you agreed to |
| You are enough | "You are enough" is why you stopped trying. You were never not enough — you were unpractised |
| Choose peace over drama | Avoiding conflict to keep the peace starts a war you host by yourself |

Write your claim at the top of the file and do not start writing lines until it is there.
Script 01 keeps it under a heading called **"The idea underneath"** — copy that habit.

If the sentence takes you three sentences, you do not have it yet. Keep cutting.

---

## Step 2 — Pick a shape

A 60–90 second script is too short to wander and too long to be one thought. It needs a
shape you chose on purpose. Four that work in this niche:

| Shape | How it moves | Beats | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Reversal** | What you think is happening → what is actually happening | 16–20 | Anything the audience already has an opinion about |
| **The Confession** | I did this thing → here is what it cost → here is what I know now | 18–22 | Building trust; the "I was nineteen" register |
| **The Reframe** | Take one common word and redefine it by the end | 14–18 | Big abstract nouns: forgiveness, discipline, love |
| **The Escalation** | Small → larger → largest → one quiet line | 20–24 | Anger, urgency, wake-up-call energy |

Script 01 is a Reversal wearing a Confession: *you think you're angry about the event* →
*you're angry about the version that never happened* → *and you were never the judge.*

Two turns, not one. **The last turn should recontextualise the opening line**, so the
viewer wants to hear the first eight seconds again. That is the entire rewatch mechanic,
and it is decided here, in the writing — not in the edit.

---

## Step 3 — One controlling image

Pick a single concrete world and pull all your vocabulary from it.

Script 01 picks a **courtroom**: building a case, collecting evidence, a trial that never
comes, the bench, the sentence, the defendant. Every abstract idea in the script arrives
wearing courtroom clothes.

You get three things free:

1. **The lines stop being abstract.** "Put down the case you've been building" is a
   picture. "Let go of resentment" is not.
2. **The visuals cohere automatically.** You now have a look, and you did not have to
   invent one per shot.
3. **The return becomes available.** Doc 07's strongest technique — show an image early,
   bring it back changed — only works if there *is* a recurring image. That is this.

**One image per video.** A courtroom *and* a storm *and* a locked door is not three
metaphors, it is none.

---

## Step 4 — Write in beats, not paragraphs

Write the voiceover as single lines with blank lines between them, the way
`scripts/01-forgive-the-past.md` does. The line breaks are your pauses; you breathe at
each one, and you cut at each one.

Three rules:

- **One idea per line.** If a line contains "and" joining two ideas, it is two beats.
- **2.5–4 seconds when read aloud.** Read it out. If you run out of breath, split it.
- **No line exists only to set up the next one.** A beat that carries no weight of its own
  is a beat the viewer uses to leave.

At 70 seconds you are writing roughly **20 beats**. That is the budget. Twenty pictures,
twenty jobs.

---

## Step 5 — Give every beat a job

This is the part the title promises. Every beat does exactly one of these:

| Job | What it does | How many | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | States the claim, or the half of it that sounds wrong | 1–2 | Tight, close, direct. A face or a detail |
| **Turn** | Flips what the previous beat meant | 2–3 | A change of scale or light — the picture must move too |
| **Evidence** | The specific, personal, unarguable detail | 4–6 | Concrete nouns. A bus, a shower, three in the morning |
| **Escalation** | Same idea, more pressure | 3–5 | Wider, louder, faster, more people |
| **Reveal** | The thing the whole script was hiding | 1 | The controlling image, seen properly for the first time |
| **Landing** | Lets it settle. Says less than you want to | 2–3 | Stillness. Hold long. Let the voice stop first |

Two rules govern the whole sheet:

- **Exactly one job per beat.** A beat doing two jobs is two beats wearing one coat.
- **A beat with no job does not get a shot. It gets deleted.**

---

## The deletion test

Go line by line. Delete the beat. Read the script aloud without it.

- **Nothing is lost** → it had no job. It stays deleted.
- **Something breaks** → that break *is* its job. Write the job in the margin.

A first-draft 70-second script usually loses three or four beats to this, and every one it
loses makes the rest land harder. If you cannot bring yourself to cut a beat you know is
dead, that is the beat the viewer cuts by swiping.

Run this *before* you generate anything. Cutting a beat costs nothing now and costs a
generated clip later.

---

## The energy has to have a shape

Doc 07 notes that a good sequence goes quiet → storm → quiet → close → loud → quiet. That
shape is not an editing decision. It is decided here.

Sketch it. Put your twenty beats on a line and mark each one loud or quiet:

```
Hook    ▁▁▃
Evidence  ▁▁▁▁▁
Escalation  ▃▅▇
Reveal      ▇
Landing       ▃▁▁
```

A flat line is a flat video, no matter how good the clips are. And the loud beat is not
there to be loud — **it is there so the quiet one after it lands**. If you have no quiet
beats, you have no loud ones either; you just have noise.

---

## Worked example — Script 01, first seven beats

| # | Line | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You're not still angry about what happened. | **Hook** — sounds wrong on purpose |
| 2 | You're angry that it didn't happen differently. | **Turn** — the claim lands |
| 3 | I was nineteen the last time I spoke to him. | **Evidence** — a real number, a real person |
| 4 | I've had that conversation four hundred times since. | **Evidence** — the specific absurd count |
| 5 | In the shower. On the bus. At three in the morning. | **Escalation** — same idea, three places |
| 6 | Every time, I win. Every time, I say the perfect thing. | **Escalation** — peak of the fantasy |
| 7 | And every time, I wake up, and it still went the way it went. | **Turn** — the floor drops out |

Every beat is doing one thing. Delete beat 4 and beat 5 has nothing to escalate. Delete
beat 6 and beat 7 has no height to fall from. That is what "gives every shot a job" means
in practice: **the sequence would break without it.**

---

## Checklist before you open the image generator

- [ ] My claim is one sentence, and someone could argue with it.
- [ ] I chose a shape, and I know where the turns are.
- [ ] I have one controlling image, not three.
- [ ] The script is written as beats with blank lines between them.
- [ ] Every beat has a job written next to it.
- [ ] I ran the deletion test on all of them.
- [ ] The energy map is not a flat line.
- [ ] The last beat recontextualises the first.

Any box unticked, you are not ready to generate. You are ready to rewrite.

---

## What goes wrong

| Failure | How it shows up | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Topic instead of claim | The video is "nice" and nobody finishes it | Step 1 — make it arguable |
| No controlling image | Every shot looks like it came from a different video | Step 3 — one world |
| Beats that only set up other beats | Viewers drop around 15–20 seconds | Deletion test |
| All escalation, no quiet | Exhausting; no rewatches | Energy map — add landings |
| Reveal in the middle | The last 20 seconds have nothing to do | Move it to beat 18–19 |
| Generating before writing | Hours of clips, none of them fit | This entire document |

---

## Then, and only then

With a beat sheet where every line has a job, go to
[`docs/07-matching-visuals-to-dialogue.md`](07-matching-visuals-to-dialogue.md) and turn
each job into a picture, then each picture into a prompt.

The story decides the shots. The shots decide the prompts. The generator is the last thing
you touch, and by then it has nothing left to decide.
