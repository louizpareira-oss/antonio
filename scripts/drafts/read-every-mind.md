## The rule of the story
- You hear thoughts. Not words: the thoughts *behind* the words.
- **Every day the range doubles. And you can never turn it off.**

## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `DAD:` is Dad; `(thought)` lines are heard in the hero's head.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can one person really read every mind on Earth?
Not in a movie. With real science. Let's find out.
This morning, your phone rings. And before you look, you know it's Mum.
You know she's going to say she's late. You know she's lying.
You didn't hear her words. You heard what's behind them.
Something woke up in your head last night. A hum, like a fridge in the next room.
Here's the rule. Every day, the range doubles. And you can never turn it off.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — DAD]
Dad was an air-traffic controller for thirty years.
DAD: Forty planes in the sky, and you listen to all of them. The trick is knowing which one is about to go wrong.
Lately, he's quiet. His hearing's going. He keeps the old headset around his neck.
He says the sky still talks to him.
He never says what it's saying.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. The range is one metre. You sit behind the smartest kid in class. Every answer, delivered straight into your head.
Day two. Ten metres. Your crush walks past, and you hear it. She likes your hoodie. She likes you.
Day three. A hundred metres. Poker night. You can't lose.
Day five. You find a lost dog, because you can hear the dog.
Day eight. The whole city. You know who's lying on the news before the news does.
The violet ring around your head is the size of a hula hoop now.
People are starting to look.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But you can never turn it off.
Day ten. You stop sleeping. Six million people dream at once, and all of them dream at you.
Day twelve. Your best friend sits down next to you, and smiles.
MAYA (thought): Please don't look at me. Please don't find the thing I did.
You find it. You wish you hadn't.
Day fourteen. The ring touches the edge of space. Eight billion voices. Every secret on Earth, in one head.
Then the secrets start leaking out of you. You talk in your sleep. Someone records it.
Passwords. Launch codes. Who really owns what.
In one week, the stock market closes. Three governments fall. Nobody can lie, so nobody can make a deal.
The World Health Organization names it the Open Mind Event.
It has one case.
You.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
Thoughts really can be read. Just not from across the room.
In 2023, scientists at the University of Texas used a brain scanner and an AI to turn people's thoughts into sentences. Not perfect. But close enough to scare them.
And at Stanford, a woman who lost her speech got a brain implant that types what she's trying to say. Sixty-two words a minute.
Your brain has about eighty-six billion neurons and a hundred trillion connections. And its signals are weaker than a watch battery. Outside your skull, they fade in millimetres.
So reading eight billion minds from one chair? No.
Reading one mind, from one centimetre away? Already happening.

[ACT 5 — DAD]
Day twenty. You come home, and the house is silent.
Not quiet. Silent. Eight billion voices, and one hole in the middle of them.
Dad. You can't hear him.
DAD: Your grandfather had it too. Nineteen eighty-one. He lasted a month.
He takes off the headset. Under it, a scar, behind his ear.
DAD: He taught me how to build a wall. I've had mine up since I was your age.
He puts his hand on your head, and the hum stops. One second of silence. The best second of your life.
DAD: I can teach you. But the wall costs something. You can't hear anyone, ever. Not even the people you love.
You take the deal.
Now you'll never know for sure if your crush meant it. If Maya forgave you. If Mum was lying.
That's the price.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Day twenty-one. The wall holds. Eight billion voices, gone.
Except one.
It's not from Earth. It's slow, and cold, and it's counting.
That night, NASA picks up a signal that's been aimed at this planet for forty years.
It's not a message. It's a question.
"Which of you can hear us?"
Your phone rings. You don't know who it is.
For the first time in three weeks, you're afraid to answer.

[END]
If you could hear one person's real thoughts, just once, who would it be?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because something out there heard yours.
```
