## The rule of the story
- Your body shrinks. Your atoms don't. You are the same atoms packed into a body the size of an ant.
- **Every time you shrink, something in you stays big.**

## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `DAD:` is Dad; `MAYA:` is your sister.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can a human really shrink to the size of an ant?
Not in a movie. With real physics. Let's find out.
This morning you woke up next to a sugar cube the size of a car.
Your bed is a mountain. Your phone is a football pitch. And something is walking toward you with six legs.
You're five millimetres tall.
Here's the rule. Your body shrinks. Your atoms don't. Every time you shrink, something in you stays big.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — DAD]
Dad keeps bees. Forty hives, twenty years, one bad knee.
DAD: Don't run from a bee. Just be boring. They've got better things to do.
He's been saving for the knee operation since you were six.
Every jar of honey goes in the tin on the fridge.
He never counts it in front of you.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. You walk out the door and across the pond. On the water. It holds you like a trampoline.
You jump. Ten times your own height, onto the shed roof. No pain on landing.
A bee lands next to you. You climb on. You fly to school.
Lunch is one crumb of your sister's sandwich, and you're full.
You nap inside a walnut shell. You watch a movie from Maya's raincoat pocket.
MAYA: Shh. He's in my pocket. He's tiny. It's fine.
By Friday, you never want to be big again.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But something in you stays big.
Your atoms didn't shrink. So your weight didn't either.
You step on the kitchen table. The table collapses. You step on the floor, and the floor cracks.
Seventy kilograms on a footprint the size of a pinhead. That's more pressure than a bullet.
You can't eat a crumb anymore. You need the whole sandwich.
And you're burning heat like a furnace. A body that heavy, that small, can't cool down. The walnut shell smokes.
Then it rains. A raindrop at your size weighs more than your head.
You hide in the garden, and the ants stop. All of them. And turn around.
They're not scared of you. You're the same size. You're just hot, and heavy, and you're walking on their roof.
NASA picks up a hot spot in your garden. Seven millimetres wide.
It's you.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
The size part is. Scientists call it the square-cube law. Make something ten times smaller, and its strength drops a hundred times, but its weight drops a thousand. That's why an ant can carry fifty times its own body, and you can't.
Water really holds you at that size. The skin on a pond is called surface tension. Water striders live on it every day.
Falling? A mouse survives a drop that would kill a horse. Air slows small things down.
But shrinking a person? No. The smallest thing we can make is an atom, and yours don't get smaller. In 2011, scientists at CERN held single atoms of antimatter in a trap. That's as small as it gets. Nobody has ever shrunk one.
Every part is real. Just not in one body.

[ACT 5 — DAD]
Sunday. Dad's hives are quiet. Too quiet.
You climb inside. The queen is dead. A mite the size of a dog is eating the babies.
You're the only one in the world small enough to fix it. So you do. Hive by hive. Forty hives. Three days.
When you climb out, your legs don't work right. The heat cooked something in you. You'll limp forever.
Dad kneels down to see you. On the bad knee.
DAD: You saved every one of them. You know what that is? That's the knee. That's the whole tin.
He puts the tin back on the fridge. He doesn't tell you the operation was Tuesday.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Monday morning, you wake up next to the sugar cube.
It's smaller. It's the size of a chair.
You're growing back. One millimetre a day.
And in the garden, the ant with the bent antenna is standing on the sugar cube.
It's bigger too. The same amount. Every day.
And it's not the only one.

[END]
If you could be ant-sized for one day, what's the first thing you'd do?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, before the ants are big enough to watch this.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Square-cube law:** scale length by 1/10 → area (strength, cooling) by 1/100, volume (mass, heat produced) by 1/1000. Explains ant strength (~10–50× body weight) and why a shrunk human keeping his mass would be crushingly dense and unable to shed heat.
- **Pressure:** 70 kg on a ~1 mm² footprint ≈ 700 MPa, well above the stagnation pressure of a bullet; cracked tiles and a collapsed table are the story's exaggeration of a real effect.
- **Surface tension:** water striders (Gerridae) stand on the pond surface; an object the size of an ant can be supported by it.
- **Terminal velocity:** small animals have low terminal velocity (Haldane, "On Being the Right Size": a mouse survives a fall a horse would not).
- **CERN, 2011:** the ALPHA collaboration trapped antihydrogen atoms for ~1000 s (*Nature Physics*, 2011). Atoms are not shrinkable; their size is set by physical constants.
- **Varroa mites** really kill bee colonies by feeding on brood; a varroa mite (~1.5 mm) would look dog-sized to a 5 mm person.
- **Story, not fact:** the shrink itself, mass-preserved shrinking, the NASA hot spot, the growing ants.
