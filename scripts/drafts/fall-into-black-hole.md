## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `MOM:` is Mom.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Your fingertips are stretching. Longer. Thinner. Like spaghetti.
Gravity is pulling your fingers harder than your elbow.
Can a human really survive falling into a black hole?
Not in a movie. With real physics. Let's find out.
Last night, a door appeared in Mom's garage.
On the other side: the giant black hole at the centre of our galaxy. Four million times heavier than the Sun.
Here's the rule. The closer you get, the slower your time runs. And out here, the world doesn't wait for you.

[ACT 1 — MOM]
Mom spent twenty years listening to the sky at the Arecibo radio telescope.
In 2020, the telescope collapsed. Now she drives a night taxi.
She still keeps a radio dish on the garage roof.
MOM: Every star hums. Black holes don't hum. They swallow the song.
The door appeared right where her dish was pointing.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. You tie a rope around your waist and open the door.
There it is. A perfect black circle, seventeen times wider than the Sun.
You throw in a tennis ball. It slows down. Turns red. And fades.
Lean closer, and you see the back of your own head. Its light went all the way around.
On the radio, Mom sounds like a chipmunk. Her words arrive sped up.
You take the best selfie in history. Two billion views by dinner.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But out here, the world doesn't wait for you.
You step a little closer. Just for ten minutes.
Back in the garage, the milk is sour. You've been gone a month.
Next time, one hour. You come back three years later.
And Mom's hair is grey.
MOM: You said one hour. I kept your plate on the table for three years.
And the door is leaking.
All over the world, the tides come in at the wrong time.
NASA calls it the Garage.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
The black hole is. It's called Sagittarius A star, twenty-seven thousand light-years away. In 2022, astronomers took its picture.
Spaghettification is real too. Fall into a small black hole, and you'd be a noodle long before the edge.
But the bigger the black hole, the gentler the stretch. At this giant, you'd cross the edge and feel nothing.
From outside, a falling person seems to freeze, turn red, and fade. Just like the tennis ball.
Once you're inside, nothing comes back out. Not even light.
Inside this one, you'd have about one minute before the centre.
So, can a human survive falling in? Yes. For about a minute.

[ACT 5 — THE DOOR]
Then the door starts to grow.
Every day it's a little wider, and the oceans a little wilder.
In a month, it takes the house. Then the town.
Someone has to close it. From the other side.
Mom grabs your hand.
MOM: I lost my telescope. I am not losing you.
But you're already through. You let go of the rope.
For Mom, you'll be falling forever. A red shadow, frozen on the edge.
Your fingertips stretch. Just a little.
You cross the edge. Nothing happens.
Behind you, the door goes dark.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
One minute left.
Then the radio crackles. Signals can still fall in.
MOM: I've pointed the dish at you. Hold on. I'm coming.
Deeper down, where there should be nothing, there's a light.
A second door. Opening.
Someone in a blue hoodie steps out. And waves.
It's you.

[END]
Would you step through the door, if you knew you'd lose three years?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because the second door is already open.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Sagittarius A\*:** about 4 million solar masses, about 26,000–27,000 light-years from Earth. Its first image was
  released in May 2022 by the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration (narrated as "astronomers").
- **"Seventeen times wider than the Sun":** Schwarzschild radius of 4.3 million Suns ≈ 12.7 million km, so the horizon
  is ≈ 25 million km across vs the Sun's 1.39 million km ≈ 18×; "seventeen" is safe (the dark "shadow" seen in the
  image is about 2.5× bigger still).
- **Spaghettification:** tidal stretching grows as M/r³; at the horizon it scales as 1/M². For a ~10-solar-mass black
  hole a human is torn apart hundreds of kilometres outside the horizon; for Sagittarius A* the stretch at the horizon
  is far smaller than what you feel standing on Earth.
- **The faller seen from outside:** an outside observer sees an infalling object slow down, redshift and fade, never
  quite seen to cross. The faller crosses in finite proper time and notices nothing special locally.
- **About a minute inside:** maximum proper time from horizon to singularity for a non-rotating hole is πGM/c³ ≈ 15.5 µs
  per solar mass; for 4.3 million Suns ≈ 67 s.
- **Light falls in after you:** signals from outside can still reach someone inside the horizon (that's why Mom's
  radio reaches you); nothing gets back out.
- **Seeing the back of your head:** at 1.5 Schwarzschild radii (the photon sphere) light can orbit the black hole, so in
  principle you could see the back of your own head looking along the orbit.
- **Chipmunk Mom:** someone hovering close to a black hole receives outside signals blueshifted (sped up and higher
  pitched), the mirror of the redshift seen from outside.
- **Gravitational time dilation:** real (Earth-surface clocks run slower than GPS satellite clocks). Strong dilation
  needs you to *hover* very close to the horizon, which would take a rocket pushing harder than any human could
  survive; the story skips that.
- **Arecibo:** 305 m radio telescope in Puerto Rico; its platform fell and the dish collapsed on 1 December 2020.
- **Story, not fact:** the door, the rope working, ten minutes = a month and one hour = three years, the leaking door,
  tides and clocks going wrong on Earth, NASA's "Garage", the door growing, the second door, the other you.
