## The voiceover script
Speaker tags: unmarked lines are the NARRATOR. `DAD:` is Dad; `NINA:` is the little sister.

```
[ACT 1 — HOOK]
Can a human really live without sleep?
Not in a movie. With real science. Let's find out.
It's four in the morning. Your eyes are wide open.
Every window on the street is dark. Every window except yours.
And there's a number glowing on your chest. Hours awake. Twenty-two. Twenty-three. Twenty-four.
Last night, the part of your brain that makes you tired just... switched off.
Here's the rule. Sleep isn't rest. It's maintenance.
Skip it, and the bill still comes.
Remember that.

[ACT 1 — DAD]
Your dad, Marcus, is a night-shift nurse. Twelve years of it.
DAD: Three coffees to start. Two to drive home. Don't tell your sister.
He sleeps in the daytime, in a room with the curtains taped shut.
Your little sister Nina draws the family with the sun and the moon in the same sky.
NINA: That's Dad's sky.

[ACT 2 — THE FUN PART]
Day one. Everyone else is asleep, and you have eight extra hours.
You beat every game you own. Then you learn piano, from videos, in a week.
You read the whole internet. Well, the good parts.
By day four, you've seen three sunrises nobody else was awake for.
The city at three a.m. is yours. Empty roads. Empty courts. Free throws until the sky turns pink.
You're not tired. You're never tired.
Your friends call you the Owl.
You love it.

[ACT 3 — THE CURSE]
But sleep is maintenance.
Day three. The shadows in the hallway start to move. Your brain is dreaming with your eyes open.
Day five. You read the same sentence nine times. The words won't stay.
Day seven. Nina asks you something, and you can't find her name.
NINA: It's Nina. You know that.
Then a company reverse-engineers your brain. They sell it as a pill. The Awake pill.
Within a month, a billion people stop sleeping.
Pilots. Surgeons. The man watching the reactor dials at three a.m.
Eight extra hours of work, and every single one of them is getting slower.
The World Health Organization counts crashes. Then it counts fires. Then it stops counting.
And the clock on your chest reads one thousand hours.

[ACT 4 — IS ANY OF THIS REAL?]
So. Is any of this real?
In 1964, a seventeen-year-old named Randy Gardner stayed awake for eleven days. On day four, he thought a street sign was a person.
Your brain washes itself while you sleep. A system of channels flushes out waste proteins. Skip the wash, and the junk builds up.
Stay awake long enough, and your brain takes sleep anyway. Micro-sleeps. Two seconds. Eyes open. No memory. At the wheel.
And there's a disease, fatal familial insomnia, where the brain loses the ability to sleep at all. Nobody survives it.
Going without sleep is so dangerous that Guinness World Records retired the category.
You can skip the sleep. You can't skip the bill.

[ACT 5 — DAD]
One night, Dad comes home at seven, and he doesn't go upstairs.
DAD: Twelve years, kid. I nodded off at the wheel on the bridge. Woke up on the wrong side of the road.
He's shaking. You've never seen him shake.
DAD: You think I wanted the night shift? I wanted the daytime. With you two.
So you give him the one thing you have. You put the Awake pill in the bin, and you ask the doctors to switch it off. Your brain. The thing that stopped.
It takes a surgery. It takes your perfect memory of those thousand hours.
And then, for the first time in forty-two days, you sleep.
You wake up with the sun on the floor, and Dad is making breakfast. In the daytime.

[ACT 5 — THE TWIST]
Then Nina shows you her new drawing.
The sun and the moon in the same sky. And under them, a line of people, all with their eyes open.
NINA: That's everyone else.
On the news, a billion people are still on the pill. And the clock on your chest, the one the doctors removed... is now glowing on hers.

[END]
If you got eight extra hours every night, what would you do with them?
Tell me in the comments. And subscribe, because the pill is still out there.
```

## Fact notes (to check before voicing)
- **Randy Gardner (1964):** 17-year-old student in San Diego, 264 hours (11 days) awake, monitored by Stanford
  researcher William Dement; hallucinations and paranoia reported by day 4 (mistook a street sign for a person).
- **Glymphatic system:** described by Maiken Nedergaard's lab (University of Rochester, *Science* 2013); cerebrospinal
  fluid flow through the brain increases during sleep and clears waste such as beta-amyloid.
- **Microsleeps:** brief episodes of sleep (fractions of a second to ~15 s) during sleep deprivation, a known factor in
  road crashes.
- **Fatal familial insomnia:** a rare inherited prion disease (PRNP gene); progressive inability to sleep, always fatal,
  typically within 1–3 years of onset.
- **Guinness World Records** stopped monitoring sleep-deprivation attempts because of the health risks.
- **WHO / NASA:** plain text names only. The WHO counting crashes is story, not fact.
- **Story, not fact:** the switch-off, the chest clock, the Awake pill, a billion users, the surgery, the transfer to Nina.
